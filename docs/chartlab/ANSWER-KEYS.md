# ChartLab — Mark the Zone
## Teaching answer keys · Version 1 · Review draft

**Purpose.** These answer keys support three illustrative W-shaped chart studies. They teach the student to mark two *areas of interest*: support around the two troughs and resistance around the intervening recovery high (the neckline).

**Important teaching distinction.** The zones do not create support or resistance; they are a way of representing areas where price previously reacted. The W is a visual structure, not a guarantee of reversal, breakout or profitable trade. These charts use invented OHLC-style data for instruction, not live or historical market data from a real instrument.

## How to use the answer keys

- Reveal reference zones only after the student submits both of their own zones.
- Freeze a submitted zone. Do not silently move or replace it when moving to the next step.
- Compare the student's placement with the reference, but do not grade on exact pixel or price equality.
- Give separate feedback for support and resistance. A student may have identified one area well and need to revisit the other.
- Ask for reasoning as well as placement. Feedback should explain the chart evidence—not merely say “correct” or “incorrect.”
- Do not tell students that support must hold, resistance must reject price, or that the W itself is an entry signal.

### Common evaluation rubric

**Strong / well supported**
- Support contains or closely brackets the two relevant trough reactions.
- Resistance contains or closely brackets the intervening swing high / neckline.
- The zones are narrow enough to be useful and wide enough to reflect the variation visible in the candles.
- The learner can point to the relevant reactions and explain the choice.

**Plausible, refine the boundaries**
- The zone overlaps the relevant reactions but misses one wick or captures extra nearby price action.
- The reasoning is sound, although the upper or lower boundary could be more carefully placed.
- Feedback should name what was captured and suggest the smallest useful adjustment.

**Revisit the placement**
- Support is drawn around only one trough while missing the other, or is placed around unrelated lows.
- Resistance is drawn around a later breakout/high rather than the intervening neckline.
- A zone is so wide that it spans much of the chart and no longer distinguishes a meaningful area.
- The student uses the zone as proof of a future move rather than describing past price behaviour.

**Suggested automated guidance, not a final grade.** Use overlap/coverage of the reference area as a signal to select feedback, not as the sole mark. A reasonable zone can differ from the reference. Always pair the comparison with the student's stated reason. Do not make numeric overlap percentages sound like statistical confidence.

# Example 01 — Clear W

## Teaching objective
Recognise the two distinct areas in a relatively clear double-bottom structure: support around the two lows, and resistance around the recovery high between them.

## Reference answer

| Zone | Reference lower boundary | Reference upper boundary | What it represents |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Support | 98.5 | 100.8 | The area around both troughs; the generated sample's two prominent trough wicks are approximately 99.15 and 98.70. |
| Resistance / neckline | 104.6 | 106.2 | The recovery area between the two lows; the relevant local high in the sample is approximately 105.9. |

These are reference coordinates for the current prototype chart, not universal levels or uniquely correct answers.

## Why these zones are reasonable

**Support:** Price declines into a similar region twice and then moves away from it. The lows are not identical, which is the reason to represent an area rather than draw a perfect-price line. The zone should cover the relevant troughs without swallowing large amounts of unrelated chart space.

**Resistance:** The recovery between the two troughs reaches a local high and then backs away. That intervening high forms the neckline area for this teaching example. Mark the reaction that occurred between the lows—not the later candles at the far right of the chart.

**Structure:** The W describes the two troughs and the high between them. A later move above the neckline is a subsequent event in this illustrative sequence. It does not prove that every W will break out or that the breakout will continue.

## Plausible alternative placements

- **Support:** A zone that includes both trough reactions around roughly 98.7–99.2, with a modest margin above and below, is defensible. For automated coaching, zones with lower boundary in about 97.8–99.2 and upper boundary in about 100.0–101.3 can be reviewed as plausible if their overall width remains sensible and they cover both lows.
- **Resistance:** A zone around roughly 104.5–106.5 can be defensible if it includes the intervening reaction. For automated coaching, boundaries around lower 104.0–105.2 and upper 105.5–106.8 are plausible review ranges, provided the zone remains centred on the neckline rather than a later high.

These coaching ranges deliberately allow visual judgement. They are not a guarantee that every combination within the ranges is good: the resulting zone must still make sense as one area.

## Feedback text library

**Both zones well placed**
> Good reading. Your support zone captures the two troughs, while your resistance zone marks the recovery high between them. The two areas describe different parts of the structure. Keep them focused on the observed reactions; neither zone guarantees what price will do next.

**Support is good; resistance needs work**
> You identified the repeated low area well. Revisit resistance: the useful area is around the high between the two troughs—the neckline—not the later move to the right. Move the zone back to the earlier reaction and keep it narrow enough to identify that structure.

**Resistance is good; support needs work**
> Your neckline area is close to the relevant recovery high. Revisit support and make sure your zone accounts for both troughs, including the difference between their wick lows. A single line through just one low misses part of the evidence.

**Zones too wide**
> You've included the relevant reactions, but the zones cover more price action than necessary. Try narrowing each boundary to the local cluster that explains the reaction. A useful zone captures evidence without making half the chart a level.

**Zones too narrow / exact-line thinking**
> Your levels are close to the important prices, but the chart does not show reactions at one perfectly identical price. Widen the zone just enough to account for the observed wick/body variation, while keeping the area distinct.

## Check for understanding

1. Which zone represents the two troughs? **Support.**
2. Which zone represents the swing high between them? **Resistance / neckline.**
3. Does the W or either zone guarantee a bullish continuation? **No. They describe observed structure and areas of interest; future behaviour remains uncertain.**

# Example 02 — Uneven lows

## Teaching objective
Understand that a second trough can probe lower than the first. Teach students to accommodate the visible difference without expanding the zone indiscriminately.

## Reference answer

| Zone | Reference lower boundary | Reference upper boundary | What it represents |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Support | 97.7 | 101.0 | Both troughs, including the second and deeper probe; generated sample trough lows are approximately 99.50 and 97.85. |
| Resistance / neckline | 104.1 | 105.9 | The recovery high between the troughs; the relevant local high is approximately 105.6. |

These are reference coordinates for the current prototype chart. The support reference is intentionally wider than Example 01 because the lows differ more.

## Why these zones are reasonable

**Support:** The second trough reaches lower than the first, but both troughs still form the bottoming area of the W-like structure. A narrow band around only the first low would omit the second reaction. A useful zone accounts for that variation, while remaining anchored to the two lows rather than extending across unrelated prices.

**Resistance:** Resistance remains the recovery high / neckline between the lows. It does not need to be as wide as the support zone simply because support is wider. Assess the variation in each area independently.

**Interpretation caution:** An uneven low does not, by itself, prove a valid reversal pattern or make a trade. This exercise asks the student to represent the visible area clearly—not to forecast the outcome.

## Plausible alternative placements

- **Support:** A coherent zone that captures both the approximate 99.5 and 97.85 trough lows is reasonable. As a coaching range, lower boundaries around 97.0–98.4 and upper boundaries around 100.0–101.6 can be considered, so long as both trough reactions are represented and the width is not excessive.
- **Resistance:** A focused zone around the neckline high near 105.6 is defensible. Coaching ranges around lower 103.7–104.8 and upper 105.3–106.5 are plausible if the resulting band captures the relevant reaction rather than the later rally.

Do not accept an arbitrarily broad support band simply because it contains both lows. Ask whether its boundaries still tell the learner something useful about where price reacted.

## Feedback text library

**Both zones well placed**
> Good work accounting for the uneven lows. Your support zone allows for the deeper second probe without insisting both lows match exactly. Your resistance zone separately captures the intervening recovery high. The two zones serve different purposes, so keep their widths tied to their own evidence.

**Support only captures the first low**
> Your zone fits the first trough, but the second probe reaches lower. Widen or reposition the lower boundary enough to represent both reactions. Avoid making it so broad that it includes unrelated price action.

**Support is much too wide**
> You included both lows, but the zone also covers more prices than the chart evidence requires. Start with the two trough reactions, then tighten the boundaries around that cluster. The goal is not to catch every possible price; it is to describe the observed area.

**Resistance is misplaced**
> Look between the two troughs for the recovery high. That is the neckline area to mark. The later climb to the right of the chart is not the intervening resistance taught in this exercise.

**Student claims the deeper low invalidates the whole structure**
> A deeper second low changes the shape, but does not automatically settle the interpretation. In this activity, observe whether both lows belong to a coherent area and describe the variation. Avoid turning a visual pattern into a guaranteed conclusion.

## Check for understanding

1. Why can support be wider in this example? **The two lows are less even, so the area needs to capture a deeper second reaction.**
2. Should resistance automatically be widened to match support? **No. Each zone's width should reflect its own price behaviour.**
3. Does a lower second trough prove the pattern will fail? **No. It changes the evidence, but this chart alone does not guarantee the next move.**

# Example 03 — Wider neckline

## Teaching objective
Mark resistance when the recovery high is less precise and price hesitates across a broader area. Reinforce that support and resistance zones should be sized independently.

## Reference answer

| Zone | Reference lower boundary | Reference upper boundary | What it represents |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Support | 98.7 | 101.0 | The two lows, whose generated sample trough wicks are approximately 99.45 and 98.90. |
| Resistance / neckline | 104.0 | 106.8 | A broader recovery / hesitation area around the intervening high; relevant local sample high is approximately 106.7. |

These are reference coordinates for the current prototype chart. The neckline band is wider because this example is designed to show a less precise recovery area.

## Why these zones are reasonable

**Support:** Both troughs remain anchored in a relatively similar low area. Do not widen support just because resistance is broader in this example.

**Resistance:** The recovery stalls across a broader band rather than respecting one clean price. A zone accounts for the cluster of nearby prices around that swing high. A thin line can imply a level of precision the chart does not support.

**Usefulness test:** Broad does not automatically mean correct. The zone should still isolate the relevant recovery area; it should not cover most of the entire move or merge unrelated chart structure into one vague band.

## Plausible alternative placements

- **Support:** A focused zone accounting for trough lows near 99.45 and 98.90 can be accepted. Coaching ranges around lower 98.0–99.4 and upper 100.1–101.5 are plausible if both lows remain represented.
- **Resistance:** A broader zone around the recovery area near 104.0–106.8 can be defensible. Coaching ranges around lower 103.5–104.8 and upper 106.0–107.5 are plausible if they capture the cluster without expanding far into unrelated prices.

The reference resistance band is deliberately broader than the first two examples. Do not mark every wide band as good: the student should be able to point to the recovery reactions that justify the width.

## Feedback text library

**Both zones well placed**
> Good reading. Your support stays anchored to the two lows, while your resistance zone allows for the broader recovery area. Notice that the two zones do not need identical widths. Let the chart evidence—not symmetry in your drawing—determine each boundary.

**Support was widened unnecessarily**
> The resistance reaction is broad here, but the two troughs still sit in a more focused area. Narrow support around the low cluster rather than copying the wider resistance width onto both zones.

**Resistance is too narrow**
> Your line may catch one part of the recovery high, but it misses the broader hesitation visible around the neckline. Consider widening the band enough to include the nearby reaction prices, then check that it still isolates this area.

**Resistance is too wide**
> The chart does show a broader neckline, but the zone should still identify a specific area. Reduce the upper or lower boundary so the band focuses on the recovery hesitation rather than unrelated price action.

**Resistance is placed at the later high**
> Return to the swing high between the two lows. The exercise asks you to mark the neckline before the later rally, not the more recent highs on the right side of the chart.

## Check for understanding

1. Why is the reference resistance wider here? **The recovery high and hesitation span a broader area.**
2. Should support be widened by the same amount? **No. Each zone reflects its own cluster of reactions.**
3. Does a broad neckline guarantee a breakout or rejection? **No. It represents an area to observe, not a prediction.**

# Suggested exercise-level feedback logic

Apply this independently to support and resistance, then combine the two feedback statements.

1. **Check structural location first.** Is support near both troughs? Is resistance near the intervening neckline? A well-drawn zone at the wrong structural location should receive a location correction before width feedback.
2. **Check coverage.** Does the zone include or closely bracket the relevant reaction points? For support, check both troughs, not just the deepest wick. For resistance, check the intervening recovery high, not later highs.
3. **Check width.** Is the zone focused enough to remain meaningful? A slightly different boundary is acceptable; an excessively broad band should be refined.
4. **Check the written reason.** Does it refer to observable reactions, wicks, closes or the swing structure? If the reason is only “because price should reverse here,” prompt the learner to distinguish evidence from prediction.
5. **Give a next action.** Every feedback state should explain one actionable revision, such as “include the second trough,” “move the zone back to the neckline,” or “narrow the band around the recovery cluster.”

## Suggested summary shown after both zones

> You have mapped two different parts of the W-shaped structure: the trough area and the intervening recovery high. The reference zones are examples of defensible interpretations, not exact answers. Review which price reactions you captured, where your boundaries differ, and whether your explanation is supported by the candles. Support and resistance describe areas of past response; they do not guarantee the next move.

## Implementation notes

- Store the reference zone boundaries per example, alongside this explanatory text.
- Store separate rationale and feedback variants for support and resistance; avoid one generic score for both.
- Use broad location/coverage/width bands for automated prompts and provide the written explanation next to them.
- Keep the student's submitted zones visible in the comparison.
- For future member progress, record the example identifier, submitted zone boundaries, selected reasons, quiz answers and completion timestamp against the member account. That persistence and dashboard integration are future work, not part of this answer-key draft.
