# Day 11 — Candlestick Patterns: Only the 5 Worth Knowing

**🎬 The hammer that ended the fastest crash in history**

March 23, 2020. The S&P 500 had fallen 34% in 23 trading days — the fastest bear market
ever recorded, Nifty crashing in lockstep, circuit breakers tripping, headlines
screaming depression. On that Monday, the S&P printed a daily candle with a long lower
wick and a small body near the top of its range — in the old language, a **hammer**.

Now, here's what separates traders from pattern-collectors: a hammer in the middle of
nowhere means nothing. This hammer meant something, because it appeared *after an
extended move* (34% down), *at a level* (the prior 2018–19 consolidation zone the whole
world was watching), with *panic volume* (the highest in history). Buyers hadn't just
appeared — they had absorbed the largest stampede of sellers in a decade and pushed the
close well off the lows.

The next five months: S&P **+50%**, new all-time highs by August. Nobody knew COVID
would "resolve." The candle didn't know either. It only reported what the auction did
that day: sellers exhausted, buyers in control. Today you learn to read those reports —
and to ignore every pattern that isn't sitting at a level.

> 💬 **Be really honest:** that March 2020 hammer — the candle that ended the fastest crash in history — do you think YOU would have bought it, in real time, while the news was apocalyptic? Note yes or no. Either way, we'll replay exactly what it *felt* like by the end of today — and you'll see what the buyers saw.

## What you'll learn
- The 5 patterns that matter and what each one says
- Why context decides whether a pattern works or fails
- How to trade a pattern without getting trapped

## The golden rule of all patterns

> **A pattern at a random location is just a shape. The same pattern at a major level,
> after a strong move, with a trend behind it is a signal.**

Before you care about the candle, ask: (1) Are we **at a level**? (2) Has price
**travelled** to get there? The March 2020 hammer passes both tests; a hammer on a quiet
Tuesday in the middle of a range fails both.

## The 5 patterns that matter

### 1. Pin bar (hammer / shooting star)
One long wick (2–3× the body), tiny body at the opposite end.
- **Says:** price was pushed hard one way and violently rejected — like March 23, 2020.
- **Trade it:** at levels, in the direction of rejection. A hammer at support = buyers
  defending; a shooting star at resistance = sellers defending.

### 2. Engulfing
A candle whose body completely swallows the previous body, opposite direction.
- **Says:** one side seized complete control of the period.
- **Trade it:** after a pullback within a trend — a bullish engulfing at a rising
  trendline is one of the cleanest entries that exists.

### 3. Inside bar
A candle entirely inside the previous candle's range.
- **Says:** volatility contraction; a decision is coming.
- **Trade it:** as a breakout trigger — buy the break of the *mother* candle's high
  (with the trend). The inside bar itself isn't the signal; its break is.

### 4. Doji (at extremes only)
Open ≈ close; a frozen tug-of-war.
- **Says:** indecision — meaningful *only* after an extended move or at a major level.
  A doji after a 12-day rally at resistance is a warning; a doji mid-range is wallpaper.

### 5. Morning star / evening star (3-candle)
Strong candle → tiny stall candle → strong opposite candle.
- **Says:** momentum handover. Rarest of the five, strongest at the right place.

## Reliability ranking (at a level, with trend)

1. Engulfing at a pullback level
2. Pin bar at a level
3. Morning/evening star at a level
4. Inside-bar breakout (trigger, not standalone)
5. Doji (warning light, not entry)

> ⚠️ **Common beginner mistakes**
> 1. Trading every pattern instead of every pattern *at a level*.
> 2. Entering on the pattern candle itself — the standard play is the break of the
>    signal candle's extreme, stop beyond its other end.
> 3. Memorizing exotic names (kicking, tasuki, ladder bottom). Nobody's P&L ever
>    depended on a "unique three river bottom."

## 🇮🇳 The Indian story: Nifty's own COVID hammer

You don't have to look at the S&P for the March 2020 lesson — Nifty printed its own.
After the circuit-frozen panic of March 13 and the 7,511 low on March 23, Nifty's
candles showed the same signature: panic selling being absorbed. When the government
announced the nationwide lockdown on March 24 — seemingly the worst possible news —
Nifty stopped falling and began recovering. The candles were reporting what headlines
couldn't: the sellers were exhausted, and someone was absorbing everything they threw.
From 7,511, Nifty added roughly **85% in eight months**. Every Indian trader saw those
candles in real time. Today you know how to read the next ones.

## Key takeaways
- Pattern + level + trend = signal (March 2020's hammer had all three).
- The 5: pin bar, engulfing, inside bar, doji (extremes only), morning/evening star.
- Enter on the break of the signal candle, stop beyond its far end.
