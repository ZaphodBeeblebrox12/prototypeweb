# Day 9 — Support & Resistance: Levels That Actually Matter

**🎬 The level that rejected Bitcoin four times — then made the trade of the decade**

Between mid-2019 and mid-2020, Bitcoin ran into roughly the same zone — $10,000 to
$10,500 — **four separate times**. Every visit, sellers slammed it back: June 2019
(pumped to $13,800 on the Libra hype, collapsed), October 2019 (China blockchain speech
pump, rejected within 48 hours), February 2020 (broke $10k, then COVID destroyed
everything), and July 2020 (poked above, pulled back one more time).

Think about what was happening at that zone: every trader on Earth could see $10k. The
people who bought the June 2019 top and watched -50% were *waiting* at their breakeven
to sell. Short sellers who'd won four times *expected* it to hold again and placed sell
orders. It wasn't magic — it was **unfinished business and remembered pain**, stacked
on top of each other at one price.

Then, at the end of July 2020, Bitcoin closed above the zone decisively — and over the
next five months ran from about $10,000 to **$42,000**. The fifth test wasn't the charm.
The fifth test was the *break* — because by then the sellers had spent their ammunition
across four failed defenses. That's the pattern you'll learn today, and it plays out
on Nifty every single quarter.

## Why S/R exists: market memory

Every price on your chart is a price where real money changed hands. People remember:

- **Support** = a zone where price fell to before, buyers stepped in, and price rallied.
  The buyers who missed it return; shorts from higher up buy back there. The zone fills
  with buy orders.
- **Resistance** = the mirror image: a zone where price rallied to before and got
  rejected. Sellers who missed the exit re-sell there.

> S/R works because of unfinished business and remembered pain — not line-drawing magic.
> When millions of traders watch the same obvious level, their orders make it real.

## Zones, not lines

Beginners draw thin lines; professionals draw boxes. Price almost never reverses at an
exact tick — it reverses within a band, because different participants place orders at
slightly different prices.

## How to mark levels properly

1. **Start on the daily chart.** Drop to lower timeframes only after the daily map is done.
2. Mark every **swing high/low** your eye catches in two seconds. If you have to squint,
   it's not a level.
3. Convert each swing point into a **zone** covering the wick extremes of the turning area.
4. Mark **round numbers** — Bitcoin's $10k, Nifty's 24,000, stocks' ₹500/₹1,000. Humans
   place orders at round numbers instinctively.
5. Mark **prior day/week high and low** — day traders watch these religiously.
6. Delete everything else. **Three to five zones per chart maximum.** More lines = more
   excuses to take bad trades.

## What makes a level strong?

| Factor | Strong level | Weak level |
|---|---|---|
| Touches | 2–4 clean rejections | 1 touch (untested) |
| Rejection style | Fast, violent (long wick) | Slow drift into it |
| Timeframe | Daily/weekly levels | 5-minute levels |
| Volume at touch | High-volume rejection | Thin, quiet touch |
| Age | Recent (weeks–months) | Ancient (years) |
| Confluence | Round number / prior level / MA | Stands alone |

**The paradox:** after 4–5 touches, a level gets *weaker*, not stronger — each visit
consumes the waiting orders. Bitcoin's $10k died on its fifth test. The level that's
"held five times" is often one visit from breaking violently.

## Role reversal: the flip

When support breaks decisively (full body close below the zone, on volume), it very
often becomes resistance on the retest. Sellers defend their breakeven; new shorts pile
in. This single concept — *trade the flip, not the break* — becomes the backbone of your
pullback entries on Day 22.

> ⚠️ **Common beginner mistakes**
> 1. A level at *every* swing point until the chart looks like a barcode.
> 2. Trading the first touch — the first approach to a level is information; the trade
>    comes from how price *reacts* there.
> 3. Stop loss *inside* the zone (the stop hunt eats you) — stops go beyond the zone.
> 4. Moving a "broken" level to make it fit. A level that broke is history.

## 🇮🇳 The Indian story: Nifty 12,000 — the flip that launched a rally

After COVID, Nifty 12,000 became India's version of Bitcoin's $10k wall. In January
2020, Nifty was trading above 12,000 comfortably. In March, it crashed far below. Then,
in **November 2020, Nifty closed back above 12,000** — and that old support, reclaimed,
became the launchpad: the level that had *supported* prices in January became the floor
that *refused to break* through every test in November and December. Sellers who had
waited all year at their breakeven got their exit; buyers defended; and from that flip,
Nifty ran to nearly 14,000 by year-end and 18,000 within the year. One round-number zone,
one flip, eight thousand points. The $10k story, translated to NSE.

## Key takeaways
- S/R = zones where remembered orders cluster ($10k Bitcoin = four defenses, then the break).
- Mark 3–5 daily zones max. Round numbers and prior day/week levels count.
- Strong = violent rejections, high volume, higher timeframe, confluence. 4–5 touches =
  running out of sellers.
- Broken support often becomes resistance — the flip is tradeable.
