# Day 16 — Multiple Timeframe Analysis (Level 2 Finale)

**🎬 The day Meta lost $230 billion — and how three timeframes would have read it**

On February 2, 2022, Meta (Facebook) reported earnings after the close: slowing growth,
heavy metaverse spending. The next morning the stock opened down and closed **−26%** in
a single session — about **$230 billion** of market value, then the largest one-day loss
in stock-market history.

Watch that week through three timeframes, the way you will learn today:

- **Daily (bias):** for months before earnings, Meta had been making lower highs from
  its September 2021 peak — the big picture had already turned down while headlines were
  still warm. The daily said: *bearish bias.*
- **1-hour (setup):** into earnings day, the stock was compressed under a clear lower
  boundary near $320 — a defined level where sellers had repeatedly shown up. The 1H
  said: *sell zone identified.*
- **15-minute (trigger):** the next morning's gap-down open, then a failed retest of
  the prior day's low from below — the first lower-timeframe structure break. The 15m
  said: *trigger fired.*

Nobody needed to predict earnings. The top-down process would have had a trader biased
short for weeks, with a level, waiting for a trigger — and the trigger printed the
morning after, on the largest one-day collapse in history. That's the power of doing
things in the right order. Today you install that order.

## The top-down workflow

| Step | Timeframe | Question | Tools |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Daily (or 4H) | **Bias** — which direction am I allowed to trade? | 200 MA, trend structure, major zones |
| 2 | 1H (or 15m) | **Setup** — where exactly is my trade zone? | S/R zones, trendline rails, Fib |
| 3 | 15m / 5m | **Trigger** — what candle/pattern gets me in? | Day 11's patterns, inside-bar breaks |

> **The rule that resolves 90% of confusion:** the higher timeframe's answer outranks the
> lower's. Daily says down and your 5-minute chart "looks bullish" (as it did inside Meta
> on January 31)? The 5-minute chart is noise. Trade with the daily or stand aside —
> never against it.

## Why lower timeframes lie

A 5-minute chart contains ~75 candles per day — mostly random auction noise around the
day's real business. A pattern that "looks perfect" on 5 minutes forms and dies in an
hour. Meanwhile the daily level it sits on might have three weeks of institutional
orders behind it — like Meta's $320 boundary.

Zooming in doesn't add information — it multiplies *opinions*. Form ONE opinion (on the
daily) and use small timeframes only for execution precision: cheaper entries, correctly
placed stops.

## The full trade, built top-down

**Example — long trade (the Nifty version of the Meta story, in reverse):**
1. **Daily:** Nifty above rising 200 MA, pulling back to a major prior-breakout zone.
   → **Bias: long only.**
2. **1H:** Pullback has retraced into the 50–61.8% Fib band, sitting on the 50 EMA, at
   the daily zone. RSI curled up from ~45. → **Setup: buy zone marked.**
3. **15m:** An inside bar forms at the zone; you buy its break. Stop below the zone's
   low. Target: prior daily high / 127.2% extension. → **Trigger on. Risk defined.**

One sentence: *"Long Nifty at a triple-confirmed daily zone, in an established uptrend,
triggered by a 15-minute pattern, risking below structure."* If you can't write your
trade in one sentence like that, you don't have a trade — you have a feeling.

## The triple-screen rules

1. **Maximum three timeframes per trade.** More = analysis paralysis.
2. **Never trade when timeframes disagree** — disagreement means "wait."
3. **Risk is defined on the timeframe of your stop** (15m trigger, daily stop → size off
   the daily distance; Day 20 makes this mechanical).

> ⚠️ **Common beginner mistakes**
> 1. Starting on the 5-minute chart and "confirming" on the daily — backwards.
    Confirmation flows downward, never upward.
> 2. Dropping to 1-minute charts for "precision" — you're just paying the spread more often.
> 3. Ignoring step 1 when "the setup is too good" — Meta's bulls did that into February 2022.

## 🇮🇳 The Indian story: Hindenburg vs Adani — three timeframes, one avalanche

On January 24, 2023, Hindenburg Research published its report on the Adani Group, and
Adani Enterprises — at ₹3,389 — began a collapse that took it to roughly **₹1,529 within
weeks**, wiping about **$150 billion** off the group's market value. Read it top-down
like Meta: **Daily bias** — the group's chart had been a vertical parabolic run; every
such run ends, question is when. The daily said: risky longs only. **1H setup** — the
first two days broke initial supports and failed to reclaim them: a defined sell zone.
**15m trigger** — failed retests of broken levels, each one a short entry with a stop
above structure. Traders didn't need to judge the report's claims. The three timeframes
described the crowd's behavior while the Twitter debate raged. Same architecture as
Meta's $230B day — same architecture for Nifty every week.

## Key takeaways
- Bias (daily) → Setup (1H) → Trigger (15m). Three timeframes, in that order, every time.
- Higher timeframe wins every disagreement ($230 billion agreed).
- If you can't describe the trade in one sentence across timeframes, don't take it.
