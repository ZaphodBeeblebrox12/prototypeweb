# Day 12 — Chart Patterns: Double Tops, Triangles, Flags

**🎬 The head & shoulders that announced −78% — while the headlines stayed bullish**

Between March 2000 and September 2001, the Nasdaq Composite built one of the most
famous structures in chart history: a **head and shoulders** topping pattern. Left
shoulder near 5,000 in March 2000. Head — the final blowoff top at **5,132 on March
10, 2000** (the dot-com peak). Right shoulder through 2001: a lower high near 2,300–2,400,
visibly weaker. Then the neckline broke.

Here is what makes this story unforgettable: *the news stayed bullish the entire time.*
"New economy." "It's different this time." Analysts were still upgrading internet stocks
as the neckline gave way. The pattern didn't read the news — it read the *transfer of
power*: each rally was making a lower high because the buyers of March 2000 were trapped,
and every bounce was distribution. By October 2002, the Nasdaq traded near **1,114** —
down **78%** from the head. The technicians who trusted the shape were out a year before
the economists admitted what had happened.

Today you learn that shape — and its five siblings.

> 💬 **Trust your eyes first:** look at the shape of the Nasdaq 2000–2001 chart in today's story — the rise, the bump, the lower bump, the slide. Without knowing a single rule… does that shape *look* like something ending to you? Note your raw instinct. Today we give that instinct rules.

## What you'll learn
- The 6 chart patterns worth knowing
- How to project targets with measured moves
- Why volume is the pattern's lie detector

## Two families

- **Reversal patterns** appear after an extended move and signal exhaustion: double
  top/bottom, head & shoulders.
- **Continuation patterns** appear *mid-trend* as the market catches its breath: flags,
  pennants, triangles. They resolve in the trend's direction more often than not.

## The 6 patterns that matter

### 1. Double top (M)
Two rallies to the same zone, the second weaker, then a break below the middle low
(the **neckline**). The crowd that bought the second top is trapped; their exits fuel
the fall.

### 2. Double bottom (W)
The mirror: two dips to the same zone, the second holds, break above the neckline.
Trapped shorts fuel the rally. **Complete only when the neckline breaks** — before that
it's just two lows.

### 3. Head & shoulders / inverse
Left shoulder → higher head (final euphoria — Nasdaq, March 2000) → lower right shoulder
(buyers exhausted). Neckline break = confirmation. It works because it visibly maps the
transfer of power from buyers to sellers.

### 4. Bull flag / pennant
A sharp near-vertical rally (the **flagpole**) followed by a small downward drift
(flag) or contraction (pennant). The drift is profit-taking, not reversal. Break of the
flag's upper edge continues the move.

### 5. Symmetrical triangle
Lower highs + higher lows converging — compression. Trade the breakout either way,
with volume as the judge.

### 6. Ascending/descending triangle
Flat edge on one side (a real wall of orders), sloping edge on the other. Flats held
3+ times are powerful; the break of the flat side is often explosive.

## The measured move: how to set targets

> **Target = breakout point ± the pattern's height**

Double bottom with 100 points between lows and neckline → target = neckline + 100.
Flag with a 200-point pole → target = flag break + 200. You don't need to believe it —
log it over 30 trades and let your own data convince you (Day 28 makes this systematic).

## Volume: the pattern's lie detector

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| Pattern forming on *declining* volume | Healthy — sellers drying up / buyers resting in flags |
| Neckline break on big volume | Confirmed — trapped side rushing out |
| Breakout on weak volume | Suspect — likely a fakeout (Day 22) |
| Second top on *higher* volume with no price progress | Distribution — strong hands unloading into excitement. Extra bearish. |

> ⚠️ **Common beginner mistakes**
> 1. Anticipating the pattern ("it's almost a double top, I'm shorting") — patterns
>    confirm only on the neckline break. Trade the break or the retest, never the guess.
> 2. Measuring from the wrong anchor.
> 3. Ignoring the bigger trend — a double top against a monster weekly uptrend fails
>    constantly. (Even the mighty 2000 H&S had a three-year uptrend behind it — that's
>    what made it a *reversal*.)

## 🇮🇳 The Indian story: Paytm's double bottom

Paytm gave Indian charts their own textbook W. After the disastrous −27% listing in
November 2021, the stock ground lower for a year and a half — to around **₹435–500 by
April–May 2023**, where something changed: the second dip refused to undercut the first
by much, selling dried up, and then the stock broke above its neckline near ₹550–600 on
expanding volume. From that W, Paytm roughly **doubled within a year**, trading above
₹900 by mid-2024. The trapped IPO crowd finally got their exits; the patient W traders
got paid. Every element from today — two lows, a neckline break, volume confirmation,
measured move — printed on an Indian chart your students watched live.

## Key takeaways
- Reversal: double top/bottom, H&S. Continuation: flags, triangles.
- Every pattern has a trigger (neckline/boundary break) and a measured-move target.
- Volume confirms; declining volume into the pattern + expansion on the break = the
  2000 Nasdaq recipe.
