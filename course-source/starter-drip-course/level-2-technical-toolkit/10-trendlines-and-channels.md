# Day 10 — Trendlines & Channels

**🎬 The channel that carried oil to $147 — and the line that warned the crash**

From early 2007 to July 2008, crude oil climbed inside one of the cleanest channels in
commodity history: from around $60 to a peak of **$147.27 on July 11, 2008**. Along the
way, every pullback found buyers near the lower rail — roughly three touches — and
every rally stalled near the upper rail. Traders who simply *rode the hallway* made
fortunes while analysts debated peak oil.

Then the channel broke. Not dramatically at first — just a slide through the lower rail
in July–August 2008, exactly the "warning, not signal" behavior you'll learn today. By
December, oil traded near **$33** — a −77% collapse in five months. The trendline break
didn't predict the global financial crisis. It simply told anyone watching that the
auction had changed hands: buyers no longer defended the lower rail. Months of headlines
later, the headlines agreed with the line.

Today you learn to draw that line honestly — and, more importantly, to read its break
honestly: as a warning, not an alarm.

## What you'll learn
- How to define and identify a trend objectively
- Drawing trendlines that are actually respected
- Using channels to plan entries *and* exits

## What is a trend, objectively?

Strip away the jargon and a trend is just a pattern of swings:

- **Uptrend** = higher highs (HH) + higher lows (HL).
- **Downtrend** = lower lows (LL) + lower highs (LH).
- **Range** = neither — price oscillates between two horizontal zones (yesterday's lesson).

> "The trend is your friend" is incomplete. The full version: **the trend on *your*
> timeframe is your friend.** Oil in 2008 was a monster uptrend on the daily — and a
> terrifying downtrend by September, on the same chart, in the same year.

## Drawing trendlines without fooling yourself

1. In an uptrend, connect at least **two higher lows**. Two points define the line;
   a **third touch confirms** it. Two touches is a hypothesis, not a level.
2. Connect the **wicks** — the extremes are where the actual fight happened.
3. Extend it forward. No adjusting to fit more points. If price slices through and your
   "trendline" looks silly, it was wrong — delete and redraw from the new structure.

## Reading the trendline

- **Respect** = price falls to the line, wicks it, bounces. Trend healthy.
- **Breaking the line** ≠ trend over. A trendline break is a *warning*, not a signal.
  What ends a trend is price making its **first lower low** — structure breaking, not a
  line breaking. Oil's lower rail broke in July 2008; the *trend* truly ended when rallies
  started failing at lower highs and the August–September swings broke down.

⚠️ **Common beginner mistake:** treating every trendline break as a reversal signal and
shorting a raging uptrend. The pros fade trendlines maybe 20% of the time; beginners try
it 80% of the time because it "feels smart."

## Channels: the trend's hallway

Draw a line parallel to your trendline, anchored at the intervening high (uptrend). Price
now travels in a hallway:

- **Lower rail (uptrend)** = the buy zone. Pullbacks to it are the highest-probability
  entries in trending markets (2007–08 oil paid these repeatedly).
- **Upper rail** = the profit-taking zone. First touch in a mature trend = bank partial
  profits, not add.

A decisive close **outside** the channel = the trend is either accelerating (a new,
steeper channel forms) or ending (like oil, July 2008). You don't know which yet —
reduce size and let structure tell you.

## 🇮🇳 The Indian story: the 2003–2007 channel that made a generation

While oil ran its channel to $147, India had its own masterclass: the great bull market
of **2003–2007**, when the Sensex climbed from around 3,000 to 21,000 inside one of the
most orderly uptrends in Indian history. Pullbacks kept finding buyers at the rising
lower rail — 2004's election shock, 2006's May crash (−30% in weeks!), 2007's
corrections — every dip to the channel was bought, every violation repaired. Traders who
simply respected the hallway rode a 7× move. Then in January 2008, the channel broke,
lower lows followed (Sensex to 7,697 by October, −63%), and the trend was over — the
line warned first, the structure confirmed it. Oil's story and the Sensex's story are the
same story, five years and one ocean apart.

## Key takeaways
- Trends = HH/HL or LL/LH. Everything else is a range.
- Two touches define, three confirm. Wicks, not closes.
- Trendline break = warning ($147 oil warned in July; the lower lows confirmed the crash).
- Channels give you a buy rail, a sell rail, and an early warning system.
