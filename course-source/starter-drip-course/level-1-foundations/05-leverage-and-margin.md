# Day 5 — Leverage & Margin: The Multiplier

**🎬 The ₹2 lakh ticket to an ₹18 lakh position**

Meet Meena. She has ₹2 lakh in her trading account, and she's bullish on the market.
Without the instrument you're about to learn, participating would be impossible: one
lot of Nifty futures represents 75 × 24,000 = **₹18 lakh of index**. Meena doesn't
have ₹18 lakh. End of story.

Except it isn't. Her broker asks for only about **₹1.5–2 lakh as margin** — a security
deposit — and lets her hold the position. That's **leverage**: the ability to control a
large position with a small deposit. It's the reason a small account can trade markets
at all, and it's available to you every single day on NSE.

Now watch the same tool do two very different jobs.

**Job one — with a plan.** Meena buys at 24,100 with a stop at 24,040 (her idea is
"wrong" below 24,040, plain and simple). Nifty rises 200 points over the next week.
Her gain: 200 × 75 = **₹15,000 — about 7.5% on her ₹2 lakh account in days.** She
controlled ₹18 lakh with ₹2 lakh, and a planned trade paid her. This is leverage doing
exactly what it was built for.

**Job two — without a plan.** Ravi, same account, same lot — but no stop, because
"it'll come back." Nifty falls 500 points in a bad week. His loss: 500 × 75 =
**₹37,500 — nearly 19% of his account**, gone. Not because leverage is evil. Because
Ravi's plan was a feeling.

Same tool. Same market. The multiplier amplified whatever was attached to it — Meena's
plan, or Ravi's hope. **Leverage is not good or bad. It simply multiplies your process.**
Today you learn to make sure what's attached to it is a plan.

> 💬 **One word will do:** Meena made ₹15,000 in a week on a planned trade. Ravi lost ₹37,500 on hope. Same tool, same market, same month. What do you think the real difference between them was? Prediction? Luck? Personality? Note your one word — then see if it survives today's lesson.

## What you'll learn
- What leverage and margin actually are (Meena's version)
- How the multiplier works in both directions
- The leverage you're actually using — and the two rules that make it safe

## What is leverage?

> **Leverage lets you control a large position with a small security deposit called
> margin.** You own the profit or loss of the full position — the margin is just the
> deposit.

Meena's example above is 9× leverage (₹18 lakh position ÷ ₹2 lakh margin). Her broker
holds the margin as security and settles her P&L in cash daily (that's "mark-to-market"
— profits added, losses deducted, every day).

## How the multiplier works

| Nifty moves | Against 1 lot (75) | On ₹2 lakh margin | With Meena's stop? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 points | ₹3,750 | 1.9% | Never reached |
| 200 points (favor) | +₹15,000 | +7.5% | Banked |
| 500 points (against) | ₹37,500 | 18.8% | Exited at −₹4,500 |

See the design: with a stop, the multiplier works **for** her on good trades and the
stop caps it on bad ones. Without a stop, there's no cap at all. The tool didn't change
— the plan did.

## The margin call

If losses eat into your margin, the broker asks for more money — a **margin call**.
Can't add funds? The position is closed for you, at whatever the market gives, usually
the worst moment. A stop loss you chose beats a margin call the broker chose, every
time. (Days 18–20 will make your stop and your size automatic.)

## The leverage you're actually using

Most beginners think: *"I don't use leverage, I trade stocks."* Check again:

| Instrument | Typical leverage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Equity delivery (CNC) | 1× | No leverage |
| Equity intraday (MIS) | 3–5× | Broker blocks a fraction of value |
| Futures | 5–10× | Daily mark-to-market |
| Options buying | Premium at risk | Can go to zero; max loss = premium paid |
| Options selling | 5–8× | Bigger obligations — we'll cover this much later |

Notice: options *buying* is the only one where the most you can lose is fixed upfront —
the premium you paid. That's one reason beginners start there.

## The two rules that make leverage safe

1. **Every leveraged position carries a stop loss chosen by you, from the first second.**
   (Day 18: placed beyond the structure, not by feeling.)
2. **Position size comes from the formula, never from the margin available.** The broker
   will happily let you use all your margin. The formula often says use a quarter of it.
   (Day 20.)

With those two rules, leverage is what lets a ₹2 lakh account build like a much larger
one. Without them, the multiplier runs the other way. Same as Meena and Ravi — the tool
was identical.

## 🇮🇳 The Indian story: crores of traders, almost none with these two rules

India runs one of the world's biggest leveraged retail markets, and SEBI measured the
result: in its FY22–FY24 study, **93% of individual F&O traders lost money**, average
losses around **₹2 lakh per trader**, total retail F&O losses near **₹1.8 lakh crore**.
Read that data carefully, though — it doesn't say leverage lost the money. It says
traders without a written stop and a calculated size lost the money. The winners in
SEBI's data used the same leverage, the same products, the same market. The difference
was never the tool. Over the next fifteen days, you install the two rules above — and
the same products that drained ₹1.8 lakh crore become usable, on your terms.

## Key takeaways
- Leverage = controlling a large position with a small margin deposit. It's the tool that
  lets small accounts trade big markets at all. (Meena: ₹18 lakh position, ₹2 lakh
  deposit, +7.5% in a planned week.)
- The multiplier runs both directions — the stop and the size decide which direction
  matters to you. Never the broker's margin limit.
- Two rules make leverage safe: your stop on every position, size from the formula.

## Today's action step
Open your broker's margin calculator. Type in one Nifty futures trade and one intraday
stock trade. Write down the margin each blocks, and your implied leverage (position
value ÷ margin). Then compute: if your stop were hit on each, what % of your account
would you lose? That last number — not the leverage number — is the only one that
matters. You're about to learn to control it precisely.

Answers: 1 — 200 × 75 = ₹15,000 ≈ +7.5% of ₹2 lakh ·
2 — Stop loss: you choose the exit level before entering. Margin call: the broker forces
an exit when your deposit runs out · 3 — Options buying (max loss = the premium you paid)
