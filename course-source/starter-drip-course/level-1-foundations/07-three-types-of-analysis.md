# Day 7 — The Three Types of Analysis

**🎬 Three men, one housing market, three completely different trades**

In 2005–2007, while Wall Street was printing money on US housing, three very different
analysts looked at the same market:

**The fundamental analyst** — Michael Burry's team at Scion Capital — read thousands of
mortgage documents, ran the default math, and concluded the loans inside mortgage bonds
were garbage. They bought insurance against those home-loan bonds failing, and held it for about a year and a half while looking *wrong* — the trade bled money until it didn't.
When 2008 came, Scion made roughly **$700 million** for its investors. His lens:
*"what is this actually worth?"*

**The technical analyst** didn't need to read a single loan file. By 2007, charts of
homebuilder stocks, mortgage insurers, and bank indices had broken their multi-year
uptrends — lower highs, neckline breaks — months before the headlines. The price action
*told* you credit was cracking. His lens: *"what is price doing right now?"*

**The sentiment analyst** watched the crowd: in 2006–07, CNBC ran "flip this house"
segments, taxi drivers discussed condo pre-sales, and mortgage originators handed loans
to anyone with a pulse. When *everyone* is bullish and the last skeptic has bought —
that's not confirmation, that's exhaustion. His lens: *"what is everyone feeling?"*

One market, three lenses, three valid edges. Today you learn all three — and which one
this course is built on.

> 💬 **Pick one, honestly:** three lenses — worth (A), behavior (B), mood (C). Which one have YOU actually been using till now, even accidentally? Most people are quietly shocked when they realize. Note A, B, or C — no judgment, we all start somewhere.

## What you'll learn
- Technical, fundamental, and sentiment analysis
- What question each one answers
- Which style fits which trader

## 1. Fundamental analysis — *"What is it worth?"*

Revenue, profits, debt, management, industry trends, macro. Home country: investors and
position traders. Strength: catches big moves early, ignores noise. Weakness: *"the market
can stay irrational longer than you can stay solvent"* — Burry's investors nearly pulled
out before the payoff, because being early looks exactly like being wrong.

## 2. Technical analysis — *"What is it doing right now?"*

Trends, levels, patterns, volume — no opinions, only behavior. Works on anything liquid
(Nifty, gold, Bitcoin, USDINR — same rules); gives exact entries, stops, targets.
Weakness: reads the *present*, not the future.

## 3. Sentiment analysis — *"What is everyone feeling?"*

Put-call ratios, open interest shifts, funding rates, news euphoria. Extremes in
sentiment often mark tops (2007 housing) and bottoms (March 2020, when panic peaked and
so did the selling). Weakness: hard to time; the crowd can stay greedy for a long time.

## Which one do you use?

> **Fundamentals decide *what* to watch. Sentiment warns *when* the crowd is extreme.
> Technicals decide *when and where* to pull the trigger.**

## Why this course teaches technicals first

Exact, testable rules (enter here, stop here, target here); works across every liquid
market; fundamentals can be added later as a filter, sentiment as a warning light.

## 🇮🇳 The Indian story: Paytm's listing and Reliance's raise — the same year, opposite lenses

2021 gave you both lenses in one year. **Fundamental:** in April–June 2020, while the
market was crashing, Reliance Industries raised **₹1.52 lakh crore in 58 days** selling
stakes in Jio — investors read the *business* and paid up even mid-panic. The stock led
Nifty's recovery. **Sentiment:** in November 2021, Paytm's IPO listed at ₹1,955 against
a ₹2,150 issue price and closed **−27% on day one**, then fell toward ₹500 as IPO euphoria
— India's own dot-com moment — deflated. Everyone wanted in; the crowd's excitement WAS
the warning. Same market, same year: one trade from the fundamentals, one lesson from
the mood.

## Key takeaways
- Fundamental = worth · Technical = behavior · Sentiment = mood. (2008 showed all three
  working in parallel.)
- Professionals blend all three; beginners master technicals first.
- Every tool answers a different question — confusion comes from asking the wrong tool.

## Today's action step
Open the chart of one stock you like. Ask the three questions out loud — What is it
worth? What is it doing? What is everyone feeling? Notice how each question pulls you
toward a different decision. That awareness is the lesson.

Answers: 1 — Technical · 2 — Fundamental; can stay "undervalued" (falling) far longer
than expected · 3 — Selling/shorting, or at least taking profits
