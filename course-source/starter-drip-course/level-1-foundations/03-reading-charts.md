# Day 3 — Reading Charts: Candles, Timeframes & Volume

**🎬 One tweet, one candle, $365 billion gone**

On May 13, 2021, at 6:06 PM, Elon Musk tweeted that Tesla would stop accepting Bitcoin
for environmental reasons. Within hours, Bitcoin printed a candle that fell from roughly
$54,000 to below $46,000 — nearly **$365 billion wiped off crypto's total value in a
single move**. Six days later, on May 19, a second wave hit: China reiterated its crypto
ban, and Bitcoin dropped 30% in a day, at one point 50% below its April high.

If you'd looked at those days on a chart, you wouldn't have needed the news first — the
candles *told* you. A giant red body with a long lower wick: sellers slammed price down,
buyers fought back at the lows. Volume bars three times the normal height: this wasn't
retail noise, it was a stampede. Today you learn to read exactly that language.

> 💬 **Try this before reading on:** open any chart you have and look at the last candle for five full seconds. Don't scroll, don't analyze. Just look. What do you think it's saying? Hold that guess — by the end of today, you'll read that candle the way you read a headline. Write your first impression in your notes if you like.

## What you'll learn
- Anatomy of a candlestick (using the May 2021 dump)
- How to read any timeframe
- What volume tells you that price hides

## Anatomy of a candlestick

A candle compresses four prices from its time period into one picture: **Open** (start),
**Close** (end), **High** and **Low** (the extremes). The thick **body** spans open →
close; the thin **wicks** show the high/low excursions.

- **Bullish candle** (green): close *above* open → buyers won the period
- **Bearish candle** (red): close *below* open → sellers won the period

**Read the wicks like a fight replay:** on May 19, 2021, Bitcoin's daily candle had a
massive *lower* wick — sellers pushed it down ~50% from the April top, and buyers
rejected those prices so hard that the candle closed far off its low. That long lower
wick was the first footprint of the bottoming process. A long *upper* wick is the
mirror: buyers pushed up and got slapped back. Wicks are footprints of who lost the fight.

## Timeframes: the same movie, different zoom

A timeframe is just how long each candle represents:

| Timeframe | Each candle = | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1D (daily) | One full day | "What's the big picture trend?" |
| 1H | One hour | "Where are institutions active?" |
| 15m / 5m | 15 / 5 minutes | "Where exactly do I enter?" |
| 1m | One minute | Scalping territory — ignore for now |

> **Golden rule:** the lower the timeframe, the more noise. During the May 2021 crash,
the *daily* candle told a clear story of panic-then-defense. The *1-minute* chart that
day was a seizure — thousands of whipsaws that would have triggered and stopped out
anything mechanical. Same event, different truth, depending on zoom.

## Volume: the lie detector

**Volume** = how many contracts/shares changed hands in that candle. Price tells you
*where*; volume tells you *how much conviction* was behind it.

- Big move + big volume → real move (May 19, 2021: record volume, real repricing)
- Big move + tiny volume → suspect move, often reverses
- Breakout + volume spike → the breakout is genuine

During March 2020, global exchanges printed their highest volumes in history — the
crash was real, and so was the recovery. When volume *dries up* at the top of a long
rally, pros start paying very close attention.

## 🇮🇳 The Indian story: the Budget-day wick

You don't need crypto for violent candles — India prints them too. On **February 1, 2021,
Budget day**, Nifty opened strong, crossed 14,000 in euphoria, then reversed over
**1,000 points intraday** to close negative — a massive upper wick on the daily chart.
Traders who chased the morning breakout bought the top of that wick; the candle itself
recorded exactly what happened: buyers pushed, sellers rejected, control changed hands
in hours. And during March 2020, Nifty's daily candles carried the same long lower
wicks as Bitcoin's May 2021 — the footprint of panic being absorbed at the lows. The
language of candles is the same on NSE, NYSE, or Binance.

## Key takeaways
- Body = who won the period; wicks = who fought and lost (Bitcoin's May 2021 lower wick
  was buyers rejecting the panic).
- Higher timeframe = signal; lower timeframe = noise. Zoom out first.
- Volume confirms or exposes a price move.
