# Day 4 — Points, Ticks & Lot Sizes: What a Move Is Worth

**🎬 The day oil went below zero — and destroyed people who never read the contract**

On April 20, 2020, something happened that had never happened in market history: the
May WTI crude oil futures contract closed at **minus $37.63 per barrel**. Sellers were
paying buyers to take oil off their hands. Thousands of traders — many in India trading
US oil ETFs and contracts through apps — were wiped out. Some owed more than they put in.

Most of them had never read the contract they were trading. They didn't know what a
"barrel" unit meant for their P&L, that futures expire and force delivery mechanics,
that the contract they held was about to roll. They knew "oil is cheap, it must bounce."
They did not know **what one point of oil was worth to them**. Today you learn the
lesson those traders paid their accounts for — it's the least glamorous and most
profitable lesson in Level 1.

> 💬 **No scrolling back:** one lot of Nifty, and it moves 100 points. What's that in rupees? Take a real guess right now — type it as a reply if you're feeling brave. Most people who've traded for a year get this wrong, and the April 2020 oil traders *lost their accounts* getting it wrong. That's why today exists.

## What you'll learn
- Points vs ticks — and why the WTI crash was a contract-knowledge disaster
- Lot size / contract multiplier
- Converting a price move into rupees

## Why this lesson exists

Ask a beginner: *"Nifty moved 50 points — how much did you make?"* Most can't answer.
If you don't know what a move is worth in money **before** you trade, you're not trading —
you're the April 20, 2020 crowd, hoping.

## The vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Example (Nifty futures) |
|---|---|---|
| **Point** | One unit of price | 24,100 → 24,150 = 50 points |
| **Tick** | Smallest price increment | Nifty = 0.05 points |
| **Lot size** | Minimum quantity per contract | Nifty futures lot = 75 (check current) |
| **Multiplier** | Lot size × 1 (for futures) | 1 point = ₹75 per lot |
| **Tick value** | Value of one tick | 0.05 × 75 = ₹3.75 |

The oil traders' fatal gap: they knew oil's price, not their exposure. WTI's contract is
1,000 barrels per contract — a $1 move = $1,000 per contract. When oil went from $18 to
-$37, that was a **-$55,000 swing per single contract**. Knowing that number beforehand
is the entire lesson.

## The only formula you need

> **Profit/Loss = Points gained × Lot size × Number of lots**

**Example:** You buy 1 lot of Nifty futures at 24,100 and exit at 24,150.
- Points gained = 50 · Lot size = 75 · **P/L = 50 × 75 × 1 = ₹3,750** (before charges)

**Same move in a stock:** buy 100 shares at ₹500, sell at ₹510 → ₹10 × 100 = **₹1,000**.

## 📌 Case file: the charge reality check

Every trade costs: brokerage + exchange fees + STT/CTT + GST + stamp duty. On a small
win these eat 10–20% of profits. Two implications:

1. **Tiny targets don't pay** — a 5-point scalp on Nifty is ₹375 gross; costs can take a
   third of it. (This is also why the oil ETFs' churn destroyed value even before the
   crash.)
2. **Overtrading bleeds you** — 20 round-trips a day in costs can exceed your skill edge.

Exact math returns on Day 20 — today, just know the costs exist and that *knowing your
per-point value* is what keeps them proportionate.

## 🇮🇳 The Indian story: MCX crude went negative too

The Indian trader's version of the oil disaster happened at home. On April 20–21, 2020,
MCX crude oil futures — the contract thousands of Indian commodity traders held — settled
**below zero**, the first time Indian retail saw negative prices on their screens. Traders
who'd "bought the dip" in crude because it was "cheap" learned they'd never calculated
their per-barrel exposure: a ₹-1,000/barrel settlement against their position size meant
losses exceeding their margins, with brokers making emergency margin calls through the
night. Before you trade anything in India — Nifty lot, BankNifty lot, one lot of silver
MCX — write down what ONE point costs you. That single habit separates you from both the
USO crowd and the MCX crude crowd.

## Key takeaways
- P/L = points × lot size × lots. Memorize it — the April 2020 oil crowd didn't.
- Always translate a potential move into rupees *before* entering.
- Small targets + frequent trades = death by charges.
