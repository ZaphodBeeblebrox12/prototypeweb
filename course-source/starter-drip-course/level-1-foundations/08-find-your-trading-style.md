# Day 8 — Find Your Trading Style (Level 1 Finale)

**🎬 The Turtle experiment — proof that style is a system, not a personality**

In 1983, two legendary commodities traders made a bet. Richard Dennis believed great
traders were **made** — teachable. His partner Bill Eckhardt believed they were **born**.
To settle it, Dennis recruited about 20 people with *no trading experience* — a
security guard, a blackjack player, an accountant — gave them a simple mechanical
trend-following system and strict risk rules, and let them trade real money.

The "Turtles" went on to earn a reported **$175 million+** in aggregate. Here's what
matters for today's lesson: the Turtle system was a **swing/position system**. It held
trades for days to weeks, risked a fixed small fraction per trade, and ignored all
intraday noise. Dennis deliberately picked the style * beginners could execute without
losing their minds* — not the fastest, not the flashiest, the *survivable* one.

Forty years later, the lesson is unchanged: the best style for you isn't the one that
makes the most money in theory — it's the one your life and your nervous system can
execute without breaking. Today you pick yours. Then you take the Level 1 quiz. 🎓

## The four styles

| Style | Holding period | Time needed | Stress level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scalping** | Seconds–minutes | Full day, fast reflexes | 🔴 Extreme |
| **Day trading** | Minutes–hours | Market hours, focused | 🟠 High |
| **Swing trading** | Days–weeks | 30–60 min/day, after close | 🟡 Medium |
| **Position trading** | Weeks–months | A few hours per week | 🟢 Low |

## Choose based on your life, not your ego

Ask honestly:

1. **Time:** Can you watch markets 9:15–3:30 daily? → day trading is possible. Job + family?
   → swing trading. That's most people, and the Turtles proved it's enough.
2. **Temperament:** Do fast decisions excite you or wreck you? Do losses at 9:45 AM ruin
   your whole day? Swing trading forgives a busy schedule *and* a sensitive nervous system.
3. **Capital:** Smaller accounts can't absorb scalping's costs and slippage. Swing
   trading stretches capital further.

> **Course recommendation:** start with **swing trading** — the Turtle way. Higher
> timeframes = less noise, fewer decisions, more time to think. You can always go
> faster later. Going from fast to slow is much harder than slow to fast.

## ⚠️ One thing before you go

Whatever style you pick, the next three levels apply to all of them. Support/resistance,
risk management, psychology — universal. Style is just your seat on the same plane.

## 🇮🇳 The Indian story: the Big Bull held Titan for decades

Rakesh Jhunjhunwala — the "Big Bull" of Dalal Street — bought Titan in the early 2000s
for a few rupees per share and held it for **two decades** as it compounded past ₹2,000+
(adjusted). That is position trading at its extreme: weeks-to-months becomes
years-to-decades when the trend and the business keep paying. On the other end of the
Indian spectrum, the intraday F&O crowd trades seconds-to-hours — and SEBI's data says
93% of them lose. India doesn't lack a style debate; it lacks survival statistics. The
Turtles chose the survivable style. Jhunjhunwala proved what it compounds into. Same
lesson, two continents.

## Key takeaways
- Style must fit your real life — time, temperament, capital. The Turtles won with the
  *survivable* style, not the fastest one.
- Start slow (swing), speed up later if you want to.
