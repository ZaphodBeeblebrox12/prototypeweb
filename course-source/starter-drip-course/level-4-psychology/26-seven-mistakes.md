# Day 26 — The 7 Mistakes That Kill Beginner Accounts

**🎬 The bank that died of "averaging down"**

Yes Bank was once India's fastest-growing private bank — ₹400+ per share in 2018,
darling of every mutual fund. Then governance questions, then a full-blown crisis, and
by March 2020 the RBI had to impose a moratorium. The stock, eventually, traded near
**₹10** — down over 95% from its peak.

Now picture the most common Indian retail story of that era: an investor buys Yes Bank
at ₹350. It falls to ₹280 — "cheap now, quality bank, averaging." It falls to ₹180 —
"double down, this is a once-in-a-decade price." ₹90: "it can't go lower, it's a
SYSTEMIC bank." At every step, the decision felt like investing wisdom — buy low,
lower your average. The math, though, was doing something else entirely: each "average"
multiplied the position size at progressively worse prices, turning a ₹1 lakh opinion
into a ₹5 lakh conviction built entirely on a falling knife. The stock that "couldn't
go lower" went ~95% lower.

Yes Bank is mistake #5 on today's list — but it's the perfect doorway, because every
one of the seven mistakes is a *correct instinct from normal life, misapplied to a
probabilistic game*. That's why they're irresistible, and why each needs a mechanical
rule instead of willpower.

## The seven, at a glance

| # | Mistake | Root cause | The fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No stop loss | "It'll come back" | Stop placed before entry, always |
| 2 | Overleveraging | Greed + small account | 1–2% risk rule (Day 19) |
| 3 | Overtrading | Action feels like progress | Trade less; A-setups only |
| 4 | Moving stops | Can't accept being wrong | Stops only trail, never widen |
| 5 | Averaging losers | Sunk cost + anchoring | Add only to winners, per plan |
| 6 | Trading illiquid/news events | Excitement + FOMO | Liquidity filter + news blackout |
| 7 | No journal/review | Ego avoids evidence | Day 27 system, 2 min/trade |

## Mistake 1 — No stop loss
**Why:** accepting the small loss feels worse than hoping. **Cost:** one gap and the
account is gone. **Fix:** non-negotiable, placed before entry, in the system, every
position, first second. (Barings Bank, Day 18: a 233-year-old institution.)

## Mistake 2 — Overleveraging
**Why:** using the full margin the broker offers, because it's there. **Cost:** ruin through normal variance when position size ignores stops (even the experts — LTCM's Nobel team — found this out). **Fix:** risk-based sizing (Day 20). Too small for sensible sizing? Keep the leverage, shrink the position — the formula decides the size, never the margin limit.

## Mistake 3 — Overtrading
**Why:** action feels like progress; commissions are invisible per-trade. **Cost:** the
chart below — a no-edge trader paying spread, slippage and charges 300 times a quarter.
**The Taiwan study from Day 24 lives here:** <1% of day traders predictably profitable —
frequency is the filter that kills them. **Fix:** cap trades per day (Day 19); A-setups
only. Boredom is not a signal.

## Mistake 4 — Moving stop losses
**Why:** the trade is "so close" to coming back; widening feels like buying time.
**Cost:** converts a series of small planned losses into one account-crippling disaster —
90%+ of catastrophic losses contain this move (Leeson, Day 18). **Fix:** stops move one
direction only — toward the target. If tempted to widen: that's the tilt signal; execute
the 10-minute rule instead.

## Mistake 5 — Averaging into losers
**Why:** "cheaper now, so my average is better" — shopping logic applied to falling
knives. **Cost:** Yes Bank math — the one trade that keeps falling now holds 3–5× size
at the worst prices. Paul Tudor Jones's famous line: *"Losers average losers."*
**Fix:** adding is only legal when the trade is *already working* and the add was
pre-planned (pyramiding). Adding to red is doubling down on being wrong.

## Mistake 6 — Trading illiquid instruments & news events
**Why:** low-priced illiquid stocks "move a lot"; news feels like opportunity. **Cost:**
wide spreads eat the edge (Day 6); news slippage makes stop prices fiction (the SNB shock,
Day 6: brokers bankrupted in 15 minutes). **Fix:** liquidity filter (minimum average daily
volume) + personal news blackout: no new positions 15 min before/after major releases.

## Mistake 7 — No journal, no review
**Why:** the ego avoids evidence; review means confronting skipped boxes. **Cost:**
repeating the same mistake for years, each time feeling like bad luck. **Fix:** tomorrow.

## The meta-lesson

Every mistake on this list is an instinct that works everywhere EXCEPT leveraged,
probabilistic markets: "don't accept the loss" (sunk cost), "buy more of what's cheap"
(Yes Bank), "do more of what you're doing" (work ethic), "hold on, it'll recover"
(relationships). Trading is the one arena where your instincts are the opponent — which
is exactly why the checklist exists.

## Key takeaways
- Seven mistakes: no stop, leverage, frequency, stop-moving, averaging down, illiquid/
  news, no journal.
- Each one is an instinct — that's why plans must be written and mechanical.
- After every losing week, audit against this list before touching strategy. (Yes Bank
  survivors had six chances to stop.)
