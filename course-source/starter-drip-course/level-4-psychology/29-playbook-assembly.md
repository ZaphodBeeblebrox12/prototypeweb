# Day 29 — Full Recap: Assembling Your Playbook

**🎬 The document that turns skill into a business**

Think about the most consistent business on Earth — McDonald's. A teenager in their
first week can produce the same burger as a 10-year veteran. Not because teenagers are
culinary geniuses, but because nothing important lives in anyone's head: the recipe, the
temperatures, the timing, the cleaning schedule — it's all written down, versioned, and
trained. The business runs on the *document*, and the document never has a bad day,
never feels FOMO, never revenge-trades.

Now think about the average trader: everything lives in their head — the "system" changes
with their mood, their rules bend under pressure, and their "edge" is whatever their last
trade felt like. They are, professionally speaking, a kitchen with no recipe book — some
days brilliant, always unrepeatable, eventually closed.

Thirty days ago you started as that kitchen. Today you write the recipe book. This one
page converts everything you've learned into a document that never has a bad day.

What do you think *actually* separates a trader with a written playbook from one without — skill? luck? discipline? Something else? Note your theory. ## The playbook: one page, eight sections

```

  MY TRADING PLAYBOOK        (name · version 1.0 · date)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
1. INSTRUMENTS   Max 5, liquid only:
                 ______________________________________
2. TIMEFRAMES    Bias: ____ · Setup: ____ · Trigger: ____
3. MY SETUP(S)   The 1–2 setup types I trade (no more):
                 Entry rule: ______ Stop rule: ______ Target rule: ______
4. RISK RULES    Risk/trade: ___% · Daily limit: ___ · Max trades/day: ___
5. CHECKLIST     My 10 boxes (Day 23 version, personalized)
6. MY STATS      Expectancy: ___R · Win rate: ___ · Max losses in a row survived: ___
7. MY TRIGGERS   After a loss I tend to: ______ → my counter-rule: ______
                 After a win I tend to:  ______ → my counter-rule: ______
8. REVIEW RITUAL Weekly: (day/time) ______ · Journal: after every trade, 2 min
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

## The three filling rules

1. **Only validated content enters.** Section 3 comes from your backtest (Day 28) and
   journal (Day 27) — not from a YouTube video you watched last night. If it isn't in
   your data, it doesn't get a line in the playbook. (McDonald's doesn't put untested
   burgers on the menu because they looked good on someone else's grill.)
2. **Blank sections are answers too.** Can't fill "My stats" yet? Then your next 20
   trades exist to fill that box — that's their purpose. The playbook makes your
   weaknesses visible instead of vague.
3. **Version it.** v1.0 today. Change one thing per weekly review (Day 27), bump the
   version, note what changed. In a year you'll have v1.0 → v4.2 of a system that
   provably evolved — that document alone separates you from 95% of market participants.

## 🇮🇳 The Indian story: the dabbawala playbook (part two)

Day 23 introduced the dabbawalas' code; today you learn the rest of it — because their
"playbook" is even better than their checklist. The Mumbai dabbawala association runs
entirely on documented convention: routes, handoffs, arbitration of errors, and
discipline — no central manager directing two lakh boxes, no GPS, no app. Studies of the
system found something remarkable: the written convention survives because **it is owned
by the people who follow it**, revised by the association, not imposed. That's your
playbook, exactly: not my template laminated to your wall, but YOUR eight sections, filled
with YOUR validated setups, versioned by YOUR weekly reviews. McDonald's runs burgers on
documents; two lakh Indians run lunch on documents. Today, you run your capital on one.
