# Day 19 — Risk Management: The 1–2% Rule

**🎬 The fund with Nobel Prize winners that had to be bailed out by the Fed**

Long-Term Capital Management, founded in 1994, was the most credentialed fund in history:
two **Nobel laureates** on the team, legends from Salomon Brothers, models that had
printed roughly **40%+ returns per year**. By 1998, LTCM had about $4.8 billion of
capital supporting positions estimated around **$1.25 trillion** — including derivatives.
That's leverage of roughly **25-to-1**, give or take, on strategies that "had never lost
more than X" according to their models.

Then Russia defaulted on its debt (August 1998) — an event their history said was
essentially impossible. The models didn't break because the math was wrong; they broke
because no model contains the future. LTCM lost over **$4 billion in weeks** — about
90%+ of its capital — and its positions were so large that the Federal Reserve quietly
organized a **$3.6 billion bailout** by 14 banks, fearing global contagion. The geniuses'
edge was real. Their *sizing* is what made a real edge fatal.

If the most sophisticated risk system ever built — Nobel Prize certified — couldn't
survive 25× leverage, what exactly do you think 10× on a Nifty futures account will do?
Today you learn the rule that makes losing streaks survivable — survival being the entire
game.

> 💬 **Math quiz, guess first:** if you risked 10% of your account per trade, how many losses in a row would effectively end you? Take a real guess — reply with your number. Then meet the table that's more brutal than your guess, and infinitely more useful. (Fair warning: Nobel laureates got this wrong with real money.)

## What you'll learn
- The 1–2% rule and the brutal math behind it
- Losing streaks: guaranteed, survivable — if sized right
- Correlation, daily loss limits, and the risk rules of professional desks

## The rule

> **On any single trade, risk a maximum of 1–2% of your account. Not "invest" — RISK.
> The amount you'd lose if your stop is hit.**

On a ₹2,00,000 account: max risk per trade = ₹2,000–4,000. That's it.

## Why 1–2%? The math of survival

Losing streaks are not bad luck — they're *mathematically guaranteed*. Even a 55% win-rate
strategy will hit 8–10 losses in a row over a few hundred trades. The only question is
what those streaks do to your account:

| Risk per trade | 10 losses in a row | 15 losses in a row | State of mind after |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1% | −9.6% | −14% | Annoyed, still trading well |
| 2% | −18.3% | −26% | Frustrated, system intact |
| 5% | −40% | −54% | Revenge trading begins |
| 10% | −65% | −79% | Broken, or doubling down |
| 20% | −89% | −96% | Account effectively over |

(LTCM at ~25× was off this table entirely.)

**Recovery math is the trap:** lose 50% → need +100% to break even. Lose 90% → need
+900%. The 1–2% rule isn't about being cautious — it's about keeping **mathematical
access to your own edge**.

## Correlation: the hidden double risk

Two trades can be one trade in disguise:

- Long Nifty futures + long Bank Nifty futures = one big bet on Indian financials.
- Long a stock + long Nifty = mostly the same market risk.

**Rule:** count correlated positions as one trade for risk purposes. Max 1–2% total
across the cluster. Diversification only counts when positions can move independently.

## The daily loss limit (your circuit breaker)

Set a hard daily stop: **3 losses in a day, or −3% of account — whichever comes first —
then the terminal closes.** Not "trade smaller after" — *closed*. Why:

1. Loss clusters correlate with bad states (tilt, fatigue, bad market conditions).
2. Your edge is worth nothing executed by an impaired brain.
3. Every blown account has a day where the trader "just kept going." This rule makes
   that day impossible. Same rule, different scale, would have changed LTCM's ending —
   they were adding, not stopping.

Same rule after big wins: euphoria is also an impaired state. Protect the money from
yourself in both directions.

## The five standing risk rules

1. Max 1–2% risk per trade, always defined by the stop.
2. Max one correlated cluster — clusters share the 1–2%.
3. Max 3 trades per day for day traders; max 5–8 open swing positions.
4. Daily loss limit −3% or 3 consecutive losses → done for the day.
5. Position size is calculated, never eyeballed (tomorrow makes this mechanical).

> ⚠️ **Common beginner mistakes**
> 1. "I'm very confident in this one" → 10% risk. Confidence is not probability; the
>    market doesn't know your conviction — LTCM's Nobel committee was confident too.
> 2. Risking "per lot" instead of per trade — three lots at 2% each is 6% risk.
> 3. No daily limit → one bad morning becomes a catastrophic day. Every. Single. Time.

## 🇮🇳 The Indian story: ADAG's pledged spiral

Anil Ambani's Reliance ADA group was, in the late 2000s, among India's most valuable
businesses. Then came debt, and then **pledged shares** — promoters borrowing against
their holdings. When RCom defaulted and prices fell, lenders dumped the pledged stock
into falling markets: ADAG companies fell -30%, -50%, -90% in cascades through 2018–19,
and what was a ₹1,00,000-crore-plus empire shrank to near nothing in about five years.
That's LTCM's leverage lesson in Indian family-business form: concentrated exposure +
borrowed money + forced selling = a death spiral no analysis can stop. India's brokers
now offer a "kill switch" feature — you can self-ban your own trading account. It exists
because Indian brokers watched a million small LTCMs: real confidence, borrowed size, no
circuit breaker. Your 1–2% rule and daily limit are the same medicine, prescribed in
writing.

## Key takeaways
- 1–2% risk per trade makes losing streaks survivable — survival is the entire game.
  (LTCM: real edge + 25× leverage = Fed bailout.)
- Correlated positions count as one trade.
- Daily loss limit of −3% / 3 losses: non-negotiable circuit breaker.
