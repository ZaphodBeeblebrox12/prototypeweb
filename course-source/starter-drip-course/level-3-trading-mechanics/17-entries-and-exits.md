# Day 17 — Entries & Exits: Where the Money Is Actually Made

**🎬 Two traders, one rocket, opposite endings**

In January 2021, GameStop went from about $20 to an intraday high of **$483** in three
weeks. Consider two fictional-but-typical traders who both "saw it early":

**Trader A** — the breakout chaser. He watched from $20 to $40, finally bought the
"momentum breakout" at $120, added at $250 on the second leg, and held for the moon.
When the squeeze peaked at $483 and reversed, his stops — placed emotionally, based on
what he *needed* the stock to do — never triggered where he imagined. He exited most of
his position around $90, weeks later. He was "right" about the biggest story of the year
and lost money.

**Trader B** — the planned trader. She didn't predict the squeeze; she traded her system.
She bought the *first pullback to a defined level* after the initial breakout ($40 zone),
with a stop *below the structure* (under $25 — invalidation, not discomfort), and a
written plan: book half at +2R, trail the rest. She exited most near $300+ and the
runner on a trailing rule around $200.

Same stock. Same month. One had an entry style and no exit plan; the other had both.
The difference wasn't prediction — GameStop was fundamentally unpredictable. The
difference was that exits, not entries, decided who kept the money. That's today's
entire lesson.

> 💬 **The GameStop question:** two traders, same rocket of a stock, opposite endings. Before you read a single rule — what do YOU think separated them: better prediction, a better entry price, or better exits? Most people guess wrong, and *the wrong guess* is why most accounts lose. What's your call?

## What you'll learn
- The three entry styles and their tradeoffs
- Exit types: target, trailing, time
- Why "how you exit" matters more than "how you enter"

## The three entry styles

### 1. Anticipation entry (limit order at the level)
Place a limit at your zone *before* confirmation — betting the level holds.
- ✅ Best price if right; stop can sit at the ideal structural point.
- ❌ Catches falling knives when the level breaks; you get filled *because* price poked
  through (the stop-hunt fill).

### 2. Confirmation entry (wait for the rejection pattern)
Price reaches your zone; wait for a pin bar / engulfing (Day 11); enter on its break.
- ✅ Higher probability — someone showed you the defense.
- ❌ Worse price; fast V-reversals leave you behind.
- **Beginners should start here** — confirmation is training wheels for reading price action.

### 3. Breakout entry (buy the break of the level/pattern)
Enter when price closes beyond a boundary.
- ✅ Rides momentum; you know fast if you're wrong.
- ❌ Worst price of the three; fakeouts are the tax (Day 22).

> There's no "best" entry — there's the best entry *for your psychology*. A system you
> execute calmly beats a theoretically better one you abandon after two losses. (Trader A
> in our story used a chaotic mix of all three.)

## Exit types — where profits are actually made

| Exit type | How it works | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed target** | Exit fully at a predefined level (measured move, extension, prior high) | Ranges, clear patterns, when you need predictability |
| **R-multiple target** | Exit at +2R or +3R regardless of levels | Trend systems, backtested setups (Day 28) |
| **Partial + runner** | Book half at +2R, trail the rest | Trends with room — the best of both worlds (Trader B's plan) |
| **Trailing stop** | Exit only when price reverses X (structure break, MA cross, ATR trail) | Strong trends you don't want to cap |
| **Time exit** | "If it hasn't moved in N days, I'm out" | Everything — dead trades tie up capital and attention |

## The two target rules

1. **Know your exit before you enter.** Exactly — entry, stop, target(s) written down
   *before* the order. If you can't name your target, you don't have a trade.
2. **Let the structure pay you.** First target at the *next* obstacle (prior high, range
   edge, measured move). Don't demand 5R in a range that has never given 2R — the chart
   tells you what it pays; your job is to listen.

## A complete example (Day 16's trade, now with exits)

- **Entry:** inside-bar break at the daily zone, 24,100.
- **Stop:** below the zone low, 24,040 (risk = 60 points).
- **Target 1:** prior swing high, 24,220 (+2R) → book half.
- **Target 2 / trail:** 127.2% extension at 24,280, then trail below the 20 EMA.
- **Time exit:** if neither target hits within 5 sessions, exit at market.

> ⚠️ **Common beginner mistakes**
> 1. Moving the target *away* as price approaches ("it'll go higher") while pulling the
>    stop *closer* — trading hope, not plan. (That's precisely Trader A's GameStop story.)
> 2. No exit planned — "I'll watch it." Watching is anxiety management, not a strategy.
> 3. Exiting winners from fear and holding losers in hope — the disposition effect
>    (Day 25 deals with the psychology; today, know the exits must be mechanical).

## 🇮🇳 The Indian story: IRCTC's two traders

In 2021, IRCTC became India's GameStop — in slow motion. The stock ran from roughly
₹900 to about **₹6,300 in under a year** on the monopoly-plus-split story. Trader A
watched it cross ₹2,000, ₹3,000, finally chased the "breakout" near ₹5,000–6,000 with
no stop and no target — "government monopoly, it'll keep going." He rode it back down
through ₹4,000 in the correction and exited near ₹3,500, exhausted, having made nothing
on a 7-bagger. Trader B never chased: she waited for the pullback to the rising 20-week
average, bought the structure with a stop below it, booked half at +2R, trailed the rest.
Same stock. Same year. One bought a story at the emotional extreme; one bought a plan at
a discount. The exit rules decided who kept the money — exactly as in the GameStop story,
and exactly as SEBI's F&O data keeps confirming at national scale.

## Key takeaways
- Three entries: anticipation (best price), confirmation (best probability), breakout
  (best momentum). Pick one per setup and stay consistent.
- Exits make the money: fixed, R-multiple, partial+runner, trailing, time — use the
  chart's own obstacles for targets. (GameStop rewarded Trader B's written exits, not
  Trader A's conviction.)
- Entry, stop, and exit written down *before* the order — non-negotiable.
