# Day 23 — Building Your Trade Checklist

**🎬 The checklist that made flying the safest thing humans do**

In 1935, the US Army held a competition to choose its next long-range bomber. Boeing's
Model 299 — faster, longer range, more bombs than anything flying — was the certain
winner. On the demonstration flight, the experienced pilot forgot to *release the
elevator lock* — a single step — and the "flying fortress" crashed, killing two crew.
The army nearly cancelled the program.

Boeing's fix wasn't a better pilot — it was a **card with steps on it**. The pre-flight
checklist was born. That plane went on to become the B-17, and the checklist went on to
make aviation the safest complex activity in human history. Decades later, surgeon
Atul Gawande brought the same idea into operating rooms: in a study across eight
hospitals worldwide, a basic surgery checklist cut deaths and complications by roughly
a third.

Now consider your arena: one person, a screen, real money, and a brain wired by
evolution to panic in exactly the moments that matter. Pilots don't rely on memory
under pressure. Surgeons don't. **Why would you?** Today you build your card.

> 💬 **Count them:** a 20-year-experienced Boeing pilot crashed a plane over ONE forgotten step — so engineers made him a list. How many steps are in YOUR trading process right now, *written down*? If your answer is "in my head" — this lesson was written specifically for you. What's your number?

## The 10-point pre-trade checklist

Before ANY order, all ten boxes — in writing, every time:

```
□ 1. HTF BIAS     Daily trend clear? I'm trading WITH it, not against it?
□ 2. LEVEL        Entry sits at a real zone (S/R, trendline, Fib band, MA)? 3–5 zones max?
□ 3. CONFLUENCE   2+ independent tools agree here? (zone + MA, fib + level, etc.)
□ 4. TRIGGER      A defined trigger candle/pattern exists (or a limit order is placed per plan)?
□ 5. STOP         Stop is beyond structure (+ buffer), NOT inside noise? Distance in points known?
□ 6. SIZE         Size computed from formula (Day 20)? Reverse-checked: stop-loss ₹ ≤ budget?
□ 7. TARGET       Target(s) defined before entry? R:R ≥ 2 at minimum expectation?
□ 8. EXPECTANCY   Setup type has positive expectancy in my journal/backtest (Day 21/28)?
□ 9. DAILY LIMIT  Am I within today's 3-loss / −3% circuit breaker (Day 19)?
□ 10. ONE-LINER   Can I write this trade in one sentence? ("Long X at Y because Z,
                   stop A, target B, risking C%") — if not, NO TRADE.
```

> The checklist isn't bureaucracy — it's the elevator lock. It takes 60 seconds between
> impulse and order, and most bad trades cannot survive ten honest questions. The Boeing
> pilot had 20 years of experience; experience didn't release the lock.

## The post-trade review (2 minutes, every trade)

Right after exit, log:

- **R result** (+2R, −1R, 0R…)
- **Setup type** (pullback / breakout / pattern name) → feeds your expectancy data
- **Checklist followed?** Y/N — and if N, *which box did you skip?* (This single field is
  the highest-value data you'll ever collect. Skipped-box losses are not strategy losses;
  they're discipline losses — and they're fixable.)
- **One-line note:** what the market did, what you felt. One line.

## Turn it into a filter, not a form

After ~30 logged trades, sort your journal by **"Checklist followed?"**:

- Trades with all 10 boxes: your win rate and expectancy here = your real edge.
- Trades with skipped boxes: almost always negative expectancy.

The conclusion writes itself: **your edge isn't a secret setup — it's your own
checklist-following.** Protect the process and the P&L follows. (Same finding as the
operating rooms: the checklist didn't make surgeons smarter — it made their good days
repeatable.)

## Your personalized checklist (homework for today)

Copy the 10 points, then:
1. **Add 2 custom boxes** for YOUR style (e.g., "□ Not within 15 min of a major news
   release?" or "□ Is this instrument in my 5-instrument watchlist?").
2. **Delete anything you never use.** A checklist that's 60% ignored trains you to
   ignore checklists.
3. Print it. Tape it to your monitor. (Pilots keep theirs in the cockpit, not in memory.)

📊 No chart needed today — the checklist replaces charts.

> ⚠️ **Common beginner mistakes**
> 1. Checklist in the head. A mental checklist is a memory of a checklist. Write it.
> 2. Filling boxes backwards — fitting answers to justify a trade you already want. The
>    checklist's job is to *kill* trades; if it never kills any, you're not using it.
> 3. Reviewing only losers. Winners skipped boxes too — those are your future disasters
>    rehearsing. (Boeing's crashed plane passed every check except one.)

## 🇮🇳 The Indian story: the dabbawala code

Mumbai's dabbawalas deliver roughly **two lakh tiffin boxes a day** across the city's
suburban rail network — through crowds, monsoons, and train delays — with accuracy rates
that have fascinated operations researchers worldwide. Their secret isn't technology;
most of the workforce is semi-literate. It's **color-coded marks and a written system
everyone follows**: each dabba's origin, destination, and route encoded in symbols, with
strict handoff rules at every station. No dabbawala "improvises" because he feels
confident about a shortcut. A system simple enough to follow in a monsoon, followed
without exception — that's the entire argument for your checklist. When you feel like
skipping box #6 on a "perfect setup," remember: nobody in Mumbai skips their code
because lunch feels like a sure thing.

## Key takeaways
- 10 boxes, in writing, every trade. The one-liner test is the final gate. (The B-17's
  entire history hinged on one forgotten step.)
- Post-trade: R result, setup type, checklist-followed, one line.
- After 30 trades, compare followed vs skipped — that gap is your leak and your roadmap.
