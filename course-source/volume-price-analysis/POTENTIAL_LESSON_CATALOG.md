# Read Volume. Find Trade Setups.
## Potential Lesson Catalog — Volume Price Analysis

> **Status:** Proposed course catalog for review. This is a standalone planning document; it has not been integrated into the public website, course pages, or existing curriculum.
>
> **Positioning:** A practical course in volume price analysis.
>
> **Course promise:** Learn to read the activity behind price movements, recognise potential trading opportunities, evaluate breakouts, and build a structured trade plan using volume and price action.

---

## Module 1 — Read the Market Behind the Candles

*Learn what volume reveals that candlesticks alone cannot, so you can recognise which price moves deserve your attention and which may be ordinary market noise.*

### Lessons

1. **What Candlesticks Don't Tell You: Reading the Volume Behind Price**
2. **Is This Move Significant? Spotting Unusual Volume**
3. **Read the Story Inside a Candle: Wicks, Range and Closing Price**
4. **When Price Moves on Low Volume: Opportunity or Warning Sign?**
5. **Which Volume Can You Trust? Understanding Data Across Different Markets**

## Module 6 — Put It All Together: Build a Complete Trading Plan

*Combine everything from the course into one structured process for analysing a chart, evaluating a potential setup, planning a trade and defining what would prove your analysis wrong.*

### Lessons

1. **Start With the Bigger Picture: What Is the Market Doing?**  
   Bring together trend, trading ranges, price structure and important levels to understand the context in which a potential trade might develop.

2. **Read the Evidence: What Are Price and Volume Telling You?**  
   Apply effort versus result, buying and selling pressure, breakout behaviour and volume-at-price concepts to assess what is happening at the levels that matter.

3. **Build Your Trade Scenario: What Needs to Happen Next?**  
   Turn your analysis into a conditional thesis. Define what could create a buying or selling opportunity, what confirmation you need and what alternative outcome you must consider.

4. **Plan the Trade Before You Take It**  
   Define your entry conditions, invalidation point, potential target, position risk and trade-management rules before committing capital.

5. **The Complete Walkthrough: From Chart Analysis to Trade Plan**  
   Work through real chart examples from beginning to end. Combine market context, volume evidence, setup selection, confirmation and risk into a written trading plan — then review what happened afterward.

## Intended Learning Outcomes

By completing the course, learners should be better equipped to:

- Interpret volume in the context of price rather than treating it as a standalone signal.
- Assess potential buying and selling pressure and recognise when price is struggling to make progress.
- Identify and evaluate potential setups around trends, pullbacks and important price levels.
- Examine breakouts and failed moves without assuming every apparent signal will succeed.
- Use volume profile and related tools to add context to chart analysis.
- Build a complete trade plan with a thesis, entry conditions, invalidation, risk management and post-trade review.

**Course goal:** Move from simply looking at volume bars to using price and volume evidence in a structured, repeatable approach to trade analysis.

> **Risk note:** Trading involves risk. Volume analysis can support decision-making, but no pattern or method guarantees a profitable outcome.

## Editorial and Research Notes

This is a proposed curriculum synthesising concepts found across established approaches to volume and market analysis. It is not a transcription of any single book. Reference works to consult during detailed lesson development include:

- Anna Coulling — *A Complete Guide to Volume Price Analysis*
- Tom Williams — *Master the Markets*
- Richard D. Wyckoff — *The Richard D. Wyckoff Course in Stock Market Science and Technique*
- David H. Weis — *Trades About to Happen*
- James F. Dalton, Eric T. Jones and Robert B. Dalton — *Mind Over Markets*
- Buff Dormeier — *Investing with Volume Analysis*

Before lesson scripts are produced, each lesson should receive a depth pass covering learning objectives, accurate explanations, worked chart examples, contrasting cases, practical exercises and mastery criteria. Claims about buying or selling pressure, absorption, or likely price behaviour should be presented as interpretations of evidence—not as certainty about participant identity or future outcomes.
