Plan the Trade Before You Take It

The setup may be plausible while the actual trade is poorly constructed. The chart should show how the trigger, invalidation and objective relate to price.

How to use this lab: The charts below show price candles and recorded volume. Complete each step before moving on. The response boxes check that you have recorded an answer; they do not automatically judge the quality of your reasoning. Every price sequence is synthetic and for learning only.

Level 1 — Notice (easy)

Start by describing what the chart shows. Do not predict the next move yet. Look at the candle sequence, the reference zone and the volume bars.

Your task

Identify the setup area, trigger, invalidation/stop and objective lines. State the role of each rather than treating them as interchangeable.

Model explanation

The area is where attention is focused; the trigger is the required event; invalidation challenges the thesis; stop logic controls planned risk; the objective is a conditional exit reference. The chart does not make the plan suitable by itself.

Level 3 — Apply (independent)

The later bars are hidden. Work from the initial event shown here and write two plausible paths before revealing what happened next in the two possible outcomes.

Your task

Use the hypothetical entry 54.5 and stop 51.5. If each price unit is worth 2 currency units, calculate price risk per unit before costs/slippage, then state why that does not automatically approve the trade.

Two possible outcomes

The initial chart was identical in both paths. Compare the additional evidence, then open the model explanation.

Path A — Continuation / supported hypothesis

Later bars retain a different price path. Review whether this supports the relevant conditional scenario.

Path B — Failure / alternative hypothesis

Later bars take the contrasting path. Note which observations changed the balance of explanations.

Reasoning feedback

The distance is 3.0 price units; multiplied by 2 currency units per price unit, the estimate is 6 currency units per unit before costs and execution effects. A correct calculation does not prove the trigger, target or personal risk rules are suitable.

Remember: a matching outcome does not prove the initial reasoning was sound; evaluate whether the observations and conditions were defensible before the later bars appeared.
