# Read Volume. Find Trade Setups.
## Learner Q&A Bank — Third Pass, Plain-Language Edition

Use these questions for lesson-end checks, revision, and ChartLab discussions. Answers are written for people who have only recently started looking at charts. They are learning explanations, not automatic trading rules.

**Assessment rule:** Give credit for clear reasoning based on the chart, not for guessing the direction price later took.

---

# Module 1 — Read the Market Behind the Candles

## Lesson 1.1 — What Candlesticks Don't Tell You: Reading the Volume Behind Price
**Q1. If two candles look the same, why might they mean different things?**  
**A.** They may appear in different places on the chart and have different levels of activity behind them. Check what price was doing before the candle, compare volume with nearby bars, and see what price does next.

**Q2. Does high volume prove that buyers or sellers are in control?**  
**A.** No. It tells you that the data source recorded a lot of activity. To understand the move, also look at how far price moved, where it closed, and what happened afterward.

## Lesson 1.2 — Is This Move Significant? Spotting Unusual Volume
**Q1. Why compare a volume bar with recent bars?**  
**A.** A raw number has little meaning by itself. Comparing it with similar recent bars helps you see whether activity is unusually high or low for that market and time of day.

**Q2. Should you trade just because volume suddenly rises?**  
**A.** No. A spike is a reason to look closer, not a trade instruction. Check where it happened and whether price made progress or quickly gave the move back.

## Lesson 1.3 — Read the Story Inside a Candle: Wicks, Range and Closing Price
**Q1. What does a long upper wick tell you?**  
**A.** Price traded higher during that candle but finished well below its high. This may show that higher prices did not hold during that period, but it does not predict a fall by itself.

**Q2. Does a wide candle always mean a strong move?**  
**A.** No. A wide candle tells you price travelled a long distance. Check where it closed, how much activity took place, and whether the next candles continued.

## Lesson 1.4 — When Price Moves on Low Volume: Opportunity or Warning Sign?
**Q1. Can price rise on low volume and still keep rising?**  
**A.** Yes. Price may move through an area where there is little opposition, or the market may simply be quiet. Low volume alone does not tell you what will happen next.

**Q2. What helps you tell a quiet pullback from a failing trend?**  
**A.** Check whether price holds an important area and whether the move down keeps making progress. If price breaks the level and cannot recover it, the original uptrend idea is weaker.

## Lesson 1.5 — Which Volume Can You Trust? Understanding Data Across Different Markets
**Q1. Why is tick volume on a retail forex chart not the same as all forex trading volume?**  
**A.** Forex trading is spread across many banks, dealers and venues. A retail chart often counts price updates from one data source, not every trade taking place around the world.

**Q2. What should you write down about the volume on your chart?**  
**A.** Record the market, the data source, what its volume number measures, the timeframe, and which bars you compare. If you do not know what the number measures, say so.

# Module 2 — Spot Buying and Selling Pressure

## Lesson 2.1 — Who's Pushing Price? Reading Buying and Selling Pressure
**Q1. What does “buying pressure” mean in this course?**  
**A.** It is a simple way to describe a situation where price is making upward progress. It does not prove who placed the orders or why they traded.

**Q2. Why look at several candles instead of just one?**  
**A.** One candle can be unclear. Several candles help you see whether price keeps moving in the same direction, stalls, or starts to change its pattern.

## Lesson 2.2 — When Volume Supports a Move: Recognising Evidence of Strength
**Q1. What makes a breakout more convincing than a quick move above resistance?**  
**A.** Price should move beyond a clearly marked area and show that it can stay there. Activity adds context, but what price does after the first move matters too.

**Q2. Must a real breakout have very high volume?**  
**A.** No. Price can sometimes move through an area on ordinary activity. Do not use one volume rule for every market; study the price move and what happens next.

## Lesson 2.3 — Heavy Volume, Little Progress: What Is Happening Behind the Move?
**Q1. What should you notice when volume is high but price barely moves?**  
**A.** A lot of activity happened, but price made little progress. Buyers and sellers may be meeting each other, or the market may be reacting to an event. The chart alone cannot prove which explanation is right.

**Q2. What would help you decide whether price is turning away from resistance?**  
**A.** Look for price to return below the area, fail to move back above it, and then make progress lower. A later move above the area that holds would weaken the rejection idea.

## Lesson 2.4 — Is the Move Losing Steam? Reading Signs of Weakening Pressure
**Q1. Does a move that is slowing down have to reverse?**  
**A.** No. It may pause before continuing. A slowdown is a reason to pay attention, not proof that the other direction will take over.

**Q2. What is stronger evidence of a reversal than one weak candle?**  
**A.** A clear change in the pattern of highs and lows, followed by price failing to return to the old pattern, gives you more evidence than one candle alone.

## Lesson 2.5 — When Price and Volume Disagree: Finding Clues Others May Overlook
**Q1. Price makes a new high, but volume is lower. What can you conclude?**  
**A.** You can say price made a new high while the data source recorded less activity than before. The move may be losing strength, or price may simply be moving easily. Watch whether it holds the new high.

**Q2. What should you do when price and volume seem to disagree?**  
**A.** Describe the mismatch, write down two possible reasons, and decide what price would need to do next to help separate them. Do not treat the mismatch as an automatic signal.

# Module 3 — Find Trade Setups at the Levels That Matter

## Lesson 3.1 — Stop Chasing Price: Finding Potential Setups in an Established Trend
**Q1. What is the difference between a setup area and an entry trigger?**  
**A.** The setup area is a place worth watching. The entry trigger is the exact price behaviour you want to see before you consider a trade. Reaching an area does not automatically mean you should enter.

**Q2. Why can buying a strong-looking candle be risky?**  
**A.** Price may already have moved far from a sensible exit point if the idea fails. The next price barrier may also be close, leaving little room for the trade to work.

## Lesson 3.2 — Is the Pullback Running Out of Steam? Reading Countertrend Moves
**Q1. What should you compare when studying a pullback?**  
**A.** Compare how far and how quickly price moved during the pullback with the move that came before it. Also check candle size, volume, and whether important highs or lows have been broken.

**Q2. Does low volume on a pullback guarantee that the trend will continue?**  
**A.** No. It may be a quiet pause, but price can still keep moving against the trend. Check whether the important level holds and whether price shows the behaviour your plan requires.

## Lesson 3.3 — Trading Near Support and Resistance: What to Look for Before Entry
**Q1. Why mark a support or resistance area instead of one exact price?**  
**A.** Price often turns within an area rather than at one perfect number. Mark the zone where price previously stopped or changed direction, then watch how it behaves when it returns.

**Q2. What are two possible things price can do at resistance?**  
**A.** It may turn away, or it may move above the area and stay there. Watch the candles and the next few bars before deciding which possibility has more support.

## Lesson 3.4 — Finding Opportunities at the Edges of a Trading Range
**Q1. What is a trading range?**  
**A.** It is a period when price moves back and forth between an upper area and a lower area without lasting progress beyond them.

**Q2. Why can a quick move outside the range be misleading?**  
**A.** Price may return inside and fail to move outside again. A move beyond the boundary is only the start of the story; see whether price can stay there.

## Lesson 3.5 — Before You Enter: Separating Promising Setups From Weak Ones
**Q1. What should you check before treating a chart pattern as a trade setup?**  
**A.** Check the bigger price pattern, the area, the volume evidence, the entry trigger, the point where the idea fails, and whether the possible reward justifies the risk.

**Q2. Is “no trade” a valid decision?**  
**A.** Yes. If the area is unclear, the trigger has not appeared, or the risk does not make sense, waiting is part of a disciplined process.

# Module 4 — Spot False Breakouts Before You Chase Them

## Lesson 4.1 — Is the Breakout Real? What Volume Can Tell You
**Q1. What should you look for after price moves beyond a range?**  
**A.** See whether price stays outside the range and continues to make progress, or quickly returns inside. The first candle alone may not tell you which will happen.

**Q2. Does high volume prove that a breakout will succeed?**  
**A.** No. High volume can appear during a successful move or a failed one. Check where the candle closes and what happens after the first break.

## Lesson 4.2 — Breakout or Trap? Reading the Signs of a Failed Move
**Q1. What makes an upside breakout look as if it has failed?**  
**A.** Price moves above the upper boundary, returns inside the range, and cannot stay above the boundary on another attempt.

**Q2. Does a failed breakout prove someone deliberately trapped traders?**  
**A.** No. The chart shows that the move did not hold; it cannot tell you someone planned it. Describe the price behaviour rather than guessing at hidden motives.

## Lesson 4.3 — When Volume Explodes at Market Extremes: Understanding Climax Activity
**Q1. Why pay attention to a huge volume spike after a long move?**  
**A.** It shows that unusual activity happened after price had already travelled a long way. That may be worth investigating, especially if price then struggles to continue.

**Q2. Does a climax bar mean the market must reverse?**  
**A.** No. Price may reverse, pause, or continue in the same direction. Look for a change in the pattern of highs and lows before treating it as a reversal.

## Lesson 4.4 — Springs and Upthrusts: Recognising Failed Moves at Range Boundaries
**Q1. What is a spring in simple terms?**  
**A.** Price moves below the bottom of a trading range and then comes back inside. The key question is whether price can stay inside or falls below the boundary again.

**Q2. What is an upthrust?**  
**A.** Price briefly moves above the top of a range and then returns inside. The name describes the move; it does not promise a reversal.

## Lesson 4.5 — Before Calling a Reversal: What to Look for After the Breakout
**Q1. What is the difference between an early warning and stronger evidence of a reversal?**  
**A.** An early warning may be that price stops making progress. Stronger evidence appears when price breaks an important high or low in the other direction and cannot quickly return to the old pattern.

**Q2. Why decide in advance where your reversal idea is wrong?**  
**A.** It helps you avoid changing the rules after price disagrees with you. If the price condition you chose occurs, you know the idea no longer fits your plan.

# Module 5 — Find the Price Levels Worth Watching

## Lesson 5.1 — Where Has the Market Traded Most? Introducing Volume Profile
**Q1. What does Volume Profile show?**  
**A.** It shows how recorded trading activity was spread across different prices during a time period you selected.

**Q2. Does a wide part of the profile guarantee price will turn there?**  
**A.** No. It marks an area where more activity took place in the past. Price may pause there, turn away, or move straight through it.

## Lesson 5.2 — Finding the Market's Reference Point: Point of Control and Value Area
**Q1. What is the Point of Control?**  
**A.** It is the price row in the chosen profile with the most recorded activity.

**Q2. Why can the Point of Control change when you change the profile period?**  
**A.** A different time period includes different price activity, so the largest concentration may appear at a different price.

## Lesson 5.3 — Where Price May Stall — or Move Quickly: High- and Low-Volume Nodes
**Q1. What does a high-volume area tell you?**  
**A.** It shows where more activity was recorded during the selected period. It is a place worth watching, not a guaranteed support or resistance level.

**Q2. Why might price travel quickly through a low-volume area?**  
**A.** Less activity was recorded there during the selected period, so price may move through it quickly again. But it may also slow down or turn; watch what happens now.

## Lesson 5.4 — When the Market's Focus Shifts: Reading Changes in Value
**Q1. What does it mean if the main area of profile activity moves higher over several sessions?**  
**A.** More recorded activity is taking place at higher prices in those sessions. This may fit a move toward higher prices, but price behaviour must show whether the shift lasts.

**Q2. Why compare similar time periods?**  
**A.** If one profile covers a few hours and another covers a full day, the difference may come from the time included. Comparing similar periods makes the change easier to understand.

## Lesson 5.5 — Putting It Together: Reading Volume Profile Alongside Price Action
**Q1. How should you use Volume Profile with candles?**  
**A.** Use the profile to mark where activity happened in the past, then use the candles and recent highs/lows to see how price behaves when it returns to those areas.

**Q2. What should you do if price moves through a profile area you expected to hold?**  
**A.** Update your reading. The profile describes past activity; it does not force price to turn there. Decide what the new price behaviour means for your plan.

# Module 6 — Put It All Together: Build a Complete Trading Plan

## Lesson 6.1 — Start With the Bigger Picture: What Is the Market Doing?
**Q1. What should you identify before looking for a trade?**  
**A.** Decide whether price is moving in a trend, moving back and forth in a range, or changing between the two. Mark only a few price areas that have a clear reason for being watched.

**Q2. Why record the source of your volume data?**  
**A.** The meaning of the volume number depends on the market and data source. Knowing what is measured helps you avoid making stronger claims than the data supports.

## Lesson 6.2 — Read the Evidence: What Are Price and Volume Telling You?
**Q1. Why separate what you see from what you think it means?**  
**A.** It stops you from treating a guess as a fact. Record the candle and volume first, then explain what they may mean and what else could explain them.

**Q2. What should you do when the evidence is mixed?**  
**A.** Write down what is clear and what is not. You can wait for another price move instead of forcing a decision.

## Lesson 6.3 — Build Your Trade Scenario: What Needs to Happen Next?
**Q1. What is an if/then trade idea?**  
**A.** It says what you see now, what price must do before you consider entering, and what would show that your idea has failed.

**Q2. Why write down another possible scenario?**  
**A.** It prepares you for a different outcome. If your first idea fails, the opposite trade is not automatically ready; it needs its own evidence and conditions.

## Lesson 6.4 — Plan the Trade Before You Take It
**Q1. Why choose the point where the idea fails before deciding how much to trade?**  
**A.** The distance between entry and that point helps determine how much you could lose if the idea is wrong. Position size must fit the risk limit you chose in advance.

**Q2. Can a good chart idea still be a bad trade?**  
**A.** Yes. The entry may be too far from the exit point, the next price barrier may be too close, or trading costs may make the opportunity unattractive.

## Lesson 6.5 — The Complete Walkthrough: From Chart Analysis to Trade Plan
**Q1. What are the main steps in a complete volume-based trade plan?**  
**A.** Understand the price pattern, record the price-and-volume evidence, write two possible scenarios, define an entry condition, choose where the idea fails, plan risk, and decide how you will review the result.

**Q2. If your plan says “wait,” but price later moves in the direction you expected, was the plan wrong?**  
**A.** Not necessarily. If the entry condition never appeared, waiting followed the plan. Review whether the rules were clear and sensible, not only whether price later moved the way you expected.

## Course-wide questions

**Q1. Can volume tell me exactly who is buying or selling?**  
**A.** No. Volume tells you about activity recorded by your data source. Price helps you interpret the result, but the chart does not reveal every trader's identity or reason.

**Q2. Is high volume always bullish and low volume always bearish?**  
**A.** No. Always ask what price did while that activity took place and where it happened on the chart.

**Q3. Does this course teach guaranteed trading signals?**  
**A.** No. It teaches you to read price and volume evidence, consider possible explanations, and plan what to do if the market behaves differently than expected.

**Q4. Is skipping a trade a mistake?**  
**A.** No. If your conditions have not appeared or the risk does not make sense, skipping the trade can be the right decision.

**Q5. Should I copy one example exactly on a live chart?**  
**A.** No. Use examples to learn the reasoning. Markets differ, and each trade still needs its own context, conditions and risk plan.

**Q6. How do I know whether I have understood a lesson?**  
**A.** Explain the main idea in your own words, point to the evidence on a new chart, give one other possible explanation, and say what you would watch next.
