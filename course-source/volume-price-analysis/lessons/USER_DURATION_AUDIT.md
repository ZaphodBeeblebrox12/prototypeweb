# User-Perceived Lesson Duration Audit
## Read Volume. Find Trade Setups.

**Branch:** `my-new-branch`  
**Audit date:** 16 October 2026  
**Scope:** All 30 source lessons across six modules, plus the 30 chart-walkthrough notes.  
**Status: NOT VERIFIED. Do not treat the outline's 15-minute target as a measured runtime.**

## Executive finding

The existing manuscripts do not, by themselves, establish that every lesson lasts more than 10 minutes for a learner. The course outlines allocate 15 minutes to each lesson, but those allocations are targets. A user-perceived duration must include spoken instruction, chart viewing, meaningful learner work, outcome reveals, feedback and recap, and it must be confirmed by timing a full run-through.

The narration and chart-walkthrough word counts are below. All estimated speaking times are calculations—not recorded or rehearsed timings. **No lesson is marked verified.** Lesson 1.1 has a standalone ChartLab prototype with an estimated 5–7 minutes of learner work, but it has not had a full end-to-end timed rehearsal and is not integrated into the public course. The remaining 29 ChartLabs are specifications, not finished learner-facing interactions.

## Method

- **Narration words** counts the main narration section plus any “Extended spoken case analysis — additional narration”.
- **Walkthrough words** counts the separate presenter notes in `CHART_WALKTHROUGHS.md`. That file describes these as supplemental segments. This audit conservatively treats them as additional spoken content only where they are actually delivered, rather than assuming they are already in a recording.
- **140 words/minute** is the faster of the two illustrative narration paces used here; it is a useful stress test because faster delivery shortens the lesson. **125 words/minute** shows a slower reading estimate. Actual delivery varies with pauses, explanations and chart demonstrations.
- **Activity budget to exceed 10 minutes** is the additional *active learner time* needed after all counted spoken content at 140 words/minute, rounded up to the next half-minute. This is a planning threshold, not proof that an activity currently exists or takes that long.
- **Activity budget for a 12-minute lesson** is the additional learner time needed to plan for 12 minutes at 140 words/minute, likewise rounded up to half-minute increments. Twelve minutes gives the ten-minute minimum a buffer.
- Activity budgets do not count empty waiting, page loading or deliberately slowed narration. Valid learner work must involve observing, comparing, writing/annotating, making a conditional interpretation, reviewing feedback or completing a meaningful knowledge check.

## Interpretation

- **All 30 main narration scripts are under 10 minutes when spoken on their own** at both illustrative paces.
- When the supplemental chart-walkthrough notes are also spoken, only the first lesson clears 10 minutes at 125 words/minute; none clears 10 minutes at 140 words/minute.
- Across all counted narration plus chart-walkthrough words, the median estimate at 140 words/minute is **7.2 minutes**, before counting learner work.
- Therefore, the course can reach the desired learner time through its planned practical exercises, but only if the exercises are implemented and timed. The current 15-minute outlines are not enough evidence to claim duration.
- The first lesson's existing ChartLab is described as taking 5–7 minutes inside the full lesson. That makes the planned length plausible, but it still needs a real user run-through. The other lessons cannot be verified on that basis because their full interactive practice is not yet built.

## Acceptance rule before marking a lesson “over 10 minutes”

For each of the 30 lessons:

1. Deliver the narration and supplemental chart walkthrough at a natural, beginner-friendly pace. Avoid rushing to meet a time target.
2. Complete all three learner stages: **Notice → Compare → Apply**. Time actual observation, comparison, independent response and any staged outcome reveal.
3. Include feedback/model reasoning and the knowledge check. The learner should have time to read, think and answer; do not count merely displaying the answer on screen as equivalent to a completed activity.
4. Run the full lesson start-to-finish and record the actual elapsed time. Exclude loading time and unplanned technical delays; include purposeful learner work.
5. Run a novice test and note whether instructions, charts and responses were understandable without instructor intervention.

**Approval threshold:** measured learner experience must be **more than 10:00 minutes**, not merely a 10:00 target on an outline. Recommended production target is **12–16 minutes**. If a rehearsal falls short, add a meaningful worked example, contrasting outcome, or transfer task—not repeated narration or filler.
