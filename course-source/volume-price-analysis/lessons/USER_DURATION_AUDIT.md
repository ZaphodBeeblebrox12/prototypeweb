## Duration standard

Keep the established target:
- **Normal target:** 12–16 minutes per lesson.
- **Hard minimum:** more than 10 minutes of meaningful learning time.
- **Ordinary upper range:** 20 minutes, unless the concept genuinely warrants more.

For the rewritten format, measure the complete learning experience: reading the explanation, inspecting the chart, completing the task, considering feedback and doing the knowledge check. Do not count page loading, idle pauses or time spent confused by a defective interface. Reading-time estimates alone are not sufficient.

## How to measure

For each lesson, record:
1. The rendered lesson version and device/screen size.
2. Time spent reading the instructional content.
3. Time spent on chart inspection and annotation.
4. Time spent answering the practice tasks.
5. Time spent reviewing feedback and the answer explanation.
6. Completion time, where the learner hesitated or became confused, and whether help was required.
7. Whether the learner met the beginner checkpoint using their own words and pointing to visible chart evidence.

Use more than one beginner when practical, and revise any task that depends on unstated assumptions. Do not use time-on-page alone as evidence of learning; pair duration with task quality and comprehension.

## Release gate

Do not mark a lesson “duration verified” until the final or representative rendered experience has been completed in a timed run-through. Do not approve the course for scaling until the pilot establishes that learners understand the instructions, can read the chart labels at normal and zoomed sizes, can complete the exercise without coaching, and receive useful feedback.

If a lesson is too short, add a missing worked example, a meaningful counterexample, or learner reasoning practice. Do not reintroduce narration paragraphs merely to increase word count.
