# Research and Adaptation Notes
## Volume Price Analysis — first-pass manuscript

This manuscript synthesises established ideas from volume analysis, Volume Spread Analysis, Wyckoff-style price/volume reading, market-generated information, and volume-based tools. It is not a transcription, abridgement, or substitute for any source book. All lesson wording and course exercises should remain original.

## Reference works and their contribution

### Anna Coulling — *A Complete Guide to Volume Price Analysis*
Useful for the course's central habit of comparing price behaviour with volume and for organising observations into a coherent reading process. Adapted here as: describe the price result, compare activity with a baseline, locate the event in structure, then assess follow-through.
- Author page: https://www.annacoulling.com/a-complete-guide-to-volume-price-analysis-by-anna-coulling/

### Tom Williams — *Master the Markets*
A key reference for the broad Volume Spread Analysis approach of relating volume, price spread/range and closing position. Adapted here as questions and comparative examples, rather than a copied dictionary of named bars or claims that one bar determines direction.
- Reference/search for bibliographic verification before final production: https://www.google.com/search?q=Tom+Williams+Master+the+Markets+official

### Richard D. Wyckoff — *Course in Stock Market Science and Technique*
The course uses broad principles associated with Wyckoff: supply and demand, market context, trading-range behaviour, tests and the study of turning points. A named formation is presented as a price event to evaluate, not as proof of hidden intent.
- Wyckoff Stock Market Institute: https://wyckoffstockmarketinstitute.com/course/

### David H. Weis — *Trades About to Happen: A Modern Adaptation of the Wyckoff Method*
Useful for close chart-by-chart study, support/resistance, the ambiguity of real-time analysis, and dedicated treatments of springs, upthrusts and absorption. Adapted here into staged chart reveals and competing-hypothesis exercises.
- Wiley book page and contents: https://www.wiley-vch.de/en/areas-interest/finance-economics-law/trades-about-to-happen-978-0-470-48780-8

### James F. Dalton, Eric T. Jones and Robert B. Dalton — *Mind Over Markets*
Contributes the auction and market-generated-information perspective: balance versus trend, initiative versus responsive activity, and the need to interpret profile structure in context. Module 5 explicitly distinguishes profile history from current resting liquidity.
- Publisher/library contents: https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/mind-over-markets/9781118659762/toc.html

### Buff Dormeier — *Investing with Volume Analysis*
Useful as a broader complement for volume analysis, price/volume relationships, trends, patterns and volume-based indicators. Its role here is to support supplementary context, not turn the course into an indicator catalogue.
- Contents: https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/investing-with-volume/9780132613330/toc.html
- Author: https://buffdormeier.com/investing-with-volume-analysis/

## Boundaries and cautions

- The cited works represent different methods and perspectives; the manuscript does not imply that their authors agree on every interpretation.
- General concepts are synthesised; do not reproduce the books' original prose, diagrams, chart annotations or proprietary course materials.
- Use original synthetic charts for early teaching examples unless licensed real examples are available.
- No single volume event proves who traded, why they traded, or what price will do next.
- Retail spot-FX tick volume is provider-specific activity data, not a consolidated count of all global spot-FX transactions.
- Crypto volume may be venue-specific or aggregated according to provider rules; state the source and method.
- Volume Profile and VWAP must be explained as tools calculated over defined data and periods, not guaranteed support/resistance or prediction tools.
- Names such as accumulation, distribution, spring, upthrust, climax and absorption must be carefully defined and taught as analytical concepts/hypotheses—not statements of hidden participant intent.
- Risk, position sizing, execution costs, instrument specifications and local product rules require separate verification before any live-trading exercise.
- The material is educational and does not constitute individual investment advice or promise profitable results.
