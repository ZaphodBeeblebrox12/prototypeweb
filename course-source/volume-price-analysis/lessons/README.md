## What changed in the module manuscripts

Each lesson now teaches the student directly. Depending on the topic, it includes a practical question, a plain-language explanation, a chart-based example, step-by-step reasoning, an independent task, feedback, a common mistake and a concise takeaway. The structure is flexible rather than a rigid template. Internal asset prompts and presenter directions are not part of the student lesson.

The lessons preserve the course's analytical rules: observation before interpretation, alternatives where evidence is ambiguous, explicit data-source limitations, and no claims that patterns guarantee outcomes or reveal hidden participant motives.

## Duration and validation

The intended delivery target remains 12–16 minutes for a typical lesson, with a hard minimum of 10 minutes and an ordinary upper range of 20 minutes. The new format includes reading, inspecting charts, completing tasks and reviewing feedback, so old narration word counts no longer describe the lesson experience. **No rewritten lesson is marked duration-verified.** Time the rendered lesson with a real learner before release; do not pad short lessons with repeated explanation.

## Production sequence

1. Review the revised student-first format and spot any substantive teaching gaps.
2. Build or revise each lesson's visual package and complete ChartLab, including clear feedback and counterexamples.
3. Test the existing M01-L01 pilot and use it as a benchmark only after browser, accessibility and true-beginner checks pass.
4. Rehearse the actual learner experience and measure time-on-task, comprehension and usability.
5. Complete a course-wide editorial, data-source, licensing and accessibility review.
6. Integrate the approved material into the member learning experience only after the teaching content and assets have been reviewed.

The course source, website integration and publication are distinct stages. A rewritten manuscript does not imply that a public course has been launched or approved for release.
