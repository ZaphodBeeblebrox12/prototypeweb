# CONSTRUCT — Technical Analysis and Applied Setups Module Outline

**Working document · Feature branch:** `construct-technical-analysis-modules`  
**Purpose:** Keep the curriculum practical and light enough to produce and bring to market soon. This is a curriculum outline, not a claim that lesson manuscripts or the live website have been updated.

## Module 1 — Price Action and Chart Reading

A concise foundation in reading price before relying on indicators.

1. **Reading a Price Chart** — chart types, OHLC, candlesticks, timeframes, and chart navigation.
2. **Candlesticks and Price Behaviour** — bodies, wicks, rejection, momentum, and context.
3. **Market Structure and Swings** — swing highs/lows, higher highs/lows, lower highs/lows, and possible structure changes.
4. **Market Conditions** — trending, ranging, and transitioning markets.
5. **Support, Resistance, Trendlines and Channels** — useful levels, zones, role reversal, and avoiding overfitted lines.
6. **Common Price Patterns and Breakouts** — selected candlestick/chart patterns, pullbacks, breakouts, retests, and failed moves.
7. **Volume and Multiple-Timeframe Context** — use volume where meaningful for the instrument, and align higher-timeframe context with a lower-timeframe idea.

**Practice outcome:** Learners can describe what price is doing, identify relevant structure and levels, and explain uncertainty without needing an indicator to make the decision for them.

## Module 2 — Essential Indicators and Technical Tools

Keep this module deliberately light. Reuse Module 1 concepts briefly where they help explain a tool; do not repeat the full price-action lessons.

1. **Moving Averages** — SMA/EMA, trend context, dynamic reference areas, and crossovers.
2. **Bollinger Bands** — middle/outer bands, bandwidth, squeeze and expansion, trend versus mean-reversion context, and common misreadings.
3. **RSI and MACD** — momentum, divergence, crossovers, and why readings are not automatic buy/sell signals.
4. **ATR and Volatility** — measuring movement, contextual stop distance, and implications for position sizing.
5. **Fibonacci Retracements and Extensions** — selecting meaningful swings, retracement/extension levels, confluence, and avoiding false precision.
6. **Pivot Points and Key Levels** — common calculations and how they relate to price structure.
7. **Putting Tools in Context** — a short guided example of comparing chart structure with one or two relevant tools; no separate indicator chart-lab lesson.

**Explicitly excluded from Module 2:** ADX/Directional Movement; VWAP and volume-based tools as standalone lessons; a separate Indicator Chart Lab. Fibonacci and Bollinger Bands remain included.

**Practice outcome:** Learners understand what the selected tools measure, what they cannot establish, and how to judge whether a tool adds useful information to a price-based thesis.

## Module 3 — Practical Trade Setups

This module turns chart-reading skills into repeatable, testable setups. Keep it setup-led and chart-first; do not introduce another indicator survey.

1. **The Anatomy of a Trade Setup** — context, location, setup conditions, trigger, invalidation, and reasons to pass.
2. **Trend Pullbacks and Continuation** — assess whether the trend remains intact, define a trigger, and identify evidence that the idea has failed.
3. **Bounces and Rejections at Important Levels** — distinguish a meaningful reaction from a touch with no useful confirmation.
4. **Breakouts, Retests and Failed Breakouts** — assess the level, follow-through, acceptance/rejection, and failure conditions.
5. **Trading Range Edges** — plan possible range-boundary trades and define when the range thesis no longer applies.
6. **Compression and Expansion Setups** — recognise tightening price action and plan for either outcome rather than assuming a breakout.
7. **Compare, Filter and Pass** — compare valid, weak, failed, and ambiguous examples; practise choosing no trade when evidence is insufficient.

**Lesson rhythm:** Notice the context → define the setup → state the trigger → mark invalidation → plan risk and management → review the outcome. Every setup should include both a successful example and a failure/ambiguous example.

**Practice outcome:** Learners can write a structured, falsifiable trade thesis for a setup and explain when they would not take it. Examples may be historical or paper-traded; reconstructed trades must be labelled as educational examples.

## Module 4 — The Trade Thesis Project

The capstone is a guided, community-based application project—not a profitability contest and not a requirement to trade live capital.

### Stage 1 — Select and explain a setup
- Choose a real chart and identify the instrument, timeframe, date/session, and relevant market context.
- Mark the important structure/level and name the setup family.
- Explain the evidence supporting the idea and the main uncertainty.

### Stage 2 — Construct the plan
- Define the trigger and the conditions required before entry.
- State the invalidation point and why it invalidates the thesis.
- Show position-risk reasoning and planned management/exit scenarios.
- Identify what would make the student pass or cancel the idea.

### Stage 3 — Share with the community
- Publish the chart and written thesis in the TradeThesis community using a simple submission template.
- Invite questions and alternative interpretations from the instructor and peers.
- Keep the discussion educational and evidence-led; do not frame posts as signals or instructions for others to copy.

### Stage 4 — Review and refine
- Respond to feedback and revise the thesis where warranted.
- Record what changed, what did not, and why.
- After the historical scenario develops—or after a paper-trade simulation—review the original plan against the available evidence.
- Submit a short reflection on decision quality, risk discipline, and lessons learned.

### Suggested student submission template
1. Instrument, timeframe, and chart date/session  
2. Market context and relevant price structure  
3. Setup family and supporting evidence  
4. Trigger and invalidation  
5. Planned risk, management, and exit scenarios  
6. Conditions that would make this a no-trade  
7. Question for the instructor/community  
8. Post-review reflection  

**Assessment principle:** Grade the clarity, completeness, evidence, risk discipline, and willingness to revise the thesis—not whether the trade made money. A well-supported no-trade decision can earn full credit. Historical-chart analysis or paper trading is sufficient; live trading is not required.

## Production guardrails

- Keep lessons practical and concise to support an early release; expand only where learner comprehension requires it.
- Use sourced, dated market charts where possible and record the source. Label schematic or reconstructed examples clearly.
- Do not imply that a chart pattern or indicator guarantees an outcome.
- Do not present community posts as trade signals or encourage copying another learner's position.
- Keep Module 3 about setup construction; reserve independent presentation, group discussion, feedback, and reflection for Module 4.
- This outline does not by itself change the public course page, the lesson manuscripts, the course manifest, or the Django runtime. Those should be reconciled separately before release.
