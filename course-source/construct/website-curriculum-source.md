## The Curriculum

### WEEK 1 — Context: What is the market doing?
- **1.1 Market regimes** — trend, range, crisis: how to know which movie you're in
- **1.2 Volatility context** — ATR, India VIX, and the Indian event calendar: the no-trade windows every trader must know
- **1.3 Review Clinic #1** — three real Nifty months, regime-mapped trade by trade
- **1.4 Worked example** — building your one-screen context dashboard
- **Weekend assignment:** your context dashboard for 5 instruments

### WEEK 2 — Trade Construction: From observation to thesis
- **2.1 The thesis format** — Because X at Y under regime Z → if Q happens, I'm wrong
- **2.2 Setup families** — pullback / breakout / range-fade / event: matching conditions to the setup that belongs there
- **2.3 Review Clinic #2** — three real theses dissected: what made them valid, what would have killed them
- **2.4 Worked example** — grading weak vs strong theses side by side
- **Weekend assignment:** write 5 theses on live charts, rubric-graded

### WEEK 3 — Entry Engineering
- **3.1 Trigger design** — anticipation vs confirmation vs breakout, defined so precisely a stranger could execute it
- **3.2 Execution mechanics** — laddered entries, partial fills, India's liquidity windows (open auction, midday, expiry afternoons)
- **3.3 Missed entries & no-trades** — the re-entry rules; why chasing is a strategy error, not a missed opportunity
- **3.4 Review Clinic #3** — the same setup entered three ways, three different R outcomes
- **Weekend assignment:** 20-entry drill log

### WEEK 4 — Risk Architecture *(the hard gate)*
- **4.1 Invalidation logic** — structural vs volatility vs time stops; writing the "I'm wrong at…" sentence before the entry price
- **4.2 Portfolio-level risk** — correlation clusters, sector concentration, gap and event risk
- **4.3 The risk ledger** — daily/weekly loss limits and the circuit breaker, pre-committed in writing
- **4.4 Worked example** — a complete risk plan for a ₹5L account (and a ₹1.5L one)
- **Weekend assignment (mid-course gate):** your written Risk Policy — reviewed before Week 5 unlocks

### WEEK 5 — Position Sizing & Instrument Selection
- **5.1 Sizing across instruments** — futures, cash, options structures; volatility-adjusted sizing so every trade risks the same rupees
- **5.2 Choosing the instrument for the thesis** — when a stock beats an index, when an option structure beats the underlying
- **5.3 Pyramiding, legally** — adding only to winners, with pre-written rules
- **5.4 Review Clinic #4** — five theses, five instruments, the selection reasoning
- **Weekend assignment:** sizing drills + instrument-selection memos for 5 live setups

### WEEK 5B — The Intraday & Expiry Arena
- **5B.1 The Session Map** — the anatomy of an Indian trading day; the 9:20-straddle cautionary tale; the honest math of intraday trading
- **5B.2 Expiry Day: the gamma arena** — theta's final hour, pinning as observation-not-law, and the Expiry Protocol (half size, defined-risk only, no new trades in the final hour, event expiries skipped)
- **5B.3 Survival Rules** — the printed card: half risk, earlier circuit breaker, one trade per direction, no averaging, written-before-noon plans
- **Weekend assignment:** an observation week + a paper-traded expiry under protocol, graded on adherence — not P&L

### WEEK 6 — Trade Management I: The After-Entry Decision Tree
- **6.1 Pre-written decisions** — the full if/then tree before entry: T1 hit, stall, gap, news, structure break
- **6.2 Partial exits & runners** — the mechanics and the expectancy math of scaling out
- **6.3 Trailing methods** — structure vs ATR vs moving average, matched to setup families
- **6.4 Review Clinic #5** — one real trade managed three ways, outcomes in R
- **Weekend assignment:** your personal After-Entry Decision Tree

### WEEK 7 — Trade Management II: Adverse Scenarios & Psychology Under Position
- **7.1 The Indian market's bad days** — gaps through stops, circuit locks, slippage: response protocols
- **7.2 In-trade psychology** — recognizing tilt while holding; early exit by rule vs by fear
- **7.3 Practical hedges** — protective puts and collars, with honest cost/benefit
- **7.4 Review Clinic #6** — disaster trades: what the plan said vs what the trader did
- **Weekend assignment:** 5 forced adverse scenarios, responses logged against your tree

### WEEK 8 — Exit Engineering
- **8.1 Exit types, deeply** — structure targets, R-multiples, time exits, thesis-change exits — chosen per setup family
- **8.2 Exit-and-re-entry legality** — continuing a trade vs revenge trading
- **8.3 Review Clinic #7** — three exit post-mortems, planned vs emotional, priced in R
- **8.4 Worked example** — exit rules appended to your playbook
- **Weekend assignment:** exit rules for each setup family

### WEEK 9 — The Review System & Strategy Validation
- **9.1 The 7-Step Review at scale** — journal schema, weekly metrics, mistake taxonomy
- **9.2 Honest backtesting** — the 50-trade protocol, with costs and slippage; expectancy, drawdown, losing streaks
- **9.3 From trades to system** — assembling your Strategy Card
- **9.4 Review Clinic #8** — the backtest audit: how results get faked, and how to keep yours honest
- **Weekend assignment:** full backtest report on one setup

### WEEK 10 — Capstone: Your Trading System
- **10.1 Assembling the playbook** — 2–3 strategies with integrated risk policy and decision tree
- **10.2 The go-live plan** — capital staging in 25% steps, month-one review cadence
- **10.3 Capstone defense** — recorded walkthrough, rubric-graded on construction, risk logic, management rules, review discipline
- **10.4 Graduation** — certified playbook; optional showcase on the graduate wall

## The Pattern Vault (included)

**A curated reference library of the market's most-used structures — each taught with the three things a syllabus never tells you: when it works, when it dies, and where it belongs in a complete trade.**

- **Candlestick patterns** — the essential set: doji families, hammer & shooting star, engulfing, morning/evening stars, and more
- **Compression setups** — NR4, NR7, inside bars, and volatility squeezes, from the short-term price-pattern tradition
- **Classic chart patterns** — head & shoulders, double tops and bottoms, flags, triangles, wedges
- **Pivots & key levels** — floor pivots, Camarilla, prior day/week highs and lows
- **Trend & fibonacci tools** — trendlines, channels, retracements

Every entry in the Vault is *evidence, not a signal* — a pattern you understand well enough to know when it doesn't apply. And through the **Pattern Census**, you'll pick one structure, log 30 historical occurrences, and measure its base rate for yourself. You learn patterns by testing them, not memorizing them.
