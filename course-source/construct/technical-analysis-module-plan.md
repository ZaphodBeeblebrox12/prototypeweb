# CONSTRUCT — Technical Analysis Curriculum Update

**Status:** Approved working curriculum on feature branch  
**Branch:** `construct-technical-analysis-modules`  
**Scope:** Curriculum content only. No merge or deployment.

## Direction

Keep Technical Analysis broad enough to be useful, but light enough that it supports CONSTRUCT's main promise: reading context, constructing a thesis, planning risk, managing a trade, and reviewing evidence. Price action remains the foundation; indicators are supporting tools, not standalone buy/sell signals.

## Module 1 — Price Action & Chart Reading

1. **Reading a Price Chart** — chart types, OHLC, candlesticks, timeframes and chart navigation.
2. **Candlesticks & Price Behaviour** — bodies, wicks, momentum, rejection and context.
3. **Market Structure & Swings** — swing highs/lows, higher highs/lows, lower highs/lows and possible transitions.
4. **Market Conditions** — trending, ranging and transitioning conditions; when to stand aside.
5. **Support & Resistance** — zones, repeated reactions, role reversal and failed levels.
6. **Trendlines & Channels** — meaningful lines, channels and avoiding forced fits.
7. **Breakouts, Breakdowns & Retests** — follow-through, failed moves and evidence that a thesis is weakening.
8. **Candlestick Patterns in Context** — doji, engulfing, hammer, shooting star, morning/evening star and selected common formations.
9. **Chart Patterns & Compression** — double tops/bottoms, head and shoulders, triangles, flags, pennants, wedges, inside bars and narrow ranges.
10. **Volume & Participation** — expansion/contraction and limitations across instruments and venues.
11. **Multiple-Timeframe Reading** — higher-timeframe context and lower-timeframe decisions.
12. **Chart Reading Practice** — short guided examples embedded in lessons, rather than a separate chart-lab lesson.

## Module 2 — Indicators & Technical Tools

1. **Moving Averages** — SMA/EMA, direction, dynamic reference areas and crossovers.
2. **Bollinger Bands** — middle/outer bands, width, squeeze and expansion; distinguish trend continuation from mean-reversion hypotheses.
3. **RSI** — momentum, ranges and divergence; overbought/oversold readings are not automatic reversal signals.
4. **MACD** — momentum, signal-line crossovers and histogram interpretation.
5. **ATR & Volatility** — measuring range, contextualising stop distance and adjusting size to risk.
6. **Fibonacci Retracements & Extensions** — selecting meaningful swings, common levels, extensions, confluence and avoiding false precision.
7. **Pivot Points & Reference Levels** — common pivot calculations and how they relate to price structure.
8. **Combining Tools Simply** — avoid redundant indicators, define a small rule set, and test whether a tool adds value.

## Explicit removals from Module 2

- ADX / Directional Movement
- VWAP and Volume-Based Tools
- A separate Indicator Chart Lab

Volume remains a price-action participation topic in Module 1; this does not create a dedicated VWAP or volume-indicator lesson in Module 2. Short chart examples remain part of teaching, but there is no standalone chart-lab lesson.

## Teaching standard

Each topic should explain:
- What the tool or structure describes—and what it does not.
- How context changes its interpretation.
- At least one clear example and one failure or misleading example.
- How a student would define a thesis, trigger, invalidation and risk before acting.
- A small practical exercise or knowledge check with answer feedback.

Use dated, properly sourced historical charts when possible. Clearly label reconstructed examples as illustrative. Do not imply that a pattern or indicator is profitable without appropriate testing. Books support the theory; TradeThesis wording, examples and lesson design must be original rather than copied from another provider.
