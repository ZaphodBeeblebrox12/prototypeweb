## Lesson 1 — What support and resistance actually tell you

### L01-A — Repeated lower reaction area

**Reference read:** The chart shows price moving into a broad lower area, moving upward away from it, and later returning to a similar area. A band around roughly 97.7–99.0 is one defensible way to describe that cluster of lower reactions.

**After the later-candle reveal:** The later sequence trades decisively below the prior area. This reinforces the lesson's point that a support area describes prior behaviour; it does not guarantee that price will hold there in the future.

**Acceptable alternatives:** A somewhat narrower or wider band can be acceptable if it captures the relevant reaction cluster and the learner can explain its edges. Do not grade by exact fictional price boundaries.

**Feedback to avoid:** “The level failed, so it was never support”; “three touches make it strong”; “price had to bounce”; or claims about institutions defending the area.

### L01-B — Repeated upper reaction area

**Reference read:** Price rises into an upper region, turns downward, revisits the region and turns down again. The displayed band around roughly 103.7–105.0 is an illustrative resistance area.

**Acceptable alternatives:** Boundaries can vary modestly while still covering the upper reactions. The learner should explain the region from visible behaviour rather than call it a perfect line.

### L01-PRACTICE-A — Clearer lower reactions

**Reference read:** Two lower reactions are relatively clustered. A lower-area band around roughly 97.7–99.1 is a reasonable example.

**Full-credit reasoning:** Identifies the repeated lower response; recognises the area as approximate; avoids claiming that a future reversal is certain.

### L01-PRACTICE-B — Ambiguous lower reactions

**Reference read:** Lower wicks and follow-through are more uneven. The shown candidate band is deliberately broad and should prompt more uncertainty than Practice A.

**Acceptable conclusion:** “There may be a candidate area here, but the evidence is less clustered. I would not claim a clean, precise zone from this excerpt alone.”

**Do not force a definitive answer** solely because a quiz expects a click.

## Lesson 2 — Read structure before drawing

### L02-A — Rising structure

**Reference read:** The broad sequence advances with generally higher turning highs and pullback lows. The chart should be described from the sequence, not just candle colour.

**Acceptable alternatives:** It is fine to note a local pause or minor irregularity while still identifying the dominant rising structure. Do not require students to mark every swing exactly as in the reference.

### L02-B — Falling structure

**Reference read:** Rallies and declines form generally lower highs and lower lows. The reference points are a small sample of relative turning points that support the broad description.

**Acceptable alternatives:** A learner may identify the structure as falling while noting that one retracement is ambiguous. Do not use “number of red candles” as the reasoning.

### L02-C — Trading range

**Reference read:** Price moves back and forth within a broad region. Highs/lows do not need to align perfectly; the bracket is an approximate structural description.

**Boundary:** This lesson's bracket should be named “trading range,” not labelled support/resistance or used to teach entries/breakouts prematurely.

### L02-D — Trend-to-range transition

**Early state:** The chart contains a clearer rising sequence and the beginning of overlap. The later range is hidden; learners should not be expected to know it in advance.

**Later state:** More overlap and less directional movement become visible. “Possible range” or “less directional” is appropriately cautious. The exact transition point is not mechanically determined.

### L02-E — Independent practice

**Reference read:** Earlier movement is declining; later candles overlap more and become less directional, allowing a possible range interpretation. The point at which the transition begins is open to interpretation.

**Acceptable alternatives:** “A decline that has slowed and is beginning to range” or “still a declining structure with recent consolidation” can both be reasonable if tied to the visible sequence.

**Acceptable alternatives:** A different selection can earn full credit if it explains the price path coherently and does not mark every wiggle. Treat the highlighted borderline point as a discussion prompt, not a mandatory inclusion/exclusion.

## Lesson 6 — Set defensible boundaries

**L06-A — Lower reactions:** Compare wick/body variation, an overly narrow zone, one defensible zone and an overly broad zone. The reference is one coherent boundary choice, not a formula. A learner should explain what was included, what was excluded and why.

**L06-B — Upper reactions:** Apply the same boundary reasoning to an upper reaction area. Do not force the exact displayed edge values if another band captures the relevant reactions consistently.

**L06-C — Independent practice:** A reference zone groups the related lower reactions while remaining distinct from unrelated movement. Accept modestly different edges if the student can defend them consistently.

**Feedback to avoid:** A universal tick-width rule, a fixed percentage/ATR multiplier presented as mandatory, or claims that the drawn band will hold.

## Lesson 7 — Keep the chart readable

**L07-A — Incremental edit:** Start with the over-marked chart, merge nearby lower areas where they describe the same general reaction region, omit minor/distant marks that do not help the current question, and show the clean final view. Explain every edit; do not imply that a zone has no historical relevance simply because it is omitted from this view.

**L07-B — Independent editing:** One reference edit removes redundant and less relevant marks while retaining main lower and upper structure. Preserving a distinct middle area can also be defensible if the learner states why it matters to the question.

**Assessment criterion:** Reward useful simplification and explicit reasoning, not the fewest possible lines as a goal in itself.

## Lesson 8 — Evaluate a zone without inventing a magic score

**L08-A — Many crossings vs fewer interpretable reactions:** Area A has more contacts but mixed, crossing behaviour; Area B has fewer visits in a clearer structural sequence. The comparison is designed to challenge touch-count thinking, not to establish that Area B is statistically more predictive.

**Acceptable conclusion:** A learner can say either area deserves further observation, neither is currently useful, or the excerpt is insufficient—provided they identify the evidence and uncertainty. Touch count alone is not a quality score, and no chart in this lesson proves a future reaction.

## Lesson 10 — Rejection versus acceptance

### L10-A — Wick through, close away, then movement away

- **First interaction state:** The candle probes below the prior area; the following evidence is hidden.
- **Close-away state:** Price closes back into/above the zone and begins moving away.
- **Follow-through state:** Later candles continue upward away from the area.

**Reference read:** Later movement provides more support for describing the event as a rejection/reaction. Avoid saying that the first wick proves the final result.

### L10-B — Close beyond followed by continued action

**Reference read:** The initial close above the band is only the beginning of the evidence. Later candles continue to operate beyond the prior zone, making acceptance/sustained movement a more plausible description in this sequence.

### L10-C — Mixed evidence

**Reference read:** Price repeatedly crosses/returns around the area. “Mixed” or “inconclusive” is the intended classification unless the student cites specific evidence supporting a different, conditional reading.

### L10 practice A–D

- **Practice A:** Wick excursion with close back into/above the band and movement away; rejection evidence is a reasonable description.
- **Practice B:** A move beyond the zone followed by continued action beyond; acceptance evidence is a reasonable description.
- **Practice C:** Repeated mixed crossings; inconclusive/mixed is the reference.
- **Practice D:** A brief move above the band is quickly challenged; do not force a decisive acceptance/rejection label without explaining the limited evidence.

All four classifications describe only the displayed sequence. None is an entry signal or guarantee.

## Lesson 11 — How a zone's significance changes

### L11 timeline stages

1. **Initial reaction:** A lower area is associated with a visible reaction and move away.
2. **Second visit:** A further visit adds context, but there is no universal “touch count” score.
3. **Deeper test:** The zone is tested more deeply; historical relevance and current usefulness must be considered separately.
4. **Move through:** Price moves below the band, challenging the current support interpretation.

**Reference read:** Explain how the conclusion changes as new candles become visible. Do not say that a zone becomes mechanically stronger through repeated touches, or that a zone losing relevance invalidates the earlier observation.

## Lesson 13 — Retests and role reversal

### L13-A / L13-B — Break and return

**Reference read:** First establish that price has moved above a former resistance area. In the next stage, price returns toward the area. At this point, call it a return/retest and assess behaviour; do not rename the area “support” before seeing the relevant response.

### L13-C1 — Return holds beyond the old area

**Reference read:** The subsequent movement remains beyond the old zone and moves away again. This supports a possible role-reversal interpretation but does not make it certain or mandatory in future cases.

### L13-C2 — Return fails

**Reference read:** Price passes back through the old zone into the earlier range. The role-reversal hypothesis has weakened/fails in this example.

**Avoid:** “Every breakout must retest,” “the old resistance must become support,” or chase language when price never returns.

## Lesson 15 — From zones to conditional scenarios

### L15-A — Two-area context

**Reference read:** Price is between a lower and an upper area. The zones frame the context but do not provide an automatic entry. A valid answer should describe what evidence near either area would make one scenario more plausible, what would weaken it, and what the learner cannot know yet.
