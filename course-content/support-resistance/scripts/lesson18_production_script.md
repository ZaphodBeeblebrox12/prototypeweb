# TradeThesis — Support & Resistance
## Lesson 18 Production Script — Final Chart Review and Knowledge Check

**Document role:** Student-facing final assessment and production handoff  
**Curriculum authority:** `tradethesis_support_resistance_curriculum_blueprint.md`  
**Current production reference:** `tradethesis_sr_lessons13-17_production_scripts.md`  
**Curriculum position:** Module 6 — From Zones to a Complete Review  
**Planned duration:** Approximately 30–35 minutes  
**Status:** Draft for curriculum review; no website or repository changes authorised  
**Format:** Independent chart read → written rationale → conditional scenarios → knowledge check → feedback and review  
**Asset rule:** Use a genuinely unfamiliar chart that has not appeared in earlier lessons or ChartLab. Do not use the instructor's actual trade chart as the sole final assessment.

---

## 1. Instructional decision

Lesson 18 is not another demonstration. It is the learner's opportunity to bring the course together without being walked through each decision. It should assess whether the learner can read an unfamiliar chart, select and defend useful support/resistance areas, interpret current price behaviour, outline conditional scenarios, and state what would cause them to reconsider.

The assessment must preserve the evidence-first approach used throughout the course. The initial chart must not contain pre-drawn zones, answer labels, indicators, entry arrows, or future candles that reveal the eventual outcome. The learner submits the main analysis before seeing the reference interpretation.

This is an assessment of **reasoning and chart reading**, not a test of prediction. A learner can demonstrate sound process while leaving a zone provisional, choosing a reasonable alternative boundary, or deciding to stand aside. Do not reward a directional guess merely because it happens to match hidden future price action.

**Design principle:** The learner should be able to explain what they observed, what they inferred, what could happen next, and what evidence would change their mind.

## 2. Learning outcomes

By the end of this assessment, the learner should be able to:

1. Describe the broad structure as rising, falling, ranging, transitional or unclear, and support that description with visible turns.
2. Select relevant support/resistance zones and explain what was deliberately left unmarked.
3. Explain boundary choices through the reactions they include and exclude.
4. Describe the current approach or reaction without treating a wick, close or crossing as a guarantee.
5. Formulate two conditional scenarios—or one scenario plus a justified decision to wait or stand aside.
6. State what evidence would invalidate or weaken the current thesis.
7. Identify the next opposing area and consider whether the available space is meaningful in context.
8. Separate observation, interpretation, uncertainty and eventual outcome.

**Not being assessed:** Whether the student predicts the next move correctly, reproduces the instructor's exact zone boundaries, or selects a trade on every chart.

## 3. Assessment setup and learner-facing opening

### On screen — title

> Final chart review

### On screen — opening copy

> This is your chance to bring the course together on a chart you have not seen before.
>
> Read the structure, choose the areas you believe matter, explain your boundaries, and describe what you would watch next. There is no need to force a trade or pretend the chart is clearer than it is.

### On screen — assessment note

> Your reasoning matters more than matching one exact drawing. A well-supported alternative or a justified decision to wait can be a strong answer.

**Production cue:** Show a compact progress outline of the stages, without revealing the rubric's detailed point allocations or reference annotations before submission. Do not use a timer that adds pressure. The planned duration is guidance, not a speed test.

### Instructor-only setup rules

- Select a chart that has **not** appeared in Lessons 1–17 or any ChartLab study.
- Prefer a clean, readable sequence with enough history to identify structure, more than one plausible area, minor distracting turns, and some real interpretive uncertainty.
- The chart must allow a defensible answer that a zone is ambiguous or that no trade is justified.
- Do not use the instructor's actual trade chart as the only final assessment. Learners may already know how that chart was analysed.
- The initial frame must show only information available at the assessment point. Hide future candles and any later outcome.
- Decide the base chart and its reveal points before writing the answer key. Do not draw a chart to match a pre-written “correct” answer.
- Use a fictional schematic chart unless historical data is deliberately selected and documented. Label schematics unobtrusively: **“Illustrative chart — not historical market data.”** Do not attach a real instrument or timeframe to fictional data.

## 4. Student-facing assessment sequence

### Stage 1 — Read the broader structure

**Show:** The unfamiliar chart with adequate left-side history and current price visible. No zones, swing labels, indicators, arrows or future candles.

**On-screen prompt:**

> **1. What is the broader structure?**
>
> Describe the path of price before marking zones. Use the major turns rather than every small fluctuation.

**Response fields:**
- Structure: `Generally rising` / `Generally falling` / `Broad range` / `Transition or mixed` / `Unclear from this excerpt`.
- Evidence: short text entry — *Which visible swings or areas support that description?*
- Uncertainty: short text entry — *What part of the structure is not yet clear?*

**Reference-answer policy:** The selected category must be supported by the actual chart. If the chart has a mixed late section, an answer that describes the earlier structure and identifies the later transition may be stronger than a single label for the whole chart.

**Feedback after submission (before reference reveal):**

> You have recorded your first reading. Keep it available as you move through the assessment; new observations may refine it, but they should not rewrite what was visible at the start.

**Production cue:** Preserve this response for the later review. Do not grade a plausible “transition / unclear” response as incorrect when the chart genuinely supports that interpretation.

### Stage 2 — Select the zones that matter

**Show:** Keep the same chart and scale. Allow the learner to add candidate zones using the course's drawing interaction or choose broad candidate areas if drawing is not available.

**On-screen prompt:**

> **2. Mark the areas you consider most relevant.**
>
> Choose zones that help explain the structure or current price context. You do not need to mark every visible turn.

**Required learner actions:**
1. Mark one or more support/resistance areas they consider relevant. The chart may justify more than one area on either side or may not justify a clear zone in one location.
2. For each selected area, provide a short rationale: *“I kept this area because…”*
3. State at least one candidate area, minor turn or historical mark intentionally left out, and why.
4. Where evidence is mixed, the learner may label a zone provisional or leave a candidate unmarked.

**On-screen reminder:**

> The point is not to produce the most marks or the fewest marks. Explain the job each retained area does in this reading.

**Instructor-only answer key requirements:**
- Identify the major structural anchors and the reactions that support the reference zones.
- Explain at least one candidate area the reference leaves out, merges or treats as secondary.
- Include any alternative zone that is reasonably defensible.
- Do not require a universal fixed number of zones; if the exercise interface applies a limit, state that it is an assessment constraint rather than a market law.

### Stage 3 — Defend the boundaries

**Show:** The learner's selected zones remain visible. No reference overlay yet.

**On-screen prompt:**

> **3. Explain your boundaries.**
>
> For each main zone, describe what the upper and lower boundaries include. You may refer to wick extremes, candle bodies/closes, or a consistent combination.

**Response prompts:**
- *What structural turn or group of reactions anchors this zone?*
- *Which visible prices or candle features helped you choose the boundaries?*
- *What evidence did you leave outside the zone, and why?*
- *Could another boundary choice also be reasonable? Explain.*

**Feedback principle:** Look for a coherent explanation of coverage and width, not precision to the tick. A narrower or wider zone may be defensible if it represents the relevant reactions clearly and the learner states its limitations.

**Common misconception to catch:** A learner says the zone must include every wick, or that a wick-only/body-only approach is universally correct. Feedback should ask what the chosen convention is intended to represent rather than prescribe one method for every chart.

### Stage 4 — Interpret the current approach or reaction

**Show:** The same chart up to the assessment cut-off point. If the chart includes a current interaction with a zone, retain only candles known at that point. Do not reveal subsequent price action.

**On-screen prompt:**

> **4. What is price doing around the area now?**
>
> First describe the evidence you can see. Then explain what it may mean—and what remains unknown.

**Response fields:**
- **Observation:** *What happened in the candles shown?* (for example, approach direction, overlap, a wick through the area, a close relative to it, or movement away).
- **Interpretation:** *What does that evidence make reasonable to consider?*
- **Uncertainty:** *What can you not conclude yet?*

**Model-answer shape (not a pre-filled answer):**

> Price has [observable description] relative to the marked area. That is consistent with [a cautious interpretation], but the sequence does not yet establish [unproven conclusion]. I would need to observe [relevant further evidence] before making a stronger claim.

**Scoring guardrail:** Do not award full credit to “it will bounce” or “it will break” without chart evidence and a clear uncertainty statement—even if the hidden outcome later matches the guess.

### Stage 5 — Build conditional scenarios

**On-screen prompt:**

> **5. What are the plausible next paths?**
>
> Write two conditional scenarios, or give one scenario and explain why waiting or standing aside is currently more reasonable.

**Scenario template:**
- **If** [specific, observable development] occurs, **then** [interpretation or next step] becomes more plausible.
- **If instead** [different observable development] occurs, **then** [alternative interpretation] becomes more plausible.
- **I would reconsider** the first scenario if [evidence that would weaken or invalidate it].

**Acceptable examples of conditional language:**
- “If price moves away from the area and subsequent candles support that reading, a return into the prior range becomes one possible path.”
- “If price trades through the area and begins to sustain itself on the other side, the original reaction thesis weakens.”
- “If the candles continue to overlap the zone without clearer follow-through, I would keep the interpretation provisional.”

These are language examples only. The actual answer key must match the selected assessment chart and must not dictate the direction of its hidden future move.

**Feedback if the learner gives only one confident prediction:**

> That is one possible outcome, but a scenario is more useful when it states the evidence it depends on. What alternative would change your reading, and what would you need to see before treating the first idea as better supported?

**Feedback if the learner chooses to stand aside:**

> Waiting can be a defensible conclusion. Explain what is unclear, what evidence you are waiting for, or why the available space does not currently support a useful plan.

### Stage 6 — Invalidation and available space

**On-screen prompt:**

> **6. What would make you reconsider—and where is the next opposing area?**

**Required response:**
- State one specific observable development that would weaken or invalidate the primary scenario.
- Identify the next meaningful opposing zone, if one can be reasonably identified.
- Explain whether the space between the current area and the opposing area appears meaningful or limited.
- If the chart does not provide enough context to identify an opposing zone, say so instead of inventing one.

**On-screen note:**

> A zone is not an entry trigger by itself. Available space and invalidation help organise an analysis; a separate method and risk plan would be required before making a real trade decision.

**Scoring guardrail:** Do not introduce an unapproved universal minimum reward-to-risk ratio. This assessment checks whether the learner considered the space and explained its relevance, not whether they applied an unstated numeric rule.

### Stage 7 — Submit and check understanding

Before revealing the reference analysis, show a concise review screen with the learner's own entries:

- Broad structure and evidence.
- Zones selected and rationale.
- Boundary notes and omitted areas.
- Current observation and interpretation.
- Scenario(s), uncertainty and invalidation.
- Next opposing area / available space.

**On-screen copy:**

> Review your notes before submitting. You can still correct an omission or clarify an explanation. Once submitted, your answers will be compared with a reasoned reference—not against a claim that there is only one valid drawing.

**Production cue:** Save all assessment responses as separate concepts from quiz answers, drawing coordinates and completion status if persistent account-backed saving is implemented. Until it is implemented and tested, do not promise cross-device persistence or a downloadable report.

## 5. Final knowledge check (6–8 questions)

Use seven questions. Present one at a time, provide explanatory feedback, and allow the learner to review incorrect responses. The knowledge check reinforces course principles; it must not replace the chart-based assessment.

### Q1. What does a support/resistance zone tell you?

A. The exact price where the next reversal must occur.  
B. An area associated with prior price behaviour that may be relevant to the current reading. **Correct.**  
C. A complete entry signal by itself.

**Feedback:** A zone organises visible historical behaviour. It is not a guarantee or a full trading plan.

### Q2. Why can two different zone boundaries both be defensible?

A. Boundaries are random, so any drawing is equally useful.  
B. Different conventions may capture the same related reactions, provided the choice and its limits are explained. **Correct.**  
C. Only the lowest wick is relevant in every chart.

**Feedback:** Reasonable alternatives are evidence-based, not arbitrary. Explain what the band includes and excludes.

### Q3. Why is touch count alone insufficient to rank a zone?

A. A count does not explain the structure, clarity of reactions or current context. **Correct.**  
B. The highest touch count always identifies the strongest zone.  
C. Zones with multiple reactions should never be considered.

**Feedback:** The count is an observation. It is not a complete quality score or a guarantee of future behaviour.

### Q4. Does one wick through a zone prove a breakout or acceptance beyond it?

A. Yes, any wick through the boundary settles the question.  
B. No. It shows that price traded through that area during the candle; the wider sequence and subsequent action still matter. **Correct.**  
C. Yes, if the wick is longer than the candle body.

**Feedback:** Describe the excursion first. Use later candles only when they are actually available.

### Q5. What is role reversal?

A. A rule that former resistance must become support after an upside break.  
B. A possible change in how a broken area behaves; the new role must be assessed from subsequent evidence. **Correct.**  
C. A reason to redraw the zone around the latest candles.

**Feedback:** Former resistance may act as support, or former support may act as resistance, but the role can fail.

### Q6. What belongs in the invalidation field of a conditional scenario?

A. A specific observable development that would weaken or contradict the scenario. **Correct.**  
B. A statement that the original zone must hold.  
C. The final outcome, written as if it had been known in advance.

**Feedback:** Invalidation keeps a thesis open to evidence. It is not a retrospective explanation.

### Q7. When is standing aside a defensible conclusion?

A. Never; every chart must provide a trade.  
B. When evidence is unclear, the next opposing area leaves limited space, or the conditions of the stated plan are absent. **Correct.**  
C. Only when price has already moved away.

**Feedback:** A chart can be worth studying without offering a clear plan. “Wait” or “stand aside” is a valid conclusion when it is explained.

## 6. Reference analysis reveal

**Reveal rule:** Show the reference analysis only after the learner submits the chart responses and knowledge check. Keep the learner's original answers visible beside the reference so the comparison is about reasoning, not replacement.

The answer key must be written after the assessment chart and all annotations are final. It should contain:

1. **Structure reading:** a reference description with the major swings that support it; note where a different structure label is reasonable.
2. **Reference zones:** each band's structural anchor, which reactions it captures, and what it leaves out.
3. **Selection choices:** why a minor or redundant candidate was omitted, combined or treated as secondary.
4. **Boundary rationale:** the chosen wick/body/close convention and the trade-off involved.
5. **Current behaviour:** strictly describe the candles visible at the assessment cut-off; do not refer to unseen future price.
6. **Conditional scenarios:** at least two plausible paths or one path plus a justified stand-aside decision.
7. **Invalidation and space:** the observable evidence that would challenge the main scenario, and the relevant opposing zone if one is visible.
8. **Uncertainty note:** at least one genuine limitation or acceptable alternative if the chart supports it.

**Model reference format:**

> **What I observed:** [facts directly visible on the chosen chart].  
> **How I read it:** [interpretation supported by those facts].  
> **What I would watch next:** [specific condition(s)].  
> **What would change my mind:** [invalidation or reassessment evidence].  
> **What remains uncertain:** [a genuine limitation of the chart or interpretation].

Do not fill these fields with generic claims before the actual chart exists. If the final geometry does not support the proposed answer, revise the chart or the key.

### Acceptable alternative policy

- A zone with slightly different boundaries can be acceptable if it captures the relevant reaction group and the learner explains the trade-off.
- A learner may retain an additional area if they can explain a distinct structural role it serves.
- “Transition / unclear” can be the strongest reading if the chart does not support a cleaner label.
- A decision to wait or stand aside can be fully defensible if the learner identifies the ambiguity, limited space or missing condition.
- Do not accept arbitrary selections simply because this course recognises interpretive judgement. The learner must still point to chart-visible evidence and explain the reasoning.

## 7. Scoring rubric (100 points)

| Component | Points | Full-credit standard |
|---|---:|---|
| Structure reading | 15 | Describes the larger sequence using meaningful swings/range behaviour, without labelling every fluctuation. |
| Zone selection | 20 | Selects areas tied to visible structure, explains why they matter and avoids unsupported clutter. |
| Boundary rationale | 15 | Explains zone width and placement, including relevant evidence included and excluded. |
| Interpretation of behaviour | 15 | Distinguishes observation from inference; does not treat one wick or close as a guarantee. |
| Conditional scenarios | 15 | Provides clear if/then reasoning and an alternative path, or a justified stand-aside decision. |
| Invalidation and space | 10 | Names evidence that would challenge the main thesis and considers the next opposing area / available space where visible. |
| Reasoning and uncertainty | 10 | Explains decisions, acknowledges ambiguity and states what remains unknown. |
| **Total** | **100** | |

### Scoring guidance

**Structure reading — 15 points**  
- Full: names the broad structure and points to the swings/sequence supporting it.  
- Partial: plausible label but little evidence or too many minor turns.  
- Low: relies mainly on candle colour or a single isolated candle.

**Zone selection — 20 points**  
- Full: selected zones have clear structural roles; omissions or merges are explained.  
- Partial: useful area identified but additional marks are poorly justified.  
- Low: marks every candidate equally or selects areas mainly to fit an imagined outcome.

**Boundary rationale — 15 points**  
- Full: boundaries capture the stated reaction area and the learner explains what remains outside.  
- Partial: broadly plausible placement but unclear width logic.  
- Low: treats one universal wick/body convention as mandatory or produces an uninformative band.

**Interpretation of behaviour — 15 points**  
- Full: reports the visible sequence, gives a cautious interpretation and states what is not established.  
- Partial: mostly sound interpretation but overstates one candle or misses some uncertainty.  
- Low: declares a guaranteed reversal/break or claims hidden motives without evidence.

**Conditional scenarios — 15 points**  
- Full: specifies distinct scenarios and observable conditions that would support or weaken them. A reasoned stand-aside conclusion may receive full credit.  
- Partial: names alternatives but does not identify evidence that distinguishes them.  
- Low: gives only a directional prediction with no conditions.

**Invalidation and space — 10 points**  
- Full: names a relevant change in price behaviour and discusses the next opposing area/space where available.  
- Partial: identifies one component but not the other, or gives a vague invalidation.  
- Low: says the zone must hold or ignores clearly visible opposing structure.

**Reasoning and uncertainty — 10 points**  
- Full: distinguishes observation from interpretation and identifies a real limitation.  
- Partial: reasoning is plausible but uncertainty is generic.  
- Low: treats the analysis as certain or changes the explanation based on the hidden result.

### Proposed completion standard — requires pilot testing

The master curriculum proposes **70/100 overall, with no zero in zone selection or conditional reasoning**. This threshold is a proposal to test with learners before publication, not a validated measure of trading competence. Give explanatory feedback. If the learner does not meet the standard, offer a **parallel unfamiliar chart** for a retry rather than making them repeat the same chart whose answer they have now seen.

Do not present completion as proof of live-trading skill, future profitability or readiness to trade independently.

## 8. Feedback and retry policy

**If the learner scores well but zones differ from the reference:**

> Your boundaries differ from the reference, but the explanation connects them to visible structure. Review what each version includes and excludes. An alternative can be reasonable when the reasoning is consistent.

**If the structure label is plausible but unsupported:**

> Revisit the larger turns. Which sequence of highs and lows supports your description? Try to explain the overall path rather than relying on one candle.

**If the chart is over-marked:**

> For each zone you kept, ask what it adds to the current reading. Consider whether nearby marks describe the same broad reaction area or whether one can be left out for this question.

**If boundaries lack rationale:**

> Before adjusting the zone, state what reactions it is meant to describe. Then explain what the selected boundaries include and why other nearby prices were left outside.

**If the answer overpredicts:**

> That is one possible path, but the chart does not establish it as certain. What evidence would support the scenario, what alternative should remain open, and what would make you reconsider?

**If the learner stands aside:**

> Waiting is a valid analytical outcome when you can explain what is unclear or what condition is missing. Compare your reason with the reference and see whether the same limitation appears there.

**After reveal:** Give the student a separate reflection prompt:

> What is one part of your analysis you would keep, and one part you would revise after seeing the reference? Explain the evidence behind each choice.

Do not overwrite the original response. The learner should be able to compare initial reasoning with post-review reasoning.

## 9. Chart asset specification

### L18-A — Final unfamiliar assessment chart

**Purpose:** Assess integrated chart reading on a chart not previously taught.

**Required properties:**
- Sufficient left-side history to recognise the main structure.
- Several meaningful swings or a broad range/transition.
- Candidate zones that require selection rather than all being obvious.
- At least one minor or redundant area that can reasonably be omitted.
- A current approach/reaction that can be described without knowing the next move.
- A plausible next opposing area where possible, but do not force one if the chart context does not support it.
- At least one reasonable ambiguity or defensible stand-aside interpretation.
- No pre-drawn zones, indicators, entry/exit arrows, pattern labels or future outcome in the initial state.

**Reveal states:**
1. Plain unfamiliar chart.
2. Learner's structure description recorded.
3. Learner's zones and omissions recorded.
4. Boundary rationale and current behaviour responses recorded.
5. Conditional scenarios, invalidation and available-space responses recorded.
6. Full submission/knowledge check.
7. Reference analysis and acceptable alternatives revealed.
8. Rubric feedback and reflection.

**Alt text template:** “Illustrative candlestick chart with a mixed sequence of major turns and minor fluctuations, shown without annotations for an independent support/resistance assessment.” Final alt text must describe the actual finished chart.

### L18-B — Parallel retry chart

- Use a different unfamiliar chart, not a cropped or mirrored version of L18-A.
- Test the same learning outcomes at comparable difficulty.
- Build its reference key only after the asset is final.
- Include acceptable alternatives and a valid stand-aside interpretation where chart evidence supports it.
- Do not reveal this chart or its answer in the public course preview.

### L18-C — Reference analysis and rubric

- Provide reference annotations as separate layers from the base chart.
- Record rationale for each retained, omitted or combined zone.
- Explicitly state valid alternate interpretations.
- Keep student marks distinguishable from reference marks using labels or line styles as well as colour.
- Ensure the annotated comparison remains readable on mobile.

## 10. Chart Reading Notes — final recap

After feedback, invite the learner to complete a final short note. This is a learning recap, not a performance journal.

- **The structural read I can defend:** …
- **The zone decision I can explain:** …
- **The evidence I would watch next:** …
- **The condition that would make me reconsider:** …
- **The uncertainty I am willing to leave unresolved:** …

**On-screen close:**

> You have completed the course's final chart review. The goal was not to remove uncertainty or predict every move. It was to make your reading explainable: what you saw, why you marked an area, what you would watch next, and what would change your mind.
>
> **Keep this:** A defensible analysis explains both its evidence and its limits.

Add the standard unobtrusive disclaimer: *“For educational purposes only. Trading involves risk.”*
