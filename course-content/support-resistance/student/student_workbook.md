# TradeThesis — Support & Resistance
## Student Workbook

**Use this workbook alongside the lessons.** The purpose is not to predict every move. It is to practise describing what is visible, explaining your interpretation, identifying what would change your view, and being comfortable with uncertainty.

> **Core habit:** Observation → Interpretation → Conditional scenario → Invalidation / reconsideration.
>
> Support and resistance areas provide context. They do not guarantee a reaction, a breakout, an entry or a profitable outcome. “Wait,” “unclear,” and “stand aside” can be well-reasoned conclusions.

---

## How to use the workbook

Keep notes before the reference answer is revealed. When the lesson reveals a model interpretation, compare the reasoning—not just whether your drawing looks the same. Two boundaries can both be defensible if each is anchored to visible structure and explained consistently.

For charts used in a lesson, record only what was visible at the point of the exercise. Do not use later price movement to claim you knew the outcome in advance.

# Quick glossary

**Support area:** A price area where downward movement has previously paused or where price has moved away upward. It is not guaranteed to hold.

**Resistance area:** A price area where upward movement has previously paused or where price has moved away downward. It is not guaranteed to hold.

**Swing point:** A local high or low that helps describe the chart’s structure. Not every small turn is equally important.

**Zone boundary:** The chosen edges of a support/resistance area. Boundaries need a visible rationale; a zone is not required to match every wick exactly.

**Rejection / reaction:** A description of price moving away from an area after interacting with it. The strength of the interpretation depends on the broader evidence.

**Acceptance / sustained movement beyond:** A description used when subsequent price action supports the view that price is operating beyond the old area. A single crossing is not enough by itself to guarantee this.

**Breakout:** A move beyond a previously relevant area. Whether it is sustained, temporary or inconclusive must be judged from subsequent evidence.

**Retest:** A return towards an area after price has moved beyond it. A retest may not occur and is not automatically an entry signal.

**Role reversal:** A possible change in how an area behaves—for example, former resistance becoming relevant as support after an upside break. It can fail.

**Invalidation:** Evidence or a condition that would materially weaken the current thesis and require reassessment. It is not a guarantee that losses are avoided.

**Opposing area / available space:** The next relevant zone in the path being considered and the distance/context between it and current price.

**Conditional scenario:** An if/then description of what becomes more plausible if specified evidence appears. It is not a prediction presented as certainty.

## Personal learning review

After finishing the course, answer these without looking back at the reference answers.

1. What can a support/resistance zone help me describe, and what can it not guarantee?
2. How do I distinguish what I see from what I infer?
3. What makes a zone useful enough to keep on my chart?
4. How do I reassess a zone as new evidence appears?
5. What would make a breakout, retest or role-reversal interpretation weaker?
6. Can I write a conditional scenario with a meaningful invalidation condition?
7. When is “I do not have enough evidence yet” the better answer?
8. What is one chart-reading habit I will keep practising?

> A good review does not require certainty. It requires an honest account of the evidence, the judgement made from it, and what would change that judgement.
