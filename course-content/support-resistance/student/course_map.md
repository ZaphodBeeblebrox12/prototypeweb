# Course map — Support & Resistance

**Student-facing overview · first-pass draft**

This is the sequence of skills the course builds. Each module gives you a piece of the process; the later modules ask you to use the earlier pieces together.

| Module | Lessons | The skill you are building | What you should carry forward |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1. Understand | 1–2 | Recognise what support/resistance describes and read structure before marking zones | Describe the chart before explaining it |
| 2. Find | 3–5 | Select important turning points and areas while leaving minor noise unmarked | Choose fewer, more defensible areas |
| 3. Draw and evaluate | 6–8 | Set boundaries consistently, simplify the chart and evaluate a zone in context | Explain your choices; avoid magic scores |
| 4. Read behaviour | 9–11 | Read the approach and distinguish reaction, acceptance and inconclusive evidence; update your view over time | Let later evidence refine—not rewrite—the earlier observation |
| 5. Breakouts and failures | 12–14 | Assess crossings, follow-through, retests, role reversal and failed attempts | Judge the sequence, not a single candle or the eventual outcome alone |
| 6. Apply and review | 15–18 | Build conditional scenarios, review authentic decisions and apply the whole method to a new chart | Make a conditional plan, state invalidation and accept “stand aside” when justified |

## Three practice labs

- **Lesson 5 — Clear W:** choose the areas that help explain the chart and remove clutter.
- **Lesson 8 — Uneven Lows:** evaluate a zone without treating repeated touches as a universal score.
- **Lesson 14 — Wider Neckline:** review a failed-breakout sequence and defend a reasonable marking.

These are the only three ChartLab studies in the curriculum.

## The through-line

**Observe → interpret → form a conditional scenario → state invalidation or reconsider.**

The process is more important than making the chart look busy. The course's reference marks are examples to reason with, not answers that every learner must copy exactly.

## What this map does not promise

Finishing the course does not guarantee trading performance. The proposed completion and final-assessment thresholds require pilot testing, and the course does not prescribe one universal entry system or risk model.
