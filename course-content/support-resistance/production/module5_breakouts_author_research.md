# Module 5 — Breakouts & Failures: Author Research Note

**Purpose:** Internal instructor/production reference for canonical Lessons 12–14. It records sources that informed original TradeThesis lesson design. It is not student-facing copy or a summary of entire books.

## John J. Murphy — *Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets*

The published contents establish Murphy's broad treatment of closing prices and gaps, volume, continuation patterns, and variations from ideal patterns. These topics support teaching learners to distinguish an intraday penetration from a close beyond a zone and to expect real sequences to deviate from textbook diagrams.

**Use in Lesson 12:** make the learner state a confirmation convention and its trade-off. Do not claim Murphy prescribes a fixed two-close rule or that any filter guarantees continuation.

Source: Google Books record and table of contents: https://books.google.com/books/about/Technical_Analysis_of_the_Financial_Mark.html?id=ojTgDwAAQBAJ

## Adam Grimes — *The Art and Science of Technical Analysis*

Publisher chapter summaries discuss transitions between trends and ranges, practical trading templates, failure tests and failed breakouts. Grimes's own article describes a failure test as a move beyond a prior pivot or support/resistance area followed by a quick reversal. These are teaching emphases, not a universal rule or proof that each failure leads to a large opposite move.

**Use in Lessons 12–14:** treat breakouts as uncertain transitions; compare first break, follow-through, return and subsequent response; reconstruct what was knowable before revealing the ending.

## Jack D. Schwager and Mark Etzkorn — *A Complete Guide to the Futures Market*

Chapter 8 covers support/resistance and possible role reversal after a range boundary is crossed. Chapter 15 gives failed signals and bull/bear traps a prominent treatment. The course applies those ideas as conditional interpretation: a move that fails to hold weakens the continuation thesis, but does not automatically define an opposite-side trade.

Sources:
- “Support and Resistance,” Chapter 8: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9781119209713.ch8
- “The Most Important Rule in Chart Analysis,” Chapter 15 preview: https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/a-complete-guide/9781118853757/c15.xhtml

## Brian Shannon — *Technical Analysis Using Multiple Timeframes*

Shannon's public material uses timeframes like different levels of magnification: a local move may look different inside a broader trend or range. His public discussion of volume also illustrates that practitioners differ on requiring unusually high volume as a universal breakout filter.

**Use in Lessons 12 and 14:** ask what scale is being analysed; introduce volume as contextual evidence, not an absolute gate. Keep this module focused on breakouts and failures rather than expanding it into a complete multi-timeframe system.

Sources:
- “6 Ways to Trade Using Multiple Timeframes”: https://alphatrends.net/archives/6-ways-you-can-trade-with-multiple-timeframes/
- Public interview discussing volume and price: https://alphatrends.net/archives/interview-stockbsessed-03112023/

## Carol L. Osler — bounded empirical context, not a universal proof

Osler's “Support for Resistance: Technical Analysis and Intraday Exchange Rates” examined supplied support/resistance levels in particular foreign-exchange settings. It is a bounded example that some studied levels had predictive value in a specific context. It does not validate all manually drawn zones, all instruments or retail breakout rules.

Source: Federal Reserve Bank of New York, *Economic Policy Review*: https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.html

## Principles distilled for the course

1. **Context before label:** identify the prior area and market state before interpreting a break.
2. **Evidence, not magic confirmation:** wick, close, distance, time outside, volume and follow-through have different trade-offs; none guarantees the next move.
3. **After the break is part of the event:** compare hold, return, move back through, no retest and inconclusive sequences.
4. **Failure revises the thesis; it does not dictate a trade:** contradictory evidence weakens continuation; consider alternatives without assuming an automatic reversal.
5. **No hidden-motive claims from OHLC alone:** do not state that institutions, smart money or stop hunters caused a move without independent evidence.
6. **Record the decision before the outcome:** staged reveals help prevent later candles from rewriting the learner's memory of what was knowable earlier.
