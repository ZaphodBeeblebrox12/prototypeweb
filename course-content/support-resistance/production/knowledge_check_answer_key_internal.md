## Lesson 01 — What support and resistance actually tell you
**Focus:** Understand zones as areas associated with prior behaviour, not promises.

### L01-KC-01. What is the most defensible description of a support/resistance zone?
**Correct answer:** An area associated with prior price behaviour that may matter to the current reading
**Rationale:** A zone organises evidence about prior behaviour. Its relevance to the current reading remains conditional.
**Common misconception to address:** Avoid turning historical reaction into a guaranteed future outcome.

### L01-KC-02. Price moves through a marked zone. What can you conclude from that fact alone?
**Correct answer:** Price traded through the marked area; the wider sequence determines what that means
**Rationale:** The crossing is an observation. Its interpretation depends on the close, subsequent action and broader context.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not force one candle or event to settle the whole question.

### L01-KC-03. Which sentence best separates observation from interpretation?
**Correct answer:** Several candles paused in this area; it may be relevant again
**Rationale:** The first clause describes visible behaviour; “may be relevant” is a qualified interpretation.
**Common misconception to address:** Avoid asserting intent, certainty or a trade signal from the chart alone.


## Lesson 02 — Read structure before drawing
**Focus:** Describe broad structure before adding zones.

### L02-KC-01. Before drawing support/resistance zones, what should the learner do first?
**Correct answer:** Describe the broad visible structure and its evidence
**Rationale:** Structure gives context for deciding which turns and areas matter.
**Common misconception to address:** Drawing first can make the learner fit the structure to their marks.

### L02-KC-02. A chart alternates between higher and lower swings without a clear sequence. What is the most defensible response?
**Correct answer:** Describe it as transitional or unclear and explain why
**Rationale:** An unclear or transitional description is valid when the sequence does not support a cleaner label.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not infer broad structure from one candle or from the desire for a label.

### L02-KC-03. What evidence most directly supports describing a chart as ranging?
**Correct answer:** Price repeatedly moving within a broad area without sustained directional progress
**Rationale:** A range is a description of the broader path, not one isolated candle.
**Common misconception to address:** Avoid treating a single event as proof of the whole structure.


## Lesson 03 — Identify meaningful swing points
**Focus:** Select consequential turns without marking every fluctuation.

### L03-KC-01. What makes a swing point useful for reading structure?
**Correct answer:** It helps explain a consequential change in the visible path
**Rationale:** Useful turns help explain the path and its changes; local turns do not all carry equal explanatory value.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not use recency or visual extremity as the only selection rule.

### L03-KC-02. Two learners select slightly different swing points, and both explain their choices with chart evidence. How should the reference be treated?
**Correct answer:** Both may be defensible if each selection explains the structure and its limits
**Rationale:** The reference is one reasoned example, not the unique correct set of coordinates.
**Common misconception to address:** Accept evidence-based alternatives, not arbitrary marking.

### L03-KC-03. Why can a simplified swing view be useful after identifying candidate turns?
**Correct answer:** It reveals whether the selected turns explain the path without unnecessary clutter
**Rationale:** Simplification tests whether the selected points communicate the structure efficiently.
**Common misconception to address:** Simplifying is an explanatory check, not proof or a substitute for rationale.


## Lesson 04 — Zones, not perfect lines
**Focus:** Choose a defensible band that captures related reactions.

### L04-KC-01. Why use a zone instead of insisting on one exact price?
**Correct answer:** Related reactions may span an area because wicks and bodies vary
**Rationale:** Price reactions can cluster across a band rather than at one perfectly repeated price.
**Common misconception to address:** Precision is not the same as accuracy; a zone still needs evidence.

### L04-KC-02. A proposed band includes only the tip of one wick and excludes nearby related reactions. What is the main concern?
**Correct answer:** It may be too narrow to represent the visible reaction area
**Rationale:** A boundary should be justified by the relevant cluster of visible behaviour, not one isolated point by default.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not grade a boundary by how neat or narrow it looks.

### L04-KC-03. What is the most useful way to compare narrow, defensible and broad zone examples?
**Correct answer:** Keep the chart scale consistent and compare what each band includes or excludes
**Rationale:** A consistent scale makes it easier to evaluate whether the boundary choices represent the same evidence fairly.
**Common misconception to address:** A band can be too narrow or too broad; size alone does not establish quality.


## Lesson 05 — Choose the right areas and reduce clutter
**Focus:** Prioritise useful zones and remove low-value marks.

### L05-KC-01. On an overmarked chart, what is the best first editing question?
**Correct answer:** Which marks materially improve the explanation of the chart?
**Rationale:** The aim is explanatory value: retain marks that help the learner read the structure and context.
**Common misconception to address:** More annotations do not automatically mean more analysis.

### L05-KC-02. Two marked areas communicate almost the same reaction history. What may improve readability?
**Correct answer:** Merge or remove redundant marks if that preserves the useful explanation
**Rationale:** Redundant areas can often be merged or removed when they do not add a distinct insight.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not confuse quantity with completeness.

### L05-KC-03. Why should a learner explain which marks were removed?
**Correct answer:** It shows the simplification was deliberate and evidence-based
**Rationale:** Naming what was removed and why makes the editing judgement reviewable.
**Common misconception to address:** The exercise is about chart explanation, not predicting price or copying coordinates.


## Lesson 06 — Set defensible zone boundaries
**Focus:** Explain upper and lower boundaries with visible evidence.

### L06-KC-01. What should guide a zone boundary choice?
**Correct answer:** The visible reaction evidence being included and excluded
**Rationale:** The boundary needs a visible rationale, including how relevant wicks and bodies are handled.
**Common misconception to address:** There is no universal width that makes every zone correct.

### L06-KC-02. Why might wicks and candle bodies both matter when evaluating a reaction area?
**Correct answer:** They show different parts of the price behaviour; the chosen convention should fit the evidence and be explained
**Rationale:** Wicks show intraperiod excursions while bodies show the open-close area; the lesson requires reasoned boundary choices, not one universal rule.
**Common misconception to address:** Avoid declaring one candle feature universally decisive.

### L06-KC-03. Two zones differ slightly in exact placement. What should be assessed before calling one wrong?
**Correct answer:** Whether the learner can explain the reactions included and excluded
**Rationale:** The rationale and representation of visible evidence matter more than pixel-perfect coordinate matching.
**Common misconception to address:** Alternative boundaries can be defensible if they are explained.


## Lesson 07 — Keep the chart readable
**Focus:** Remove marks that do not improve the explanation.

### L07-KC-01. What is a good reason to remove a mark from a chart?
**Correct answer:** It does not add distinct explanatory value, or duplicates a better-supported mark
**Rationale:** Marks should earn their place by helping the chart’s explanation, not by matching a rigid appearance.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not remove context simply to make the chart look tidy.

### L07-KC-02. When might leaving an area unmarked be reasonable?
**Correct answer:** When marking it would add little to the current structural explanation
**Rationale:** Omission can be a deliberate choice when a mark does not help the current read.
**Common misconception to address:** A clean chart is not an empty chart; omissions should be contextual.

### L07-KC-03. After merging redundant zones, what should the learner check?
**Correct answer:** Whether the remaining marks still explain the important structure and reactions
**Rationale:** Editing should improve readability without removing the key information needed to interpret the chart.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not optimise for a fixed number of marks.


## Lesson 08 — Evaluate a zone without inventing a magic score
**Focus:** Evaluate relevance contextually, without a universal numeric score.

### L08-KC-01. Why is touch count alone insufficient to rank a zone?
**Correct answer:** Because the count does not capture reaction clarity, structure or current context by itself
**Rationale:** Count is one observation, not a complete measure of a zone’s usefulness.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not turn one visible feature into a universal score.

### L08-KC-02. A chart crosses an area many times but shows little clear response around it. What is a defensible conclusion?
**Correct answer:** The area may be less interpretable; compare the behaviour and context before deciding
**Rationale:** Repeated crossing can be less informative than a smaller number of clear, contextual reactions.
**Common misconception to address:** More visits do not necessarily mean better evidence.

### L08-KC-03. What is the purpose of comparing a high-crossing area with fewer, more interpretable reactions?
**Correct answer:** To practise qualitative judgement using the character of the evidence
**Rationale:** The comparison develops contextual evaluation rather than a magic score or prediction rule.
**Common misconception to address:** Neither low nor high touch count automatically determines quality.


## Lesson 09 — Read the approach to a zone
**Focus:** Describe approach behaviour before the zone is reached.

### L09-KC-01. When studying an approach to a zone, what should be recorded first?
**Correct answer:** Observable features such as direction, pace and overlap
**Rationale:** Start with what the chart visibly shows before interpreting its possible significance.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not turn visual movement into an unsupported account of market participants’ intent.

### L09-KC-02. Price approaches a zone with several directional candles. What does that establish?
**Correct answer:** The approach has visible directional character; the response at the zone is not yet known
**Rationale:** Approach behaviour can be described before interaction; it does not settle the eventual reaction.
**Common misconception to address:** Avoid hindsight and do not confuse approach with outcome.

### L09-KC-03. Why compare a directional approach with an overlapping approach?
**Correct answer:** To observe how the path into the area differs without making the outcome certain
**Rationale:** The contrast builds descriptive skill while preserving uncertainty about what happens at the zone.
**Common misconception to address:** The lesson is not a deterministic entry rule.


## Lesson 10 — Rejection versus acceptance
**Focus:** Separate reaction away, possible acceptance beyond and inconclusive evidence.

### L10-KC-01. A candle wicks through a zone and closes back inside. What is the safest immediate description?
**Correct answer:** Price traded beyond the boundary intraperiod and closed back inside; further context may matter
**Rationale:** Describe the candle accurately, then consider the wider sequence rather than declaring a final outcome from the wick.
**Common misconception to address:** A wick alone is not universal proof of acceptance or breakout.

### L10-KC-02. What most supports describing movement beyond a zone as possible acceptance?
**Correct answer:** A wider sequence showing sustained movement or follow-through beyond the area
**Rationale:** Sustained action and follow-through offer more evidence than a brief excursion.
**Common misconception to address:** Possible acceptance remains an interpretation, not certainty.

### L10-KC-03. The chart shows a crossing but subsequent candles are mixed. What response is allowed?
**Correct answer:** Call the reading inconclusive and state what additional evidence would matter
**Rationale:** An inconclusive classification is valid when the current evidence does not support a firmer conclusion.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not force a binary answer simply because the activity offers categories.


## Lesson 11 — How a zone’s significance changes
**Focus:** Update a zone assessment as evidence accumulates or weakens.

### L11-KC-01. A zone produces a clear reaction, then later price moves through it with little response. What should the learner do?
**Correct answer:** Update the assessment and state which new evidence changed it
**Rationale:** A zone assessment is contextual and can change as new evidence arrives.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not treat a prior reaction as permanent proof.

### L11-KC-02. What should an evidence log record?
**Correct answer:** What happened at each stage, what interpretation followed and what remains uncertain
**Rationale:** The log should preserve the chronology between visible evidence and changing interpretation.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not reduce evolving evidence to a touch counter.

### L11-KC-03. After several visits, why might a learner judge a zone differently from the first visit?
**Correct answer:** The character and clarity of the reactions, plus the changing context, may have altered the evidence
**Rationale:** Assessment should respond to the nature of new evidence, not visit count alone.
**Common misconception to address:** Neither a fixed nor a purely recency-based rule is justified.


## Lesson 12 — Breakout or temporary penetration?
**Focus:** Distinguish a brief crossing from more sustained movement beyond a zone.

### L12-KC-01. On seeing the first candle cross a zone boundary, what is the best immediate response?
**Correct answer:** Record the crossing and wait for the next available evidence before strengthening the interpretation
**Rationale:** The first crossing is an event to observe; subsequent action may support or weaken the breakout interpretation.
**Common misconception to address:** Preserve the information available at each stage.

### L12-KC-02. After an initial crossing, price returns inside the zone. What does that do to the reading?
**Correct answer:** It adds evidence against sustained acceptance at that moment, while leaving future outcomes open
**Rationale:** A return inside matters to the current interpretation but does not guarantee the next path.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not convert one sequence into a universal trading instruction.

### L12-KC-03. A move beyond the zone is followed by sustained movement and follow-through. How should it be described?
**Correct answer:** As stronger evidence of a breakout/acceptance than the first crossing alone
**Rationale:** Follow-through adds evidence for sustained movement beyond the area.
**Common misconception to address:** A stronger read is not a guarantee of future continuation.


## Lesson 13 — Retests and role reversal
**Focus:** Treat a return test and role reversal as possibilities, not requirements.

### L13-KC-01. After an upside break, the former resistance area may act as support. How should this be framed?
**Correct answer:** It can happen and should be assessed from the return behaviour if it occurs
**Rationale:** Role reversal is a possibility, not a rule. Subsequent price behaviour determines whether the area appears to hold.
**Common misconception to address:** Avoid “must” and “always” language.

### L13-KC-02. Price breaks beyond a zone and never returns to test it. What is the most accurate lesson?
**Correct answer:** A retest is not mandatory; assess the evidence that actually appears
**Rationale:** The course does not teach a mandatory retest rule.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not impose an event that the chart did not produce.

### L13-KC-03. On a return to a former resistance area, price moves back below it rather than holding above. What should the learner compare?
**Correct answer:** The actual return behaviour with the conditions that would support or weaken the role-reversal idea
**Rationale:** A holding and a failed return provide different evidence about the possible new role.
**Common misconception to address:** A former level’s role is conditional, not guaranteed by the initial break.


## Lesson 14 — Failed breakouts and alternate outcomes
**Focus:** Analyse a failed break without hindsight and consider alternatives.

### L14-KC-01. What distinguishes a failed-breakout sequence from a chart that has only just crossed a boundary?
**Correct answer:** Later evidence shows a move beyond followed by a return through the area, while the first crossing alone is not enough
**Rationale:** Failure becomes interpretable only as the sequence develops; the initial state cannot contain future evidence.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not leak hindsight into the pre-break view.

### L14-KC-02. Why must the “before the break” chart stop before the later failure is visible?
**Correct answer:** To let learners reason from the evidence available at that moment
**Rationale:** The exercise tests chronological reasoning rather than hindsight.
**Common misconception to address:** A fair task gives only the information that would have been available then.

### L14-KC-03. What is the instructional value of comparing a failed break with a pullback that holds?
**Correct answer:** It keeps alternative outcomes visible and discourages one-outcome thinking
**Rationale:** Comparing contrasting paths demonstrates that similar beginnings can develop differently.
**Common misconception to address:** Avoid converting examples into deterministic recipes.


## Lesson 15 — From zones to conditional scenarios
**Focus:** Create conditional scenarios with alternatives, invalidation and available space.

### L15-KC-01. Which is the strongest conditional scenario statement?
**Correct answer:** If price holds above the area on the evidence specified, I will reassess the continuation case; a move back through the area weakens it
**Rationale:** A conditional scenario names evidence that supports a reading and evidence that would weaken it.
**Common misconception to address:** A scenario is not a prediction or entry signal.

### L15-KC-02. What belongs in the invalidation field?
**Correct answer:** An observable development that would weaken or contradict the thesis
**Rationale:** Invalidation makes the thesis testable and open to new evidence.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not confuse a desired outcome with a condition that would disconfirm the view.

### L15-KC-03. The next opposing zone is close, the evidence is mixed, and neither scenario is clear. What is acceptable?
**Correct answer:** Stand aside and explain why the evidence/available space is not sufficient
**Rationale:** Stand-aside is valid when it is reasoned from the evidence and conditions.
**Common misconception to address:** The task does not require a directional answer on every chart.


## Lesson 16 — Case study: a trade based on a reaction
**Focus:** Review a real reaction-based trade chronologically and separate process from outcome.

### L16-KC-01. Why should a reaction-based trade case reveal its information in chronological stages?
**Correct answer:** To let the learner judge each decision using only what was known at that point
**Rationale:** Chronology prevents hindsight from making the original decision look more certain than it was.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not reconstruct earlier certainty using information learned later.

### L16-KC-02. A trade made money, but the stated plan and risk controls were not followed. What is the best process review?
**Correct answer:** The profitable result does not by itself make the process sound; review the plan and execution separately
**Rationale:** Outcome and process quality are related but distinct. A positive outcome does not erase process weaknesses.
**Common misconception to address:** Avoid outcome bias.

### L16-KC-03. What evidence is needed for an authentic instructor case?
**Correct answer:** The real chart/context and accurately represented thesis, risk, management and outcome; disclose any reconstruction or missing record
**Rationale:** The learner must see an honest record of what was known and done. Missing evidence or reconstruction must be labelled.
**Common misconception to address:** Never invent instrument, prices, rationale or outcome and present them as the instructor’s.


## Lesson 17 — When the thesis fails, or you stay out
**Focus:** Distinguish thesis failure from process failure and review stand-aside decisions.

### L17-KC-01. A thesis is contradicted by new evidence, but the trader had defined that evidence as invalidation and responded as planned. What does this most directly indicate?
**Correct answer:** The thesis failed; that does not by itself prove the process failed
**Rationale:** A thesis can fail while the process remains disciplined if the invalidation and response were clear and followed.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not judge process quality from the outcome alone.

### L17-KC-02. Which is a defensible stand-aside example?
**Correct answer:** No trade because the stated conditions were absent or evidence remained insufficient, with the reason recorded
**Rationale:** A no-trade decision can be intentional and reviewable when grounded in absent conditions or insufficient evidence.
**Common misconception to address:** Distinguish deliberate restraint from an unexplained omission.

### L17-KC-03. How should a case explain what changed the original thesis?
**Correct answer:** Preserve the sequence of new evidence, the original invalidation and the actual decision taken
**Rationale:** Chronology makes the reasoning and the decision auditable and reduces hindsight bias.
**Common misconception to address:** Do not silently rewrite the past to fit the outcome.

## General scoring and feedback principles

- Award one point per formative item; do not infer ability, trading performance or likely profitability from a short knowledge check.
- Provide an explanation for the selected answer after submission. A wrong answer should be described as a concept to revisit, not as evidence of unsuitability for trading.
- Avoid scoring free-text chart rationale automatically unless a separate evaluation system has been implemented and validated.
- Preserve “not enough evidence yet” and “stand aside” wherever the underlying activity supports those answers.
