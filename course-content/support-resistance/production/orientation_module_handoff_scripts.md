# TradeThesis — Orientation and Module Handoff Scripts

**Audience:** Internal production / recording  
**Status:** First-pass script draft; check against canonical lesson scripts during second pass.  
**Purpose:** Make the 18-lesson progression feel like one coherent learning journey. Read the learner-facing copy naturally, without adding market predictions or promises.

## Opening orientation — before Lesson 1

**Estimated duration:** 2–3 minutes  
**On screen:** Course title; one unlabelled illustrative chart; then the six-module map. Keep any later answer/reference overlays hidden.

**Narration**

“Welcome to TradeThesis Support & Resistance.

In this course, we are not looking for magic prices or a level that promises to stop the market. We are learning to read areas where price has responded before, explain what the chart shows, and think conditionally about what may happen next.

We will work in stages. First, learn to read structure. Then decide which turning points and areas matter. Next, draw boundaries you can defend. After that, study how price behaves around a zone, including breaks and failures. Finally, bring the pieces together in conditional scenarios and case reviews.

As you work, separate observation from interpretation. State what would change your view. And remember that uncertainty is not a mistake: sometimes the most defensible conclusion is to wait for more evidence or stand aside.

The charts will ask you to try before you see a reference explanation. Do not worry about copying a line exactly. Explain the evidence and the choices behind your marking.

Let's begin with the most important question: what can a support or resistance zone actually tell us?”

## Module 1 — Understand (Lessons 1–2)

**Module opener narration**

“Before we draw anything, we need a reliable way to describe what we are looking at. In this first module, we will clarify what support and resistance can tell us, what they cannot tell us, and how to describe the broad structure of price. We will keep the chart clean and hold off on choosing zones until the structure itself is easier to see.”

**Module exit / handoff narration**

“You now have a starting vocabulary: price can trend, range, transition, or remain unclear; and a zone describes an area of prior response, not a guaranteed barrier. Next, we will use that structural view to select the turning points and areas that genuinely help explain the chart. The goal is not to mark everything—it is to decide what deserves attention.”

**Assembly note:** End on a clean chart, not a completed support/resistance map. Bridge to Lesson 3's candidate turns.

---

## Module 2 — Find the important areas (Lessons 3–5)

**Module opener narration**

“Once we can read the broad structure, the next challenge is selection. A chart can contain many small turns, but not all of them deserve equal attention. This module is about choosing the turning points that help explain the current structure, grouping related reactions into areas, and removing marks that add clutter rather than clarity.”

**Module exit / handoff narration**

“By the end of this module, you should be able to explain why you kept some areas and left others unmarked. The Clear W exercise was not a search for one perfect drawing; it was practice in making the chart easier to reason about. Next, we will examine how wide a zone should be, how to keep the chart readable, and how to judge a zone without relying on a magic score.”

**Assembly note:** ChartLab Clear W must follow the Lesson 5 teaching sequence. Preserve learner attempt before reference/reasoning reveal.

---

## Module 3 — Draw and evaluate zones (Lessons 6–8)

**Module opener narration**

“Finding a useful area is only part of the work. We also need to represent it in a way that captures the relevant price behaviour without ignoring normal variation or making the zone so broad that it stops helping. In this module, you will practise defending the upper and lower boundaries, simplifying redundant markings, and reviewing a zone in context rather than giving it an artificial score.”

**Module exit / handoff narration**

“You now have a marking method you can explain: why this area, why these boundaries, and why other marks were left out. But a zone is not a static verdict. Its relevance can change as price approaches, reacts, moves through or returns to it. The next module shifts from drawing the area to observing the behaviour around it.”

**Assembly note:** ChartLab Uneven Lows is the second and final lab in the early drawing sequence. Keep the qualitative review; do not add numerical strength badges.

---

## Module 4 — Read behaviour around a zone (Lessons 9–11)

**Module opener narration**

“Now that the zones are on the chart, we need to read the sequence around them. The approach matters because the same area can meet very different conditions. A reaction may suggest rejection, movement beyond the area may or may not become acceptance, and one candle rarely settles the question by itself. We will practise describing what happened before deciding what it might mean—and updating that view as more evidence arrives.”

**Module exit / handoff narration**

“The key habit is to let the evidence accumulate. A quick response, a sustained move beyond the area and a mixed sequence are not the same thing. We should not turn one candle into a universal rule, nor should we ignore new information because our first marking looked convincing. Next, we will look at crossings, retests and failed attempts, where timing and the sequence become especially important.”

**Assembly note — evidence dependency:** Lesson 9's instructor micro-case must be built from an authentic instructor chart and rationale. Do not record or release the case-specific reveal until that material has been supplied and identified as contemporaneous or reconstructed. This does not block recording the general module bridge.

## Module 5 — Breakouts, retests and failed attempts (Lessons 12–14)

**Module opener narration**

“A move beyond a zone is an event to investigate, not an automatic verdict. We need to distinguish a first crossing from evidence of follow-through, consider what a retest does or does not show, and recognise that role reversal is possible rather than mandatory. We will also study failed attempts without using the eventual outcome to pretend that the result was obvious from the start.”

**Module exit / handoff narration**

“You have now examined both continuation and failure without treating either as inevitable. The same discipline applies when a thesis stops fitting the evidence: describe what changed, state what would invalidate the idea, and avoid rewriting the original chart with hindsight. In the final module, we will turn this reading process into conditional scenarios and review actual decision-making.”

**Assembly note:** ChartLab Wider Neckline remains the third and only lab for this module. Keep the pre-break view free of future candles and separate the provisional thesis from the updated view.

---

## Module 6 — Apply and review (Lessons 15–18)

**Module opener narration**

“You have built the pieces one at a time. Now we will combine them. A zone can provide context, but it is not an entry signal on its own. A useful scenario states what needs to happen, what would weaken the idea, what the next opposing area leaves available, and what alternative—or stand-aside decision—remains. We will then review real decisions, separating the quality of a process from the outcome it produced.”

**Module exit / course close narration**

“The final chart is an opportunity to show your reasoning without copying a chart you have already seen. Describe the structure, defend your areas, separate observation from interpretation, and state your conditions and invalidation. More marks are not necessarily better marks, and a confident prediction is not necessarily a good explanation.

Support and resistance become useful when they help you organise evidence and decisions—not when you treat them as promises. Carry the process forward: observe, interpret, plan conditionally, and reconsider when the evidence changes.”

**Assembly note — evidence dependencies:** Lessons 16 and 17 require authentic instructor records. Do not substitute fictional instructor trades. If a case is reconstructed from historical data, label it clearly and do not claim unsupported contemporaneous thoughts or annotations. Lesson 18's assessment and parallel retry charts must remain unseen during prior instruction; show reference answers only after the learner's submission is recorded.
