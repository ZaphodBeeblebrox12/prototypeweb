# TradeThesis Support & Resistance — Facilitator & Producer Runbook v1

**Status:** first-pass handoff draft; use with the canonical scripts and cue sheets. Not an instructional sign-off or permission to publish.

## Purpose

This is the practical companion for anyone recording, presenting, facilitating, or assembling the 18-lesson course. It turns the course sequence into operating guidance without replacing the canonical lesson scripts. Exact narration, on-screen prompts, asset IDs, and reveal order remain in the scripts and `production/recording_assembly_cue_sheets_v1.md`.

The planned learner experience is primarily self-paced. “Facilitator” here means the person recording or running the learning experience, not a promise that live tutoring or a live cohort is included.

## Non-negotiable teaching stance

- Start with what is observable on the chart; only then invite interpretation.
- Use conditional scenarios and explicit invalidation/reconsideration rather than predictions presented as facts.
- “Inconclusive” and “stand aside” are valid when the evidence is insufficient.
- A support/resistance zone is an area where a response may occur, not a guarantee.
- A reference drawing is one reasoned example. Give credit to alternative boundaries when the learner's reasoning is defensible.
- Touch count is not a universal quality score. No single wick, candle, retest, crossing, or role reversal is proof by itself.
- Never infer institutional intent solely from a chart.
- Synthetic charts must be clearly described as **“Illustrative chart — not historical market data.”** Never present fictional OHLC values as historical market prices.
- Do not invent the instructor's real trades, rationale, instrument, price, chronology, outcome, or recollection.

## Before running or recording a lesson

1. Open the canonical script for that lesson and its cue sheet section. Confirm you have the current version; do not rely on an old exported screenshot or standalone script if the bundled canonical pack supersedes it.
2. Open every listed chart state before recording. Check the filename and state against the cue. If a state is missing or mismatched, mark an asset gap; do not substitute a reference image for the clean view.
3. Confirm the first chart view contains no reference line, future candle, answer, or outcome that should remain hidden.
4. Check the worksheet/activity and knowledge check that accompany the lesson. Use exact learner-facing prompt text from the canonical source.
5. Confirm whether the lesson has a dependency or hard gate. Lessons 9, 16, and 17 have instructor-material dependencies; they remain incomplete until authentic evidence is supplied.
6. Test the planned screen at desktop and narrow mobile widths. Make sure candle evidence and prompt text remain readable and any chart overlay does not hide important candles.
7. Keep student content and instructor/production material separate. Never upload internal answer keys, reference overlays, raw datasets, or internal notes into a public/student asset folder.

## During delivery: a repeatable rhythm

1. **Orient:** state the skill and the question the learner will answer.
2. **Observe:** show the clean chart. Invite a plain-language description before adding technical labels.
3. **Commit:** pause for a written, selected, or drawn response. Do not reveal the reference until the required submission has been recorded by the actual learning room.
4. **Reveal progressively:** reveal only the next planned stage. Do not jump to a later result to make the explanation look more convincing.
5. **Compare reasoning:** explain the reference rationale and limits. Do not frame exact coordinate matching as the goal.
6. **Reconsider:** ask what changed, what did not change, and what would weaken the current interpretation.
7. **Check understanding:** use the prescribed knowledge check and feedback after submission.
8. **Handoff:** connect the skill to the next lesson. Do not introduce extra ChartLabs beyond Lessons 5, 8, and 14.

Pause long enough for the learner to inspect the evidence and respond. Use the cue sheet's time ranges as an assembly guide, not as a reason to rush a learner past a required response. If a learner's answer is unexpected but evidence-based, use the feedback bank's reasoning-oriented pattern rather than steering them toward a directional guess.

## Special handling rules

### The three authentic instructor cases

- **Lesson 9:** source a genuine pre-interaction chart/export and the actual observation/rationale. The learner-facing crop should stop before the relevant interaction and hide the instructor's markings and outcome. Show later material only in its separate reveal. If the case is not supplied, display a clearly marked “instructor case pending” placeholder in production—never write a fictional first-person case.
- **Lesson 16:** obtain original context and the decision chronology (thesis, alternative, invalidation, trigger, risk, management, exit and review). If some points are reconstructed from memory rather than contemporaneous notes, label them explicitly and state the evidence limits.
- **Lesson 17:** obtain an actual failed/invalidated thesis, loss, or justified no-trade example and review it candidly. Preserve the original thesis and invalidation chronology; do not retrofit the decision using later candles.

These are blockers only for falsely presenting the affected case as complete. They do not stop assembly of unrelated lessons.

### Three prescribed ChartLabs

1. **Lesson 5 — Clear W:** choose useful areas and reduce clutter.
2. **Lesson 8 — Uneven Lows:** make a contextual qualitative assessment without a magic score.
3. **Lesson 14 — Wider Neckline:** analyse a failed-break sequence and alternate outcomes.

For each, run the exact sequence in the specification: untouched → mark/draw → submit → preserve work → reveal reference → compare/feedback → completion. Integration and access control are not verified by having prototype files or screenshots.

### Knowledge checks and completion

- Student checks remain separate from the internal answer key and structured question bank.
- Explain answers only after the response is submitted.
- The proposed 2/3 formative threshold is not validated until pilot evidence supports it.
- Lesson 18's proposed 70/100 threshold and no-zero rule are provisional and must be calibrated on sample submissions.
- Completion, concept-check performance, and final assessment result are distinct states. Do not imply the learner demonstrated trading competence or profitability from a quiz score.
