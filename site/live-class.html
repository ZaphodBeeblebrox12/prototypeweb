Trade Construction & Management

You can spot a setup. Can you explain the whole trade?

Across six live weekend sessions, practise connecting what price is doing to the decisions that follow: your thesis, entry, invalidation, risk, management, exit and review. The aim is not to hand you calls to copy. It is to help you build a process you can explain, question and improve.

What the work is for

By the end, your thinking should be easier to explain.

Not because uncertainty disappears, but because you have practised asking the questions that make a trade idea more complete.

01
Read context before looking for an entry. Describe structure, location and relevant conditions without turning a single candle into a conclusion.
02
Build a thesis—and a countercase. Explain what supports the idea, what else might be happening, and what evidence would change your mind.
03
Make risk part of the plan. Connect invalidation, position size and potential loss before considering the trade complete.
04
Review the decision, not only the result. Use a structured review to separate process quality from the luck of one outcome.
A good answer includes what you still don't know. We are practising decision quality, not grading predictions.
Six live sessions · one connected process

Each weekend leaves you with something you can use.

The sessions follow the life of a trade idea. Each worksheet becomes part of the final playbook, so the last session is an assembly and review—not a blank page.

01
Session 01 · Observe

Map the context

Describe what price is doing, the chart's location and the levels or conditions worth watching. Separate direct observations from assumptions before forming a view.

Take-away · Context worksheet Your Context Map A written snapshot of the market, relevant levels, timeframe and unanswered questions.
02
Session 02 · Interpret

Construct the thesis

Build a case from evidence, then make room for another plausible interpretation. Decide what would support the idea and what would weaken it.

Take-away · Thesis worksheet Your Thesis Card Evidence, alternative scenario, confidence limits and the conditions that would change your mind.
03
Session 03 · Define

Plan the entry and invalidation

Translate an idea into conditions you would need to see. Identify where the thesis no longer holds instead of treating entry as an isolated trigger.

Take-away · Decision worksheet Your Entry Plan Conditions to wait for, what would invalidate the idea, and what still needs confirmation.
04
Session 04 · Quantify

Make risk explicit

Work through hypothetical position-size and loss calculations, and examine how costs, slippage and leverage can change exposure.

Take-away · Risk worksheet Your Risk Sheet Assumptions, planned invalidation, quantity calculation and an explicit statement of what is at stake.
05
Session 05 · Respond

Plan management and exit

Think ahead about decisions that may arise once a hypothetical trade is active. Consider what would justify holding, reducing or exiting—and what would only be noise.

Take-away · If/then worksheet Your Management Map Prewritten responses to key scenarios, including when not to improvise.
06
Session 06 · Review

Review and assemble the playbook

Review the logic across the earlier worksheets. Identify gaps, write a repeatable checklist and note which parts need more practice or testing before use.

Take-away · Final playbook Your written process A personal document that connects context, thesis, entry, risk, management, exit and review.
The work comes together

Leave with more than notes from six weekends.

Your final playbook is built from the work you have already done. It should show how you think, where your rules are clear, and where you still need evidence or practice.

What the live class includes

Guided practice, not six long lectures.

The value is in applying one connected framework, discussing the reasoning, and leaving each session with a clear piece of work to carry forward.

01 / Discuss

Live chart walkthroughs

Use chart examples to practise observing structure, comparing interpretations and explaining what evidence is missing.

02 / Write

Six guided worksheets

Record the key thinking from each session in a reusable format that feeds into the final playbook.

03 / Reflect

Checkpoint questions

At each stage, pause to check whether you can explain the decision in your own words—not just repeat terminology.

04 / Connect

A full trade lifecycle

Bring context, thesis, entry, risk, management, exit and review into the same conversation.

05 / Assemble

A personal playbook

Pull your session work into a concise written process, with questions and gaps clearly marked.

06 / Continue

A next-practice plan

Finish by identifying what to rehearse, what to test and what not to assume yet.

How progress works

Progress is a clearer explanation—not a winning prediction.

The live class is designed around guided checkpoints and written work, not a pass/fail grade for guessing market direction.

Notice Can you separate what the chart shows from what you think it means?
Explain Can you state the thesis, alternative and evidence that might change it?
Plan Can you describe invalidation, exposure and possible responses before acting?
Review Can you explain what you learned and what needs more practice?

Is this class a fit?

A room for people willing to explain their thinking.

You do not need a perfect track record. You do need to be willing to question your first interpretation and work through the less glamorous parts of a decision.

You may find it useful if…

  • You know some chart concepts but struggle to connect them into a plan.
  • You want to practise explaining your reasoning and hearing other interpretations.
  • You want risk, exit and review to matter as much as entry.
  • You are willing to complete short worksheets between sessions.

This is not…

  • A live buy/sell signal or trade-copying room.
  • A promise of returns or a guaranteed outcome.
  • A substitute for your own risk decisions or further practice.
  • A claim that the class removes uncertainty from markets.
Before you ask to join

Useful things to know.

We want you to understand the format and the limits of the class before spending your time or money.

Is this the same as The TradeThesis Trading Path?

No. Trade Construction & Management is a six-week live classroom with guided discussion, worksheets and a final playbook. The TradeThesis Trading Path is a separate self-paced course with its own curriculum and enrolment.

What do I leave with?

The intended outputs are a context map, thesis card, entry and invalidation plan, risk sheet, management map and final written playbook. The class focuses on building and explaining a process, not promising a trading outcome.

Do I need to know everything about charts first?

You should be comfortable enough to look at a chart and discuss what you see. If you are brand new to market screens, the self-paced TradeThesis Trading Path may be a more suitable starting point.

Are the examples live trade signals?

No. Examples are used to teach observation, reasoning and risk questions. They are not recommendations to buy or sell any financial instrument.
